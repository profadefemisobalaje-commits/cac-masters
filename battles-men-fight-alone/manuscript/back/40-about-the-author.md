# About the Author {.back}

**Prof. Adeleke Daniel Durojaiye (PhD)** is a scholar, educator, and writer whose work addresses the intersection of professional practice, human development, and the responsibilities that men carry within their families, their institutions, and their communities.

His writing is characterised by a concern with the ordinary rather than the dramatic: the arrangements by which a man conducts his position, the conventions of speech and silence that he inherits, and the practices by which those conventions can be altered. His academic work has taken him across the disciplines of law and social policy, and he writes for the general reader with the conviction that a scholarly understanding of a human difficulty should be usable by the person experiencing it.

*Battles Men Fight Alone* is the product of sustained observation of the ways in which men carry serious responsibilities without stating what the carrying costs them, and of the conditions under which that silence can be interrupted. The book is written for the man who recognises himself in it, for the parties who live with him, and for the practitioners — teachers, clinicians, counsellors, employers, and community leaders — who are in a position to alter the arrangements that produce the isolation it describes.

The author is a committed advocate for the proposition with which this book begins and ends: that men can carry serious responsibilities without being expected to endure emotional suffering in isolation.
