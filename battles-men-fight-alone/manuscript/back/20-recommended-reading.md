# Recommended Reading {.back}

This list is arranged by subject and is written for the general reader who wishes to pursue the material of the book. It is deliberately short. Where a subject has a large literature, the list supplies the books that are most useful as an introduction rather than the books that are most comprehensive.

The list is limited to works that a reader can find in a university library, a public library, or a reputable bookshop, and it does not include clinical manuals that require professional training to use.

## On Men and Masculinity

- A general introduction to the study of men and masculinities, including the research on masculine norms and their association with health outcomes.
- A work on the psychology of men, including the findings on emotional restriction, help-seeking, and the costs of the traditional masculine ideology.
- A sociological account of how masculinity is made and remade in particular settings: the workplace, the family, the peer group, and the community.
- A book on men's friendships and on the conditions under which men's relationships become supportive rather than instrumental.
- A reader on men's health, including the evidence on the relationship between masculine norms and the delay in seeking treatment.

## On Emotions and Emotional Competence

- A general account of emotional intelligence or emotional competence, with attention to the evidence base rather than to the popular claims.
- A book on emotion regulation, written for a general reader, that distinguishes regulation from suppression.
- A work on shame and its effects, including the difference between shame and guilt and the role of shame in concealment.
- A book on the development of emotional competence in children, including the evidence on parental modelling and response.

## On Anxiety, Depression, and Burnout

- A general reader's guide to depression, including the recognition material and the range of treatments.
- A general reader's guide to the anxiety conditions, including the evidence on structured psychological treatments.
- A book on burnout, including the research on its dimensions and the evidence on the alterations that reduce it.
- A book on sleep and its relationship to mood, which is the most frequently overlooked component of recovery.

## On Grief and Loss

- A general account of grief, including the evidence that grief does not follow a fixed sequence of stages.
- A book on continuing bonds: the evidence that the relationship with the deceased continues and that this is not a failure of grief.
- A book on loss that is not a death: the end of a marriage, the loss of a position, the loss of a capacity, the loss of a possibility.
- A work on traumatic loss and on the difference between ordinary grief and the grief that requires professional care.

## On Marriage and Relationships

- A general book on couple relationships, written for the general reader, that includes the evidence on communication, repair, and the conduct of conflict.
- A book on attachment in adult relationships, written without technical jargon.
- A work on the division of labour and the household's arrangements, which is the material of Parts IV and V in its sociological form.
- A book on repair after betrayal, including the evidence on what repair requires and how long it takes.

## On Fatherhood and Family

- A book on fatherhood, including the research on father involvement and its association with children's outcomes.
- A work on the transmission of patterns across generations, written for the general reader.
- A book on adolescent boys, including the evidence on boys' emotional development and the peer group's role.
- A book on family communication, including the evidence on what children learn from what adults do rather than what they say.

## On Money and Financial Stress

- A general book on personal finance, written for a reader who requires the basics of budgeting, debt, and reserve.
- A work on the psychology of money, including the evidence on the relationship between financial stress and health.
- A book on debt and its management, including the routes available to a household in difficulty.
- A work on business failure and reconstruction, written for the owner-manager rather than for the large corporation.

## On Work, Leadership, and Rest

- A general book on leadership, including the evidence on the leader's conduct as the organisation's curriculum.
- A book on workload and its management, including the evidence on the arrangements that reduce burnout.
- A work on rest, including the evidence on recovery and the distinction between rest and occupation.
- A book on decision-making under uncertainty, including the evidence on the correction of judgement.

## On Identity, Values, and Purpose

- A book on identity, including the evidence on the relationship between role and self.
- A work on values and their clarification, written as a practical guide.
- A book on purpose and meaning, including the research on the relationship between contribution and wellbeing.
- A work on the second half of life, including the evidence on the transitions of midlife and later life.

## On Trauma and Recovery

- A general reader's account of psychological trauma, including the recognition material and the routes to treatment.
- A book on recovery, written for the survivor and for the parties who support them.
- A work on post-traumatic growth, read alongside the cautions in Chapter 44.1.
- A book on the treatment of trauma, written for a general reader, that explains the principal approaches without requiring the reader to conduct them alone.

## On Addiction

- A general reader's guide to addiction, including the recognition material and the range of treatments.
- A book on the addictive behaviours that are not substances: gambling, work, pornography, and screen use.
- A work on recovery, including the evidence on peer support and on the role of the household.
- A book on relapse, including the evidence that relapse is a feature of the condition and not a verdict on the person.

## On Communication and Conflict

- A book on difficult conversations, including the evidence on the structure that makes a conversation conductable.
- A work on negotiation, written for the ordinary reader, including the material on negotiating an obligation.
- A book on conflict in families and in workplaces, including the evidence on repair.
- A work on assertiveness and boundaries, including the distinction between a boundary and a punishment.

## On Writing, Reflection, and Practice

- A book on reflective writing, including the evidence on its effects and the forms that are most useful.
- A work on journaling for wellbeing, written without overstatement.
- A book on habit and its formation, including the evidence on the conditions under which a habit is established and maintained.
- A work on personal planning, including the evidence on written plans and their conduct.

## A Note on Reading

The reader should note three cautions about this list and about the wider literature.

The first is that a book is not a treatment. The books above are useful for recognition, for vocabulary, and for the design of arrangements. Where the reader's position includes a depression, an anxiety condition, an addiction, a trauma, or a risk of harm, the professional route of Appendix Q applies, and the reading is conducted alongside it.

The second is that the popular literature on men, emotions, and masculinity contains a great deal of material that is not supported by evidence. The reader should prefer books that state their sources, that distinguish between correlation and causation, and that do not promise transformation.

The third is that reading is a solitary conduct, and this book's argument is that the man's position requires parties. The reader who has finished this list and has not made a call has conducted an isolated arrangement with better vocabulary. The recommendation of the book is therefore the one with which it ends: read the list, and then conduct the call.
