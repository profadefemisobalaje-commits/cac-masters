# How to Use This Book {.front}

This is a working book. It can be read from beginning to end like a narrative, but it was designed to be used — marked, annotated, returned to, and worked through a chapter at a time. The following notes explain its structure and suggest routes through it for different readers.

## The Structure of a Chapter

Every chapter follows the same architecture, so that the reader always knows where he is.

| Element | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Chapter Objectives** | Six aims, in plain language, stating what the chapter is intended to make possible. |
| **Illustrative Story** | An original composite narrative — a man, a family, a situation — that opens the chapter's subject concretely. |
| **Body sections** | Six numbered sections that explain, describe, and instruct. Each corresponds to one of the topics listed in the table of contents. |
| **Figures and tables** | Diagrams and organisers that reduce a complex experience to a structure the reader can hold in mind. |
| **Illustrative case** | A second, shorter composite case, usually presenting a turning point or a contrast. |
| **Practical exercise** | A written exercise, conversation rehearsal, or planning task, to be done rather than read. |
| **Chapter Summary** | The chapter's argument compressed to half a page. |
| **Reflection questions** | Questions for personal reflection or for discussion in a group. |
| **Further reading** | Directions for readers who want to go deeper. |

## Routes Through the Book

**If you are reading for yourself, and you are not in crisis.** Read Part One first. It establishes the vocabulary on which everything else depends. After that, move to the parts that correspond to the battles you actually recognise. The book is arranged so that each part is intelligible on its own: if your battles are financial, go to Part Three; if they concern marriage or fatherhood, Parts Four and Five; if they concern grief or regret, Part Eight.

**If you are reading for yourself, and you are struggling now.** Begin with Chapter 34 (*When Silence Becomes Dangerous*), Appendix B, and Appendix Q. These deal with warning signs, crisis support, and how to reach help. Then return to Part One and read at your own pace. You are under no obligation to read the book in order, and no obligation to read it quickly.

**If you are reading because of someone else.** Parts Four, Five, and Seven are the most useful for partners and family members, and Chapter 33 for those who find themselves carrying other people's problems. Appendix E (communication exercises) and Appendix F (marriage reflection) are designed to be used with another person. Appendix Q lists sources of professional help.

**If you are a counsellor, therapist, physician, teacher, or community leader.** The book is written for general readers, so it does not attempt clinical instruction. Its value to professionals is likely to lie in the vocabulary and framing offered to men, and in the exercises and worksheets, which can be adapted for use with clients. Part Two and Part Ten describe how men typically present distress and what the recognised pathways to care are.

**If you are using the book with a group.** Appendix S provides discussion questions for men's groups, book clubs, church and community men's fellowships, and workplace sessions. The appendices are designed so that a group can work through one chapter and one worksheet per meeting. Group use multiplies the value of the book: the material is about isolation, and the most direct remedy for isolation is other people in the same room.

## Pace and Practice

A chapter is roughly the length of a long magazine feature read aloud. Two or three chapters a week is a realistic pace for a serious reader; a chapter a week is a realistic pace for a group. Speed is not a virtue here. The measure of success is not how much of the book has been read but how much of it has been done.

Each chapter contains a practical exercise. The exercises are cumulative. The early ones help a man describe what he is carrying — a task that many men have never attempted in writing. The middle ones help him test whether his situation can be discussed with another person, and rehearse how. The later ones help him plan changes in work, money, marriage, health, and life direction. Readers who skip the exercises will still get something from the book. Readers who do them will get considerably more.

A notebook is assumed rather than supplied. Many exercises ask for written answers, and writing produces a different kind of honesty from thinking. Where an exercise can be completed on a single sheet, a copy can be made; the appendices are formatted so that they can be printed or copied by hand.

## A Note on Order, Safety, and Limits

The parts of the book are arranged in a deliberate sequence, from the description of the silent world outwards to recovery. That sequence is a suggestion, not a rule.

Three cautions apply throughout.

- **Do not use this book as a substitute for care.** Self-help material is useful for milder difficulties, and useful as an accompaniment to professional care. It is not a treatment for severe depression, post-traumatic stress, alcohol dependence, or any condition in which a person's safety is at risk. Where the book recommends professional consultation, that recommendation should be taken literally.
- **Do not push through distress alone.** If an exercise or a chapter stirs up intense distress, particularly around grief, trauma, or shame, stop and speak to a qualified professional. Distress of that intensity is information about what kind of help is needed, not evidence that the reader has failed.
- **Do not use the book as a weapon, against yourself or anyone else.** Nothing in these pages is intended to give a man a diagnosis of his wife, his son, his father, or himself. It is intended to give him language, understanding, and a plan.

## A Word to the Reader Who Is Not a Man

If you have picked up this book as a wife, a mother, a sister, a daughter, a colleague, a manager, or a therapist, welcome. The book is written in the voice of a man addressing men, but nothing in it is closed to you. Understanding how men are taught to be silent, what their silence conceals, and how it can be interrupted is useful to anyone who lives or works with men. It may also be uncomfortable in places: the book describes how common it is for a man to be seen and not known, and how frequently those closest to him are the last to learn what he has been carrying. That discomfort is not an accusation. It is an invitation to a better conversation.
