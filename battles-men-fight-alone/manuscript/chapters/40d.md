## 40.5 The Audit of the Second Half

The exercise is conducted in one page of three lists, and its purpose is to establish what the man's remaining years consist of him doing. The form is supplied in Section 40.8; the section here supplies the four questions that classify each item.

### Question One: Is It Owed?

The four tests of Chapter 33.4, applied to the item:

**Whose responsibility is it in principle?** The item's owner. **Whose capacity is it?** Whether the man is the party able to conduct it. **Is it mine to do, or mine to make possible?** The distinction that produces the audit's largest revision. **What does it cost the people I am responsible for?** The household's material, counted.

**The classification.** Where the item is owed, it goes on the first list with its arrangement. Where it is not owed and the man is merely capable, it goes on the second list, with the arrangement by which the other party's capacity is to be constructed.

### Question Two: Do I Want It?

The question of the man's own position, and the one the reader may be unable to answer. The instruction from Section 40.3's third loss: the answer is recorded as it arrives, without assessment, and where the answer is *I do not know*, the item is marked and returned to in the following month after the preference register has been conducted.

**The distinction the chapter requires.** There is a class of items that the man does not want and that are owed: the children's requirements, the household's arrangements, the repairs of Chapter 29, the obligations given. **The chapter's position on this class is not that it should be released; it is that it should be named accurately.** A man who knows that he is conducting an obligation he does not want is in a different position from the man who has arranged not to notice, because the first man can arrange his own life around it and the second is conducting it as the whole of his life.

### Question Three: Would I Take It Up Now?

The test of the item's actual claim, and the one that resolves the majority of the audit's difficult cases. The specification: **if the item arrived today, in my actual position, with my actual capacities and obligations, would I take it up?**

**The instances.** The position held because it was obtained; the relationship conducted because it was begun; the arrangement maintained because it was the arrangement; the community's office accepted in a year the man cannot now identify. The test's function: it supplies the classification for the items that are not owed and not wanted and that have been conducted through inertia, which the case material records as the largest class in most audits.

**The caution stated.** The test should not be applied to the items of Parts III to V without the four tests of Question One. A man's children's requirements would not be taken up if they arrived today in the sense that no man would choose them; the item is owed, and the test does not apply to it.

### Question Four: What Would Its Ending Require?

The question that governs the second list, since the item's release is generally an arrangement and not a cessation. The material of Chapters 33.6 and 35.4: the disclosure, the arrangement, the period of transition, the other party's capacity, and the man's own conduct through the period.

**The three forms of release.**

**The closure.** The item ends, and it ends by agreement or by the man's statement. The forms: the community's office, the arrangement, the membership, the irrelevant obligation.

**The transfer.** The item is handed over: to a sibling, a colleague, a subordinate, another member of the family. The form's requirement: the transfer is arranged with notice and with the transferee's capacity addressed, which is the material of Chapter 33.6's second part.

**The reduction.** The item is continued at a level the man's own life can fund. The form is the most frequent of the three in the case material, and the material of Chapter 40.6 supplies the household presentation by which it is conducted.

### The Four Categories of the Second List

The audit's second list — what the man will set down — contains four categories, and the reader should record his own items in each.

**Category one: the obligations that were never his.** Accepted because nobody else would, because it was offered, because the man was competent, or because the other party's capacity was never tested.

**Category two: the positions held for the standing.** The office, the title, the committee, the association's responsibility, the arrangement conducted for the community's account of him. The material of Chapters 25.6 and 30.5 applies, and the specific finding is that the standing produced by a position of this kind is generally less than the man's estimate.

**Category three: the arrangements maintained through inertia.** The relationship, the membership, the commitment, the property, the arrangement whose continuation has not been examined. The class recorded by Question Three.

**Category four: the man's own conduct that consumes his resources without producing anything.** The specific and least examined class: the hours of the position that have grown, the habit, the arrangement of the day that consumes the evening, the screen, the drink of Chapter 22, the competition, the anger of Chapter 34. The class is included in the audit because its items are the ones that are released without negotiation and that supply the largest recoverable amount of time in most audits.

### The Third List

**What the man will take up.** The list that has been empty, and the one the chapter exists for.

**The four categories.**

**The unfinished item that is still available.** The qualification, the practice, the trade, the piece of land, the business, the study, the craft.

**The item that was never begun.** The thing the man intended and did not start. The case material's finding: the class is generally populated and its items are generally small and specific rather than large and general.

**The relationship the man will construct rather than maintain.** The specific person, and the specific arrangement, in the form of Chapter 27.5.

**The conduct that is the man's own.** The place, the pursuit, the hours, the rest of Chapter 37, the health of Chapter 23, the practice of Chapter 39.5.

**The requirement for the third list.** Each item must be specific, dated, and arranged, and it must be recorded in a form that states what the first step is. The finding from the case material: the men whose third list contained items without a first step did not take them up, and the men whose lists contained a first step with a date generally did.

## 40.6 Conducting the Choice

The mechanics. The section supplies the four matters that determine whether the chapter's work is conducted or abandoned.

### Matter One: The Household

**The position.** The household has its own arrangements, its own expectations, and frequently its own account of what the man's remaining years will consist of. Its members' responses to the man's choices are legitimate, since the household's position may be affected by them.

**The arrangement.** The four arrangements of Chapter 38.6, applied to this material: the disclosure, in the four-part form, of the man's position — what he has been, what he has found, what he intends, and what he requires; the household's own arrangements, at the level the man's choices permit; the household's role in the choice, which is generally substantial; and the household's own people and rest.

**The sequence.** The disclosure precedes the conduct. The finding from the case material: the men who conducted the changes and informed the household afterwards met the household's resistance and, in a substantial minority of cases, the household's arrangement of its own life in the departure of one of its members; the men who conducted the disclosure first met the household's participation.

**The household's statement.** The form recorded in the practice material, and the reader should use it: *This is my position. This is what I have found about my own life in the last month. This is what I intend to keep, what I intend to set down, and what I intend to take up. I am telling you before I conduct it because our arrangements concern you, and I require your account of what it does to you.*

### Matter Two: The People Who Will Resist

**The position, stated plainly.** The chapter's material will be resisted by some parties, and their resistance is frequently legitimate: the household member whose arrangements depend on the man's availability; the family member whose obligation the man has carried; the organisation that requires his availability; the person whose position in the arrangement is produced by the man's continuation. The resistance is not evidence that the work is wrong and it is evidence that the work has consequences.

**The arrangement.** Three instructions. **First: the distinction between the two kinds of resistance.** The party who is affected has a claim on the arrangement; the party who objects that the man has no business conducting his own life has an argument, and the argument is not owed an answer. **Second: the arrangement before the conduct.** The commitment is reduced, transferred, or ended with notice, with the period stated, and with the party's capacity addressed where the man has been carrying it. **Third: the resistance is endured.** The material of Chapters 26.6 and 32.5: the man's tolerance of another party's account of him, recorded and not argued, with the conduct unaltered.

**The two prohibitions.** The man does not conduct the change as an argument with the resisting party, which is the form in which the work becomes a quarrel about the man's rights. And the man does not require the resisting party's agreement before conducting the change, since the requirement is the arrangement in which nothing is ever altered.

### Matter Three: The Timing

**The findings.** The case material supplies three. **First: the change is conducted in a period of transition and not in a crisis.** The men who conducted the audit during a collapse, a bereavement, or a health event found the audit's items distorted, and the audit's conduct is generally better at a distance of some months from the event. **Second: the changes are distributed.** The men who conducted all of the changes in one quarter met the household's and the family's combined resistance and, in the case material, frequently abandoned the whole of the work; the men who conducted one or two items at a time over a year or two conducted the majority of the audit. **Third: the third list is conducted first.** The finding is the chapter's principal sequencing rule: a man who takes up an item from the third list before he sets down the items of the second list has established the claim of his own life, and the reduction of the obligations is thereafter conducted as a rearrangement rather than as a withdrawal.

### Matter Four: The Instruments the Man Requires

**The ally.** The material of Section 38.2's fourth task: one person who has the whole of the man's position, and who is told what the man is not to do, including the two prohibitions of Matter Two.

**The professional route.** Where the audit includes the marriage, the man's employment, the property, the finances, or the household's arrangements, the professional is engaged: the legal, the financial, the clinical, or the relationship. The material of Chapters 30.4 and 40.3's second loss.

**The written record.** The three lists, the dates, the first steps, and the household's statement. The finding: the men who recorded the audit in writing conducted substantially more of it than the men who conducted it in their thinking, which is a difference reported consistently enough in the practice material to be the section's principal instruction.

**The review.** The audit is reviewed at three months, at six months, and at a year, with the ally where possible. The review's questions: what has been conducted, what has not, what the resistance has been, and what the third list now contains.
