## 42.1 The Six Domains

The section supplies the material. The reader's task is to record, for each domain, the fact that would have to be known and the parties who must not learn it.

### Domain One: The Money

**The common form.** The man's income and his obligations are represented to be different from what they are. The household's expenditure is conducted at a level the man's income does not support; the position is maintained by borrowing, by facilities, by instalments, by the household's relatives' assistance, by an inheritance, or by the sale of assets; and the accounts are held by the man alone.

**The specific acts.** The statement of a salary higher than the actual; the concealed facility; the second facility; the instalment arrangements whose total is not stated; the school fees funded by a borrowing the household believes is the man's income; the contributions to the extended family funded by a shortfall in the household; the vehicle whose instalments are unknown; the occasion conducted at a level that requires a borrowing; the account held in a form the wife is not shown.

**The parties who must not learn.** Generally the wife, the children, the extended family, and the community. The finding from the case material: the man's account of *why* is almost always the same, and it is the material of Chapters 25 and 41 — *because I could not be seen to be unable* — and the specific form the case material records most frequently is a sentence about the man's father, his cohort, or his wife's family.

**The measure.** The gap between the household's expenditure and the man's actual income, recorded monthly; and the total of the concealed obligations, recorded as a figure. The reader who cannot state either of these figures is in the position this domain describes.

### Domain Two: The Work and the Competence

**The common form.** The man's position, his income's source, or his competence is represented to be other than what it is. The business that has ceased to trade; the contract that was not renewed; the practice that lost its principal client; the employment that ended in the previous quarter; the competence the man does not hold and represents that he does; the qualification that is expected and was not obtained; the project that is not proceeding; the promotion that is not available.

**The specific acts.** The man who leaves the house at the usual hour and conducts the day without employment; the business whose closure is known to the man and not to the household; the man who accepts work beyond his competence because the disclosure of the limit was unavailable to him; the project's reports conducted from the man's own accounts; the arrangements with the creditors conducted in secret.

**The parties who must not learn.** The household, the extended family, the professional body, the client, the community. The finding: the work domain's pretence is the one with the shortest duration, because the income's reduction becomes visible in the household's arrangements within months, and the case material records the specific and painful period of the man who leaves at the usual hour and returns at the usual hour with nothing accomplished.

**The measure.** The answer to the question *what would my household learn if it read my bank statements and my post for the last three months?*

### Domain Three: The Marriage

**The common form.** The marriage is represented to be other than what it is: to the extended family, the community, the household's children, the man's colleagues, and frequently to the man himself.

**The specific acts.** The performance of the marriage at the occasions; the joint attendance; the photographs; the account of the marriage given to the relatives; the separate arrangements conducted under one address; the man's own report to his friends that his marriage is satisfactory; the silence about the years of the arrangement described in Chapter 39.3's first cost.

**The parties who must not learn.** The extended family, the community, frequently the children, and — the specific and most consequential form — the man himself. The finding from the case material: this domain contains the pretence that is maintained for the longest period and the one whose disclosure the man most resists, and the material of Chapters 39 and 40.7's third choice is its substance.

**The measure.** The answer to the question *if the person closest to me were asked tomorrow to describe our marriage in four sentences, what would they say, and how far is it from what I would say?*

### Domain Four: The Household

**The common form.** The household's position is represented to be other than what it is: the provision that is actually conducted by the wife's labour, the school fees funded by a relative, the house that is not owned, the arrangement that is not on the terms represented, the household's difficulties concealed from the extended family or the community.

**The specific acts.** The man who accepts the credit for a provision that was substantially his wife's; the household conducted on the wife's income while the man's account of the household is that he is the provider; the assets that are not owned; the accommodation that is represented as owned; the man's arrangements with the extended family that are conducted at the household's expense without the household's knowledge.

**The measure.** The answer to the question *what does my household's position actually consist of, and who is actually conducting it?* The finding recorded in the case material: men in this domain generally list the household's material accurately when asked directly and have never stated it to the household, and the pretence is one of attribution rather than of fact.

### Domain Five: The Health and the Body

**The common form.** The man's health is represented to be other than what it is: the symptom that has not been investigated, the condition that has been diagnosed and not disclosed, the medication that is taken privately, the functioning that is supported by alcohol, by stimulants, by caffeine, or by an effort that is no longer sustainable.

**The specific acts.** The appointment not made; the result not collected; the diagnosis not stated to the household; the medication in the vehicle; the drinking whose measure is known to the man and not the household; the sleep that the man accounts for as ordinary; the injury not reported; the fatigue attributed to work.

**The parties who must not learn.** The household, the employer, the professional body, the colleagues, the insurer. The finding: this domain's pretence has the most serious consequences in the case material of this book, since the condition that is concealed is the condition that is not treated, and the material of Chapters 23, 31, and 36 supplies the outcomes.

**The measure.** The answers to three questions: what have I not had investigated; what do I know about my health that my household does not know; and what am I using to function that I have not disclosed?

### Domain Six: The Standing and the Position

**The common form.** The man's position in his community, his profession, his household's extended family, his place of worship, his association, or his trade is represented to be other than what it is: the titles, the offices, the membership of the arrangements, the contributions at the occasions, the assistance extended to the extended family, the fees paid to the associations, the position in the community's accounts.

**The specific acts.** The contribution to the extended family's occasions that requires a borrowing; the office accepted and not conducted; the title held without the substance; the contributions to the community whose level is sustained by the man's borrowing; the vehicle, the clothing, the house, and the occasions maintained at a level produced by the audience rather than by the man's obligations.

**The measure.** The answer to the question *what am I paying for, in this domain, that produces nothing except the audience's account of me?* The finding from the case material, and the material of Chapters 25.6 and 40.5's Category Two: the amount is generally substantial, its release is generally the largest single recovery in a man's audit, and its release is the one he resists most.

::: keypoint
A pretence, in this chapter's sense, is a state of affairs in which the man's position can continue only for as long as specified parties do not learn specified facts. Privacy is not a pretence, and the ordinary management of a man's presentation is not a pretence; the test is whether the man is arranging his conduct so that a particular party does not learn a particular fact, and whether his arrangements would require alteration if that party learned it. The six domains are the money, the work and the competence, the marriage, the household, the health, and the standing.
:::

## 42.2 The Five Costs

The costs are five, and the first is paid before anything is discovered. The reader should apply each to his own record and should note that the first is the one the case material records as the most significant.

### Cost One: The Vigilance

**The material.** A life that requires specified parties to remain uninformed requires an active arrangement to keep them so, and the arrangement is conducted continuously.

**The specific forms.** The telephone calls taken outside the room; the correspondence collected before it is seen; the message deleted; the account's password; the two parties kept apart; the meeting declined; the visit avoided; the question answered without the answer's subject; the figures in the head that must be consistent with the figures previously stated; the story that must be maintained with each party separately and consistently; the household's routines observed in order to know what has been seen.

**The finding.** The vigilance is **the man's most significant ongoing cost**, and it is unpaid in no currency the man can see, which is the reason it is undercounted. Its material: the attention that is not available for the man's work; the sleep that is reduced by the night-time accounting; the anticipatory dread of the discovery, which is the condition Chapter 30.2 described; the man's own withdrawal from ordinary occasions, because ordinary occasions are the settings in which the pretence may be punctured; and the specific exhaustion that the case material records in the man's own words: *I was not tired from the work. I was tired from the managing.*

**The particular mechanism.** The vigilance's cost increases with the pretence's age, because the number of parties who must be kept apart increases, the number of accounts that must be consistent increases, and the number of small items that must be remembered increases. The finding: the man who has maintained a pretence for five years is conducting more arrangement to maintain it than he is conducting to conduct his position.

### Cost Two: The Escalation

**The material.** A pretence requires the covering of its own evidence, and the covering generally produces further borrowing, further statement, or further arrangements that must themselves be concealed. This is the mechanism of Chapter 38.3's second error extended over time.

**The specific forms.** The facility taken to cover the instalments; the second facility taken to cover the first; the new contract taken to fund the current obligations; the borrowing to conduct the occasion that maintains the standing; the promotion sought not for the income or the work but for the account of the position; the compounding of the story as the facts diverge further.

**The finding.** The escalation is the mechanism by which a pretence becomes a collapse, and the case material records the specific progression in figures: a shortfall of a manageable size in the first year, a shortfall that is a multiple of it in the third, and a position that cannot be covered in the fifth. The finding is worth stating in the reader's own terms: **the pretence does not hold the position; it delays the discovery of the position's size, and it increases the size while it delays.**

### Cost Three: The Reduction of the Man's Relationships

**The material.** A pretence cannot be conducted in the presence of the parties who would puncture it, and the arrangement's consequence is that the man's relationships reduce.

**The specific forms.** The wife, who is the first party from whom the money, the work, the health, and frequently the marriage itself must be concealed; the friend whose questions are searching; the household's members whose arrangements touch the concealed domain; the colleague who knows the work's position; the relative who would need to be told the truth about the extended family's arrangements. The finding: the man's relationships reduce to those parties before whom the pretence can be maintained, and those parties are generally the ones who require nothing of him.

**The particular consequence.** The disclosure function of Chapter 27 is removed, which is the finding of the case material: the man conducting a pretence is the man with no one who knows the whole of his position, and the material of Chapters 27, 31, and 35 supplies the outcomes — the delays, the isolation, the unnegotiated positions, and the conditions that are treated late.

### Cost Four: The Reduction of the Man's Own Judgement

**The material.** A man conducting two accounts cannot use either accurately. The specific forms: the decisions taken on the basis of the position he has represented rather than the position he has; the orders accepted on a false income; the occasions conducted at a level that requires a further borrowing; the risk accepted because the shortfall could not be stated; the negotiation conducted without the household's material; the route chosen because the alternative would require the disclosure.

**The finding.** The judgement's reduction is structural rather than psychological: the man's decisions are taken in the world he has described, and the world he has described is not the world he is in. The case material records the outcome with the specific and consistent form: **the man's decisions were correct for the position he had told people he was in, and wrong for the position he was actually in.** The material of Chapter 38.4's reckoning — the written statement in figures — is the chapter's corrective.

### Cost Five: The Discovery, and Its Disproportion

**The material.** The final cost, and the one the man has been arranging to prevent, which is precisely the reason it is disproportionately severe when it arrives.

**The specific forms of the discovery.** The bank letter opened by the wife, as in Chapter 38.6's case; the creditor at the door; the facility called; the business's closure known through a third party; the diagnosis discovered; the account's balance seen; the relationship discovered; the community's remark; the relative's enquiry; the employer's notice.

**The finding, and it is the chapter's central mechanical statement.** The discovery's cost is not the loss. The loss is the position's actual size, which existed before the discovery and which the man would have had to address in either case. **The discovery's additional cost is the loss of the parties' trust in the man's account of himself, and the specific finding from the case material is that the parties' response to the loss is generally competent and their response to the concealment is not.** The wife who says that she was not shocked about the money and was shocked that he had been carrying it alone, in Chapter 38.6's case, is not an unusual respondent; she is the ordinary one.

**The specific disproportion.** The case material records four findings. **First:** the discovery arrives at the moment of the parties' least preparation, which is the moment at which the man has exhausted the options and can no longer construct an arrangement. **Second:** the discovery removes the man's ability to state the position in his own order, so the parties construct it from fragments and generally construct the worst version. **Third:** the discovery's timing is frequently connected to a third party — the creditor, the bank, the employer, the relative — which produces the specific humiliation of being disclosed by an outsider rather than by the man. **Fourth:** the discovery destroys the man's authority over his own account of himself, which is the material of Chapter 25.6 and the reason the standing is the domain that recovers most slowly.

**The comparison the chapter requires the reader to conduct.** The disclosure, at the man's own timing, with the statement and the plan: the parties are informed; the arrangements are constructed with their participation; the man retains the account of himself; the position is addressed at its actual size; and the trust's restoration is a separate matter that requires its own period. The exposure, at the discovery: the parties are informed by circumstance; the arrangements are constructed in the parties' reaction; the man's account is composed of what the fragments suggest; the position is generally larger than it needed to be; and the trust's restoration is required to begin from the position of having been deceived. The chapter's statement: **the two outcomes contain the same facts and the second contains them in the form that costs the most.**
