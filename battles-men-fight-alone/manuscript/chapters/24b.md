## 24.2 When the Roles Disappear

The events that remove a man's roles are specific and datable, and each has a distinct signature. They are set out below with the material that is lost in each case, because the treatment depends on identifying what has actually gone.

### Retirement

**What is removed.** The occupation, the position, the daily structure, the audience, the professional relationships, the income, and the answer to the question *what do you do?* For a man whose identity is assembled from his roles, retirement removes four of the five materials at once.

**The characteristic progression.** The first two to six weeks are frequently described as a holiday and are managed well. Then the structure's absence produces a specific effect: the day has no shape, and the man's time is not claimed by anybody. Between the second and the sixth month, the symptoms appear: insomnia, irritability, the sense of being in the way, and, in a substantial proportion of cases, the use of alcohol in the afternoon. The condition deepens over the following year, and it is frequently described by the household as the man's growing difficult rather than as a loss.

**The specific feature.** Retirement removes the roles without removing the man's capacity to occupy them. The man is still fully capable of work and is not required to do it, which produces the arrangement described by men in retirement as the worst element: the sense of being unnecessary while still competent.

### Redundancy and the Loss of a Business

**What is removed.** The occupation, the income, the position, the professional relationships, and, in many cases, the man's public standing and his own account of his competence.

**The characteristic progression.** Unlike retirement, which is scheduled, redundancy is a rupture: it removes the roles without preparation and it carries a judgement. The man's response is initially active — applications, contacts, plans — and then, frequently, collapses after six to nine months if the search has not succeeded. The condition that follows is described in Chapters 11 and 38.

**The specific feature.** The loss carries a verdict. Retirement removes a role; redundancy removes a role and supplies an assessment of the man's value. The second element is the reason the loss is frequently concealed, and the concealment is described in Chapter 9.

### The Children's Departure

**What is removed.** The father's daily function: the supervision, the provision of the immediate practical requirements, the presence, the structure of the household, and the position of the person on whom the household's arrangements depend.

**The characteristic progression.** The departure is gradual and it is generally accompanied by the man's satisfaction at the child's success, which prevents the loss from being recognised. The effects appear between six months and three years later, frequently at a point at which the man is asked to describe his life and discovers that he has nothing to describe except work.

**The specific feature.** The loss is ungrievable in public, because the child's departure is an achievement and any expression of difficulty is read as a failure to support it. The father therefore carries the material privately, and the household interprets his irritability or increased work as a reaction to something else.

### Separation and Divorce

**What is removed.** The husband role, the household structure, the daily presence of the children, the position in the extended family, and, in many cases, a substantial part of the man's social network, which was maintained through the marriage.

**The characteristic progression.** The material of Chapter 13 applies. The specific identity element is that separation removes both the role and the standing, and, in customary settings, supplies an interpretation. Men frequently respond by the consolidation of the one role that remains — the occupation — with the consequences described in Chapter 12.

**The specific feature.** The man's position in the extended family frequently changes without any discussion, and the change is not reversed when the marriage is. Men should note that the role's removal is frequently more durable than the marriage's interruption.

### Bereavement

**What is removed.** The husband or father role, the identity as the person who is responsible for a particular individual, the household's structure, and, in many cases, the man's only confidant.

**The characteristic progression.** Described in Chapter 13's case and in Chapter 25: initial containment and activity, followed by a period of collapse that arrives when the practical work concludes, and then a long period of restructuring that is not linear.

**The specific feature.** The research on widowers identifies this group as at elevated risk across every measured outcome, including mortality in the first year. The reason is partly the loss of the relationship and substantially the loss of the roles: the man has lost the person for whom he was responsible, the household he managed, and the person to whom he spoke.

### Illness, Injury, and Incapacity

**What is removed.** The capacity to provide, to protect, to perform, and to occupy the physical role. The identity materials are removed not by circumstances but by the man's body.

**The characteristic progression.** The medical condition, the treatment, the period of incapacity, the revision of the man's expectations, and the reconstruction of an arrangement that the condition permits. The psychological material is described in Chapter 33, and the specific identity element is that the loss is not attributable to anybody and therefore cannot be resented, which produces a particular form of difficulty: the man has no object for his anger.

**The specific feature.** The body becomes the visible evidence of the loss, and men frequently describe the specific moment at which they understood that they would not recover what they had, which is the beginning of the reconstruction.

### Migration and Displacement

**What is removed.** The standing accumulated in a community over decades, the position in the family's structure, the professional identity, the network, and the language in which the man was competent.

**The specific feature.** The loss is frequently invisible to the receiving community, which sees a man with a job and a household. The identity material that was built over thirty years is not transferable, and the reconstruction of standing in a new setting takes years and is frequently not undertaken, because the man's account of himself is still located in the place he left.

### The Common Structure

Four features recur in every case, and each is a point of intervention.

**The loss is dated.** The man can identify the day, which is useful, because the reconstruction can be dated from it.

**The roles disappear faster than the man's capacity to occupy them.** The man remains fully capable, which is the source of the specific frustration described under retirement.

**The loss is not socially recognised as a loss.** Retirement is congratulated, children's departure is celebrated, recovery from illness is expected. The absence of recognition prevents the grief, and ungrieved loss produces the condition described in the following section.

**The man's own explanation is generally about something else.** He attributes his state to boredom, to age, to his wife, to the state of the country. The accurate attribution — I have lost the material of which I was constructed — is generally not available to him, and the whole purpose of this chapter is to supply it.
