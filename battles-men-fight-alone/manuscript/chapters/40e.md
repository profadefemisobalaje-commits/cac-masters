## 40.7 The Choices

The specific decisions. The section supplies the seven choices the audit generally produces, with the criteria for each. The reader should apply the criteria to his own position and should not adopt the conclusions of the case material.

### Choice One: The Work

**The question.** Whether to continue in the work, to alter it, or to leave it, and on what terms.

**The criteria.** **The requirement:** the income the household requires, stated in figures, and the route's minimum. **The cost:** the hours, the travel, the health, the household's position, and the man's state, recorded in the form of Chapter 33.4's fourth test. **The alternative:** stated as an actual arrangement rather than as a possibility, with its income, its requirements, and the period of transition. **The age and the qualification:** the route's availability at the man's actual position. **The man's own material:** what he wants, from the preference register, recorded even where the answer is *I do not know*.

**The finding from the case material.** The most frequent outcome is not the ending of the work but its conduct on altered terms: the reduction of the hours, the refusal of the promotion, the transfer, the ending of the additional engagement, the negotiation of the arrangement. The second most frequent is the establishment of the second income, generally the item of the third list, conducted while the first income is maintained. The outright ending is the least frequent outcome and is generally conducted with the alternative in place and with the household's arrangements stated.

**The prohibition.** The man does not end the income on the basis of the audit alone, in a period of transition, without the alternative's arrangement and without the professional advice where the household's position is affected. The material of Chapter 38.3's errors applies in this material in full.

### Choice Two: The Household's Arrangements and the Extended Family

**The question.** What the man will continue to carry, at what level, and for whom.

**The criteria.** The four tests of Chapter 33.4; the household's own requirements, which take precedence over the extended family's in the case material and in the chapter's position; the other parties' capacity, required to be tested; the arrangement by which the commitment is reduced or transferred; and the disclosure of Section 40.6's Matter One.

**The finding.** The most frequent outcome is a substantial reduction conducted over a period of one to two years, with two or three items transferred and one or two ended, and with the household's requirements funded before the others. The specific finding for readers whose position is the load of Chapter 33: the reduction is generally met with the extended family's resistance in the first year and with its accommodation in the second, and the receiving parties' capacity appears.

### Choice Three: The Marriage, or the Partnership

**The question.** Whether the marriage is to be conducted as it is, altered, or ended.

**The criteria, and the chapter states them in full because this choice is the one most frequently mishandled.**

**First: the position of the man's own conduct.** Chapter 39's material applied to the man's own case: where the marriage's state is produced by the man's absence, coldness, or unavailability and not by the relationship's material, the choice is the conduct of Chapter 39.5's practices and not the marriage's ending. The case material is emphatic on the point, and Section 39.7's case is its illustration.

**Second: the repairs owed.** The material of Chapter 29.6: the man's own account, the disclosure, the apologies, and the altered conduct over a period. The chapter's rule: the choice is not made until the repairs have been conducted.

**Third: the relationship's material.** Where the disclosure, the repairs, the altered conduct, and the arrangements of Chapter 27 have been conducted over a period of a year and the position is unaltered, the relationship's material is the matter, and the professional route is engaged: the couple's counselling, and separately the legal advice where the choice is considered.

**Fourth: the household's members.** The children's position, their ages, and the arrangements that the choice requires. The material of Chapters 18 and 20 applies in full, and the chapter's finding is the one the research supplies: the household's outcome is determined less by the marriage's continuation than by the conflict's conduct and by the man's continued presence as a father.

**The prohibition.** The man does not leave the marriage on the basis of the audit, of a new relationship, or of the report that his life is unfulfilled. The case material records the outcome of that version consistently, and it is the material of Section 40.1's third prohibition.

### Choice Four: The Children

**The question.** What the man's remaining periods with his children consist of.

**The criteria.** The children's ages and their position; the material of Chapters 18, 20, and 39; the repairs owed, conducted in the individual form of Chapter 29.6's fourth element; the practices of Chapter 39.6; and the specific matter of what the man will be for each of them, which the case material records as the question that most alters men's conduct in this domain.

**The finding.** The most frequent outcome is not a change in the man's provision, which is generally established, but the construction of the relationship's content: the individual time, the specific question, the participation in what matters to the child, and the conduct of the repairs where the man has been absent. The finding from the case material's own reporting: the children's response is generally available, and the man's delay is generally explained by the difficulty of the first conversation rather than by the child's position.

### Choice Five: The Man's Own Arrangements

**The question.** What the man's own life consists of, in its practical and arranged form.

**The criteria.** The third list of Section 40.5; the material of Chapter 27.4's five sources and Chapter 27.5's arrangements; the rest of Chapter 37; the health of Chapter 23; the physical channel of Chapter 39.5; and the arrangements' cost, in time and money, which the man's own ledger is required to fund.

**The finding.** The most frequent outcome in the case material: three or four arrangements, of which at least one is physical, at least one is a relationship in which the man is known, and at least one is an item from the third list. The men's own reports identify the finding that makes the section's material effective: the arrangements were established before the man felt that he had time for them, and the time appeared once they existed.

### Choice Six: The Standing

**The question.** What the man's remaining years are for in the account of the community, the profession, and his own household.

**The criteria.** The material of Chapter 25.6 — the audience's authority, the personal measure, the recognition's actual value — conducted as a decision rather than as an analysis; and the material of Part XII's contribution question, which is the substance of the chapter's own conclusion: what the man will be for beyond his own household.

**The finding.** The most frequent outcome is the identification of one or two domains in which the man's contribution is conducted and the release of the remainder, which is the material of Section 40.5's Category Two. The men's own accounts describe the outcome in the terms of Chapter 25.6's third prohibition: the audience's authority reduced, and the man's actual contribution increased.

### Choice Seven: The Years

**The question.** The chapter's final choice, and the one that the audit's third list addresses: what the man's remaining years consist of, stated in the terms of what he will have produced and whom he will have been for.

**The criteria.** The material of Chapter 28.6's sixth section and of the reflection questions of Chapter 44: the years' actual number at the man's own table; the items of the third list; the relationships; the work; the contribution; and the rest.

**The form of the choice.** The chapter's instruction is that the choice be written in the form used by the men in the case material: one page, in the man's own hand, dated, containing the three lists, the specific choices, the first steps, and the sentence that states what the years are for. The page is kept, reviewed at the intervals of Section 40.6's Matter Four, and shown to the ally and to the household.

## 40.8 Illustrative Case: The Man Who Chose Late

::: casebox
**Composite case.** Constructed for teaching purposes from patterns reported in counselling of men, family practice, and career counselling.

**Joseph is fifty-eight, a quantity surveyor, who conducted his practice as a sole practitioner from the age of thirty-one, married to Ngozi for thirty-one years, with four children aged twenty-nine, twenty-seven, twenty-three, and nineteen.**

Joseph's practice was established, competent, and satisfactory in its income. His position in the case is the one this chapter describes: at fifty-eight, he was carrying a domestic load that consisted of his household, two widowed sisters, a nephew's fees, a cousin's rent, a building project he had begun in 2011, and nine years of a professional association's secretaryship that he described as *the thing that eats my Saturdays*. His savings were adequate. His health was described by his doctor as *satisfactory with two warnings*. His account of why he had come to the consultation, given in the first sitting and recorded in the case document, was: *I have everything and I am not interested in any of it. I do not know what I want. My wife says I have not been myself for two years and I cannot argue with her because I do not know what myself is.*

The audit, which took three sittings, produced the following.

**The first list — what he would keep.** The household's requirements; two of the four children's remaining obligations; the building project, which he revised to a smaller and completable form; one of the two sisters' maintenance, reduced and dated; his own practice, at two-thirds of its hours, with the introduction of an assistant; the marriage, identified as the item he would take up and not the item he would leave.

**The second list — what he would set down.** The association's secretaryship, which he described as *nine years and I have never once been asked whether I still wanted it*; the cousin's rent, which he had been paying since 2015 and which he described as *nobody has ever discussed it with me*; the second sister's maintenance, transferred to his son and his brother with a period and an arrangement; three investment arrangements he had entered on advice and had never examined; the involvement in two community committees; his Fridays' additional engagement; and the habit he described as *the two hours in the evenings that I could not account for*.

**The third list — what he would take up.** A six-month course in a technical subject he had intended since 2004 and had never begun; a weekly walk with his wife, arranged on a fixed day; a repair of the relationship with his second son, with whom he had been in a dispute since 2021 over a matter he described in the audit as *my own fault and I have never said so*; and one arrangement of his own, which he described in the audit as unchanged from its first form: *to read for two hours on a Sunday afternoon and not apologise for it.*

The conduct, over about eighteen months, is the case's material.

**The third list was conducted first.** Joseph enrolled in the course in the second month and attended on Tuesday evenings. His account of the first weeks: *I walked into a room with nine people and I was the oldest by about twenty years and the lecturer asked me what I did and I said I was a quantity surveyor and he said, so why are you here. I said, because I have wanted to do this since 2004 and I am fifty-eight. He said, welcome. I sat down and I had a feeling and I could not name it and I wrote it in my notebook on the bus and the word was pleased.*

**The disclosure to the household** was conducted in the fourth month, in the four-part form, with the audit's three lists in hand and the paper on the table. Ngozi's account, recorded in the case document: *He read it out and I knew most of it before he said it. What I did not know was the course and the Sunday. He said, I am not asking you to agree, and I am asking you to tell me what it does to you, and I told him that the Tuesdays were not a problem and the Sunday was not a problem and that I had been afraid that he was going to say something else.*

**The association's secretaryship was ended** in the fifth month, with a letter, with notice, with a nominated successor, and with the arrangement for the handover period. Joseph's account: *I wrote the letter four times. I had the speech ready for the chairman, with everything I had done for nine years. He said, we have been waiting for you to say it for two years, we did not want to ask. I have written to the association once since and I have not attended since June and nobody has said anything to me except the man who took over, who calls me about once a month and whom I am glad to help.*

**The cousin's rent was ended** with three months' notice and an arrangement, and Joseph's account of the conversation is the finding the chapter wishes to record: *He said that he had been afraid that I would stop, for four years. He said that he had asked his employer for a rise in 2022 and had not got it, and that he had not asked again because of this. I said four sentences and I did not argue with him about the past, and I gave him the three months. In the second month after the notice he told me he had obtained the increase in his allowance. I have contributed nothing to him since December of last year.*

**The son's repair** was conducted in the seventh month. Joseph's account: *I telephoned him and I said four sentences. I said that I had been wrong about the matter in 2021, that I did not have a reason, that it was not his fault, and that I wanted to see him. He was quiet for about fifteen seconds and then he said, I was waiting for you to say that, and I have seen him eleven times since and he brought his daughter at Christmas and she sat on my lap.*

**The review at eighteen months**, conducted in the case and recorded in the document, contained the following items. The course was completed, with the certificate kept in a folder that Joseph described as *the most expensive thing I own and I did not need the certificate*. The Tuesday evening and the Sunday afternoon continued. The walk with Ngozi continued, and Joseph described its effect in the household's own terms: *She told me in the eighth month that the forty minutes on Saturday was the first time in twenty years that we have talked about something that was not a decision.* The practice continued at the reduced hours with the assistant, and its income was reduced by about a fifth and its hours by about a third. The nephew's fees continued and the second sister's maintenance continued at the transferred arrangement. Two of the three investment arrangements were closed. His health's two warnings were addressed and one was resolved. His own sentence, recorded at the review: *I was the item that was never on the list, and I put it on the list, and I did it in the year I turned fifty-eight. I have four children and I want them to know that I did something that nobody asked me to do, and that I sat in a room as the worst student in it, and that I have a Sunday.*
:::

Five features of this case deserve isolation.

**The deferral's stages were all present, and the anchor was the duty anchor.** Joseph's obligations were accepted over three decades, each for a reason that was valid at the time, and his own life was deferred to a condition that was met in about 2019 and replaced by another. The case is included because the reader's own audit will generally find the same structure: not one decision, but thirty years of competent deferrals.

**The audit was small and the third list was the smallest item on the page.** The course, the walk, the repair, and the Sunday. The case is included because the chapter's material is frequently read as a proposal for a large reconstruction, and the case material's finding is the reverse: the third list's items are generally small, specific, and available, and their effect on the man's position is disproportionate to their size.

**The third list was conducted first, and it established the others.** The course in the second month, before the association's letter, the cousin's notice, and the son's telephone call. This is Section 40.6's Matter Three and the case is included because the sequencing rule is the chapter's most useful mechanical instruction: the man who has taken something up conducts the reductions as a rearrangement of his life rather than as the surrender of it.

**The second list was conducted with the arrangement and not with an argument.** The letter with notice and a successor; the cousin with three months and a statement; the association with the handover. The case's material records the receiving parties' responses, and they are the finding of Chapter 33.6 in its clearest form: the chairman who had been waiting for two years, the cousin who had been afraid for four, and the increase that appeared in the cousin's allowance within months of the notice.

**The repairs were conducted as a separate and specific work.** The second son, the four sentences, and the eleven visits since. The case is included because the chapter's material is not only about the man's own life: the audit's first list contains the relationship he would take up, and the repairs owed under Chapter 29 are conducted as part of it. The finding that the case supplies — *I was waiting for you to say that* — is the one reported by the parties on the receiving end of this material across the case material of this book.
