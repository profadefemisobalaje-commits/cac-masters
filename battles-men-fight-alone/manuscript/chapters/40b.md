## 40.1 What Choosing Yourself Does and Does Not Mean

The section supplies the boundary, and it is the most important material in the chapter because it determines whether the reader can conduct the work or is required to reject it.

### What It Does Not Mean

**It does not mean the abandonment of the people the man is responsible for.** The obligations of Parts III to V are the man's, and the audit of Section 40.5 is conducted on them rather than around them. A man's children's requirements, his household's arrangements, the obligations he has undertaken, and the repairs owed under Chapter 29 are not items that the chapter proposes to release.

**It does not mean the man's pleasure as a criterion.** The chapter's test is not whether an arrangement pleases the man. It is whether the arrangement is his: whether he has chosen it, whether it is owed, and whether it is the item he would fund if he were conducting the accounting rather than being conducted by it. The distinction matters: a man may choose a demanding obligation and be choosing himself in doing it, provided the choice is his.

**It does not mean the ending of a marriage for a better one.** The research and the case material on this point are unambiguous, and the chapter states the finding: the man who reconstructs his life around a new relationship while the circumstances that produced his unhappiness are unaddressed generally reproduces them. The specific choices of Section 40.7 include the marriage, and the criteria for it are supplied there and not here.

**It does not mean the announcement that he is now putting himself first.** The version of the chapter's material that men most frequently report as damaging to their households: the man's statement that he has decided to live for himself, delivered without an arrangement, which the household experiences as the withdrawal of the man who was providing for it.

**It does not mean the abandonment of the arrangements of this book.** The measure of Chapter 25.5, the arrangements of Chapter 27, the rest of Chapter 37, and the discipline of Chapter 39 are the man's own life being conducted; the chapter is not a substitute for them but their application to the question of what the remaining years are for.

### What It Does Mean

**The man's own life is an item on the ledger.** It has a cost, a budget, and a claim, and it is placed with the other items rather than after them. Its content: the health, the rest, the arrangements of Chapter 27, the work the man has chosen rather than the work that arrived, the relationships he has chosen rather than the ones that were assigned, the time, and the conduct he regards as his own.

**The man's own requirements are legitimate.** They are not a reward for the discharge of the obligations; they are items with their own claim. The finding that establishes this in the case material is the one from Chapter 37.5: the man's capacity, judgement, and conduct are the resources through which the obligations are discharged, and the neglect of his own life degrades the resources on which everything else depends.

**The man's obligations are audited rather than assumed.** The audit's mechanism is the material of Chapter 33.4's four tests, applied to every item the man is carrying: whose responsibility it is in principle, whose capacity it is, whether it is his to do or to make possible, and what it costs the people he is responsible for. The audit's output is three lists rather than one, and the case material's finding is that the second list — what the man will set down — is generally smaller than he expects and the third — what he will take up — is the one that has been empty for years.

**The man's own preference is information that he is required to have.** The finding of Chapter 39.3's fifth cost: the man who cannot register his own states cannot use them as information about his life. The chapter's preliminary condition is therefore the work of Chapter 39.5, and the reader who reports that he does not know what he wants should conduct the registration practice before conducting the audit, since the audit requires the material that the practice supplies.

**The choice is conducted in the years that remain and not in the years that were lost.** The material of Chapter 28.5 applies in full: what is available now, in the actual circumstances, with the actual capacities and the actual obligations. The chapter's finding is that men who conduct the audit discover that the available items are more numerous than the losses they have been counting, and that the losses' significance reduces when the available items are taken up.

### The Test of the Two Versions

The chapter supplies a test by which the reader can establish whether the work he is conducting is the version this book describes or the version that damages people.

**The test: what does the household's position look like at the end of the work?**

In the version described in this book, the household's arrangements are known to the household; the obligations owed are conducted; the repairs owed are made or arranged; the man's capacities are maintained; and the man is present in the household in a form the household can describe. In the damaging version, the household is informed of the man's decision; the obligations are reduced without arrangement; the repairs are not conducted; and the household's arrangements are conducted without the man.

The test's second element: **the man's own account of the work, offered to the household.** Where the account is *I have decided to put myself first*, the version is the damaging one. Where the account is *I have been the last item on my own list for thirty years and I am going to be an item on it, and here is what I am keeping, what I am setting down, and what I am taking up*, the version is this book's.

::: keypoint
Choosing oneself does not mean the abandonment of the people a man is responsible for, the man's pleasure as a criterion, the ending of a marriage for a better one, or the announcement that he is now putting himself first. It means that the man's own life is an item on the ledger with the other items, that his requirements are legitimate rather than a reward, that the obligations are audited rather than assumed, that his preference is information he is required to have, and that the choice is conducted in the years that remain. The test of which version is operating is what the household's position looks like at the end of the work.
:::

## 40.2 The Man Who Never Chose

The deferral is a process, and its stages are consistent. The reader should locate his own position and, more usefully, identify the specific rule or occasion on which his own deferral is anchored.

### The Seven Stages

**Stage one: the man's requirements are subordinated early.** The childhood in which the man's own wants were not attended to, or were attended to only after the household's other requirements were met; the eldest son's position; the household of scarcity in which any requirement was a luxury. The stage's lesson is the rule of Section 40.1's rule: the man's requirements are legitimate but deferred.

**Stage two: the man's first obligations are accepted.** The family's fees, the sibling's school, the parent's assistance, the household's requirements after a death. The stage's finding is the first competence: the man's provision is effective, and its effect on other people is visible.

**Stage three: the arrangement is established and rewarded.** The man is the one who provides; the family's account of him is composed of it; the man's standing in the community, as Chapter 25 described, is produced by it. The stage's finding is that the arrangement is now an identity and not only a conduct.

**Stage four: the deferral acquires a date.** The man's own requirements are deferred to a specific condition: when the children finish school; when the house is built; when the business is established; when the position is obtained; when the debts are cleared; when the parents are cared for. The stage's finding is that the condition is generally met, and the deferral is replaced by another condition, which the case material records in almost every instance: *I said I would do it when the last one finished school, and she finished in 2019, and I did not notice that I had stopped saying it.*

**Stage five: the man's own requirements are no longer formulated.** The most consequential stage and the least visible. The man stops producing the items: the things he intended, the work he wanted, the place, the pursuit, the rest, the relationships of his own choosing. The stage's marker is in the man's language: the requests, the wants, and the preferences disappear from the man's account of himself.

**Stage six: the man's own material is not counted.** The man's health, sleep, friendships, finances, and time are not among the items he audits or reports to anybody. The stage's finding, from the case material: the man who can give a complete account of his household's position and a partial account of his own, whose own arrangements are unknown to him in figures.

**Stage seven: the discovery.** The condition met; the retirement; the bankruptcy; the loss; the illness; the wife's statement; the bereavement; the child's departure; the daughter's wedding. The stage's material is that of Chapters 24, 28, and 30, and its presentation is generally the one that brings the man into the arrangements of this part of the book.

### The Five Anchors

The deferral is anchored to a rule or an occasion, and the reader should identify his own, because the anchor is what the arrangement must address.

**The worth anchor.** *I am what I provide.* The anchor is the one examined in Chapter 25.5 and its correction is the personal measure.

**The duty anchor.** *I am the one who was given the position and the obligation.* Its correction is the audit of Section 40.5, conducted with the four tests of Chapter 33.4.

**The fear anchor.** *If I stop, everything will collapse.* Its correction is Chapter 37.5's second part and the arithmetic of the collapse.

**The permission anchor.** *I have not earned it.* Its correction is the four permissions of Section 40.4.

**The identity anchor.** *I do not know who I would be.* Its correction is Chapters 24 and 28, and its conduct is the audit's third list.

### The Two Findings the Case Material Supplies

**The deferral is generally invisible to the man.** This is the reason the chapter's audit is a written exercise: the arrangement is conducted competently, the man's account of it is that he has been fulfilling his obligations, and the specific question of what his own life consists of has never been asked. The finding from the case material: the men who conduct the exercise discover the deferral's size within an hour.

**The deferral is generally incorrect in its accounting.** The men who conduct the audit find that a substantial proportion of what they have been carrying is neither owed nor wanted: the obligation that was accepted because nobody else would, the arrangement maintained because it was the arrangement, the position held because it had been obtained, the relationship conducted because the man could not raise the matter. The finding is the reason the audit's first list — what he will keep — is generally larger than the man's fear and the second — what he will set down — is generally larger than he had considered.
