# When Silence Becomes Dangerous {.chapter num="34" short="When Silence Becomes Dangerous"}

::: objectives
After reading this chapter, the reader should be able to:

1. Distinguish between silence that performs a function and silence that has become dangerous, using three explicit criteria.
2. Describe what sustained concealment does to a man's body, his sleep, his judgement, and his capacity, and explain the mechanisms involved.
3. Identify the five silences that carry a specific risk, including the silence concerning a risk to the man's own life.
4. Apply a checklist of indicators to his own position and to the position of a man he is concerned about.
5. Recognise the situations that require immediate action rather than a plan, and construct a personal safety plan in the form used in clinical practice.
6. Break a silence safely, in the graduated form set out in Section 34.6, including where the disclosure is complicated by its consequences for other people.
:::

## Introduction

The material in this book has established the case for a man's silence and has taken it seriously. Men's decisions not to speak about what they are carrying are frequently reasonable: the disclosure would cost them their position, their household's stability, the confidence of the people who depend on them, or a relationship they cannot afford to lose. Chapter 27's barriers were treated as real considerations rather than as errors, and the book has repeatedly declined to instruct men to talk about everything.

This chapter establishes the limit.

Silence has a range in which it functions: it protects, it preserves the arrangement, it holds a household steady through a difficulty, and it supplies the man with the time he requires in order to decide what to do. That range has an edge, and past the edge the same practice produces a different set of outcomes. The concealment stops performing its function, the material continues to operate, the man's body and judgement bear the cost, the people who depend on him are affected by conditions they cannot name, and the possibility of the outcomes the silence was intended to prevent increases rather than decreases.

This chapter concerns the edge and what occurs past it.

Its approach has five commitments.

The first is that the distinction is specific and can be stated. Sections 34.1 and 34.4 supply criteria, and the reader should be able to apply them to his own position rather than to assess the matter generally.

The second is that this chapter is not an argument for disclosure in general. The five silences identified in Section 34.3 are the ones that carry a specific risk, and they are the chapter's subject. A man's silence about an ordinary difficulty, a private history, or a matter he has decided to carry alone is addressed elsewhere in this book and is not the material of this chapter.

The third is that the chapter takes the position that a man's silence about a risk to his own life is the most consequential item in this book and requires a professional response rather than a private one. This position is stated plainly and it is repeated in the sections that follow, because the men in this material are a group with a documented elevated risk and because the facility that they would ordinarily rely upon — their own judgement — is the facility that the condition degrades. Chapter 31.4 stated the position in a leadership context; this chapter states it generally.

The fourth is that the chapter supplies practical forms for both the emergency and the ordinary case. Section 34.5 supplies the safety plan used in clinical practice, and Section 34.6 supplies the graduated form for a disclosure that must be conducted without damaging the man's household or his position.

The fifth is that a man's silence frequently affects people who cannot see it, and the chapter's clearest argument for the work is not the man's relief but the accuracy of the arrangements around him. A household that is managing the consequences of a man's unspoken state, in the absence of an explanation, generally constructs a worse one, and the effects of that construction are borne by the people in it.
