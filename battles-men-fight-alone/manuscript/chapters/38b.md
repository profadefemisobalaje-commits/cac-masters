## 38.1 What "Everything Falls Apart" Means

The phrase is used loosely, and it must be defined, because the definition determines the work.

### The Definition

**A total collapse is a loss of the man's standing arrangements in two or more domains at once, in a manner that has removed the structure through which he would ordinarily address any one of them.**

The definition has four components, and each should be tested against the reader's position.

**The loss is in more than one domain.** The domains are: the income and the work; the property and the assets; the household and the marriage; the standing in the community and the profession; the health and the body; and the support the man relies upon. A single loss, however severe, is addressed through the arrangements in the other domains. Two or more losses remove the arrangements through which each would be met.

**The structure has been removed.** The man who has lost a business but retains his employment, his household, and his relationships has a structure. The man who has lost his business and, through it, his income, his household's security, his standing in the community, and his relationships with the people who were connected to the business, has lost the structure itself.

**The man's account of his position has been affected.** The definition includes the identity material of Chapter 24: the man's answer to the question of who he is is frequently composed substantially of the arrangements that have failed, and the collapse therefore removes his account of himself along with his position.

**The position requires its own method.** Partial difficulties are addressed with the material of the preceding chapters; a collapse requires the sequence in this chapter, because the ordinary methods assume arrangements the collapse has removed.

### The Six Forms

**1. The business collapse.** The enterprise fails, the debts remain, the personal guarantees are called, the household's security is affected, and the standing in the community is altered. Frequently accompanied by the loss of the relationships that were connected to the business and by the man's own sense of having taken his household's security into a venture.

**2. The employment collapse.** The redundancy, the dismissal, the contract's ending, the position's removal, particularly where it has occurred in a period of general contraction, where the man's age is a factor, or where the position carried the household's arrangements and the man's own standing.

**3. The household collapse.** The separation or divorce, particularly where it combines with a financial loss, the loss of the house, and the loss of daily contact with the children. This form carries the highest reported load in the material and it combines the losses of Parts III and V with the practical material of this chapter.

**4. The health collapse.** The illness, injury, or condition that removes the man's capacity to work, his physical arrangements, and, through both, his household's position. Its specific feature is that the instrument through which the collapse would ordinarily be addressed — the man's own capacity — is the thing affected.

**5. The legal or positional collapse.** The loss of the licence, the registration, the position of trust, the reputation, or the liberty. Its specific feature is the alteration of the man's public account: what was known about him is replaced by something else, and the community's assessment, described in Chapter 25, becomes the principal practical obstacle.

**6. The compound collapse.** Two or more of the above arriving together or in sequence, which is the most common form in the case material: the business failure followed by the separation; the redundancy followed by the illness; the bereavement followed by the financial discovery. The compound form is the one for which this chapter is principally written.

### The Five Features That Make It Difficult

**The instruments are gone.** The man's income, network, standing, and household stability were the means by which he addressed difficulties, and they are the matters that the collapse has affected.

**The obligations continue.** The debts, the maintenance, the household's requirements, the obligations of Part IX, and the expectations of the community do not pause while the man rebuilds. The finding is the reason the first period's tasks are supplied as they are: they are directed at the obligations that must be contained rather than at the collapse as a whole.

**The man's judgement is affected.** The material of Chapter 27.3 applies in its strongest form: the collapse produces the isolated decision-making that the collapse itself requires the man not to conduct. The finding from the practice material is consistent: the men who make the largest errors in the collapse's first period are the men who conducted it alone.

**The audience.** The standing material of Chapter 25 applies, and in the collapse's case the audience is frequently real: this is the setting in which men are written off, and the practice material records that the community's assessment of the man is frequently formed in the first months and revised slowly.

**The shame.** The material of Chapters 8 and 21 applies: the collapse of the arrangement that supplied the man's standing produces the private account described in Chapter 21.2, and the account is generally harsher than the facts.

### The Question of Timing

One finding from the practice material should be stated at the outset because it determines the chapter's urgency.

The collapse's first period — from the event to about three months after — determines the shape of the following years in a manner that is out of proportion to its length. The obligations that are triaged, the assets that are protected, the income that is restored, the ally who is engaged, the household that is informed, and the errors avoided in that period govern the reconstruction's starting position. The men in the case material who rebuilt efficiently describe the same finding: *the whole thing turned on about six weeks.*
