# Redefining What It Means to Be a Man {.chapter num="41" short="Redefining What It Means to Be a Man"}

::: objectives
After reading this chapter, the reader should be able to:

1. Describe the definition of manhood he inherited, and identify its sources, its functions, and its clauses.
2. Analyse the five clauses of the inherited definition and state what each actually requires.
3. Explain how the definition operates as a rule rather than as a description, and how it is enforced without anybody enforcing it.
4. Apply the eight tests by which a clause of the definition is examined, and classify his own clauses.
5. Construct a working definition of manhood in the form used in this book: a statement of conduct rather than of status, with its obligations, its permissions, and its prohibitions named.
6. Conduct the substitution exercise, in which the inherited clauses are replaced by specific conduct in the reader's actual circumstances.
:::

## Introduction

This chapter is the first of the book's final part, and it addresses the subject that the previous forty chapters have been circling: the definition of manhood that the reader holds, and the question of whether he chose it.

The chapters to this point have established the difficulties and the arrangements. The battles of silence, the wounding, the losses, the load, the provision, the friendships that do not reach the material, the emotional arrangements, and the reconstructions. The present chapter addresses the rule that has been behind them, because the case material of this book has recorded one finding consistently and it is the finding on which the final part is constructed: **the difficulties are maintained by a definition of manhood that the men in this book did not write and have not examined.**

The definition is not a superstition and it is not an invention of the men who hold it. It is a set of instructions that were transmitted by parties who had the man's interests at heart — his father, his mother, his household, his school, his training, his trade, his profession, his community, his friends, and his culture — and it produced outcomes in the conditions for which it was written. It instructed men to be reliable, to provide, to endure, to protect, to be composed under pressure, to keep their word, and to carry what they are given. Those instructions built the households, the trades, the institutions, and the settlements that the man's generation inherited.

The chapter's position is not that the definition is false. It is that the definition is **incomplete, that its incompleteness has been concealed by its authority, and that the specific omissions are the material of this book.**

The definition gives a man an instruction about what to do with his fear, his grief, his shame, his exhaustion, and his own requirements, and the instruction in each case is a form of silence or suppression. Chapter 34 measured the cost of the first, Chapter 37 the cost of the second, and Chapter 39 the cost of the third. The chapter now addresses the source.

Its approach has five commitments.

**The first is that the reader is not to be treated as a victim of the definition.** Men hold their definitions for reasons that were valid and that remain partly valid; the definition has, in the case material of this book, saved men's lives, their households, and their positions. The chapter's examination is conducted with respect for what the definition has done, and the reader who approaches it expecting an indictment of his father's generation will not find one here.

**The second is that the definition is analysable.** It is not a mood or a tradition; it is a set of clauses, and clauses can be stated, examined, and revised. Sections 41.1 to 41.3 supply the analysis, and the reader should be able to write his own definition's clauses before he examines them.

**The third is that the definition is enforced socially but held personally.** The audience of Chapter 25 is the mechanism, and the definition's enforcement does not require an enforcer: the man conducts the examination of his own conduct against the definition, and his compliance is voluntary, which is the reason its revision is possible at the level of the individual man.

**The fourth is that the replacement is constructed rather than adopted.** The chapter supplies eight tests, a working definition, and a substitution exercise, and the reader's product is his own statement in his own words. The case material is consistent on the point: the men who adopted another man's formulation reproduced their original difficulty in a new vocabulary, and the men who constructed their own altered their conduct.

**The fifth is that the work is conducted in the second half of the man's life and it changes what he leaves.** The material of Chapter 43 is the substance of the chapter's closing sections: a man's definition is transmitted to his sons, his daughters, the young men in his trade, his household, and the men who work for him, and its revision is the mechanism by which a generation's inheritance is altered without a lecture being delivered to anybody.
