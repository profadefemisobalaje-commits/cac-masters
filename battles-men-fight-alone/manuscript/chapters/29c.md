## 29.3 The Anatomy of Regret

Regret has a structure, and the structure is important because the man's difficulty is generally not the original event but the process the event has been operating since.

### The Five Components

**1. The event.** What occurred, with its date, its setting, and its participants. The event is fixed and small, and men are frequently surprised, when they describe it for the first time, by how short the account is. This is because the event has been compressed and expanded alternately in the man's mind over a period of years.

**2. The counterfactual.** The alternative the man constructs: what would have happened if he had acted differently. The counterfactual is the single most important component of the material, and it is generally unfounded. The man's construction has three characteristic properties: it is certain — *if I had gone back, he would have recovered*; it is complete — the alternative is imagined through to a good outcome, with none of the intermediate complications; and it is favourable to the man's self-accusation, since the alternative always displays him as effective and the actual event displays him as he was. The research on counterfactual thinking identifies a consistent finding that is useful here: the ease of constructing an alternative is not evidence that the alternative was available.

**3. The verdict.** The conclusion the man has drawn about himself: that he is a coward, that he is not the man he believed he was, that he failed the one test that mattered. The verdict is the component that converts an event into an identity, and it is the material of Chapters 21 and 24.

**4. The debt.** The position that something is owed, and that it has not been paid. The debt may be owed to a person, to an outcome, or, in the private class, to nobody the man can identify, which is what makes it durable: a debt to an unnamed creditor is carried indefinitely, because there is no transaction that could settle it.

**5. The management.** The practices the man has developed to keep the material out of view: the avoidance of the place, the person, or the subject; the compensating conduct described in Section 29.4; the drinking at particular hours; the busyness; the silence at particular moments. The management is the largest component in terms of the man's daily life, and it is the one that most frequently produces the effects the man attributes to the original event.

### The Two Operating Modes

Regret operates in two modes, and the distinction determines everything in Sections 29.4 to 29.6.

**The corrective mode.** The material is available, has been examined, and supplies information the man acts upon. It functions as a standard: it is not revisited continuously, it does not produce the verdict, and it is engaged when a comparable situation arises. Men describe this mode accurately: *I do not dwell on it. It is part of what I know about myself, and it makes me careful in the specific way that it should.*

**The punitive mode.** The material operates as a standing sentence. It is revisited without being examined, the counterfactual is replayed without being corrected, the verdict is sustained, and no conduct is altered by the material because the function of the operation is not correction but sentence. Men describe this mode in terms that are consistent across cases: the material arrives at particular times — the early hours, the long drive, the specific date, the anniversary, the month — and it produces a specific sensation of exposure, lowness, and, in a substantial proportion of cases, the belief that the man's ordinary contentment is illegitimate.

### The Five Markers of the Punitive Mode

The following five markers distinguish the punitive mode, and each is a practical criterion that the reader can apply.

**1. The material is replayed without alteration.** The account the man rehearses is identical to the account he rehearsed a decade ago. New information has never entered it and the counterfactual has never been tested.

**2. No conduct has been altered by it.** The material is not producing any change in how the man acts; it is producing only an alteration in how he feels. This is the most useful criterion of the five, and it is the one that decides the treatment.

**3. The material is held privately and its disclosure is refused.** The punitive mode protects its material: the man declines the disclosure even where the disclosure is affordable, on the grounds that the matter should not be raised, which for a punitive-mode item is generally a statement about the sentence rather than about the audience.

**4. The man's ordinary goods are treated as illegitimate.** The household, the position, the children's progress, and the man's own satisfaction are treated as though they were obtained fraudulently or are not entirely his to enjoy. This marker is the one that produces the pattern described in Chapter 7 and it is the reason the material in this chapter and in Chapter 24 are adjacent.

**5. The material escalates in response to low mood, alcohol, or anniversary.** The escalation is the marker: a corrective item is stable across states, and a punitive item is amplified by states, which indicates that the material is operating as an instrument of self-sanction rather than as a source of information.

## 29.4 The Uses and the Abuses of Regret

Regret is not a defect of character and it serves a documented function. It also has a documented capacity to do harm, and the chapter requires the man to distinguish the two in his own material.

### The Uses

**1. Correction.** The material supplies the specific information required for the man to behave differently in comparable circumstances: the moment at which he should have spoken, the warning he should have noticed, the commitment he should have documented, the person he should have asked, the conduct he should avoid in drink. Men report that this function works when the material has been examined once and stored in this form rather than replayed.

**2. Prevention.** The generalised form: the man who has failed a person through cowardice becomes a man who intervenes in comparable situations, frequently at cost, for the remainder of his life. This is the function described in Section 29.6: the material converted into conduct. It is the most useful outcome available and it is the one the chapter pursues for irreparable matters.

**3. The calibration of a man's standard.** A man without regret has no evidence that his standard is operative. The regret is a demonstration that the man's values are real to him, and men in the corrective mode describe the material with a specific quality: they wish it had not occurred, and they recognise that it is evidence of what they hold.

**4. The grounds of a man's treatment of others.** Men who have failed somebody are frequently, subsequently, the men who stand with those who are failing: they visit the hospital, they attend the funeral, they make the call. This function's record in practice is strong, and it is included because the men who carry the heaviest material are frequently, in their conduct, the most reliable.

### The Abuses

**1. Rumination.** The replay without examination described in Section 29.3. The literature on rumination identifies its central feature accurately: the process feels like work while doing none, and it consumes the attention that the corresponding corrective function would require in a fraction of the time. A man who has replayed an event four hundred times has not examined it once.

**2. Self-punishment.** The maintenance of the sentence, and its specific feature is that it consumes the man's capacity without supplying anything to the person harmed. Section 29.6 addresses it at length, and the central proposition is worth stating here: an unrepaid debt to the person harmed is not discharged by the man's suffering, and where a man's suffering is the only payment being made, the ledger has been redirected rather than settled.

**3. Compensation in the wrong direction.** A distinct pattern with substantial costs, described in more detail in Section 29.7: the man who failed his younger brother becomes the over-attentive father of a son; the man who was absent from a dying parent's bedside becomes the man who will not permit his children to leave; the man who did not speak up becomes a man who speaks with disproportionate force. The compensations are real efforts and they are directed at people who were not the injured parties, which means the original debt remains and a new set of arrangements is imposed on people who did not require them.

**4. The denial of the man's present standing.** The practice of conducting the man's life in the shadow of the material, described in Chapter 7: the position declined, the pursuit abandoned, the attention that the man regards himself as not entitled to. The abuse is frequently presented as humility and its mechanism is a refusal of the man's actual standing, which does not benefit anybody.

**5. The avoidance of the specific place or person.** The management described in Section 29.3 as the fifth component: the man does not attend the village, does not visit the person, does not speak to the relative, does not open the letter. The avoidance is expensive in a specific way: it removes exactly the arrangements through which the matter might be repaired or, where it cannot be repaired, acknowledged.
