## 42.7 Illustrative Case: The Man Who Was Doing Well

::: casebox
**Composite case.** Constructed for teaching purposes from patterns reported in insolvency practice, family practice, and counselling of men.

**Emeka is thirty-nine, a builder and contractor, married to Grace for fourteen years, with two children aged eleven and seven.**

Emeka's position in the case is the chapter's central example, because his standing in the community, on the community development committee, in the association, and among the extended family was produced by his conduct of business, and his business was conducted on an appearance that his position could not fund.

**The record at the beginning of the case.** Emeka's company had a reasonable turnover. His household's position was maintained at the level of a successful contractor: a house on a modest mortgage, two vehicles, the children at a private school, the extended family's contributions, the occasion's hospitality, a quantity of machinery acquired on instalments, and a reputation that Emeka described as *the kind of name that gets the job without a long conversation*.

**The position beneath it, recorded by Emeka in the first sitting.** One principal client accounted for about sixty per cent of the turnover; the client's payments were running ninety days late and had been running later for two years; the company's facilities were at their limit and one was secured against the house; a second facility had been taken eleven months before the case and had been used to pay the wages of the previous quarter; the machinery's instalments were in arrears of two periods; the children's school fees for the current term had been paid from a friend's loan; and the extended family's contributions for the last year had been met from the second facility. His own statement of the position: *I am about two hundred and forty thousand in arrears, and about a hundred and ten of it is concealed from my wife, and if the client pays in the next four weeks, none of it exists.*

**The six domains, recorded in the case.** The money: the concealed facilities, the school fees, the extended family's contributions, and the machinery's arrears. The work: the client's position was concealed from the household and the company's position was represented to the association; the second facility had been taken without a written agreement, on a verbal understanding with a lender Emeka described as *a man I have known for twelve years*. The marriage: conducted at the level of the appearance, with Grace's own business's income — she had a small catering business — being used, in fact, to conduct the household's monthly expenditure, a fact Emeka had never stated. The household: the school's arrangements represented as affordable and the house's position unstated. The health: the sleep, the drinking of between three and six units of beer nightly, and a blood-pressure reading of 158 over 104 recorded at a pharmacy in an odd moment and not acted on. The standing: the building committee's contributions, the association's dues, the extended family's occasions, the household's hospitality, and the vehicle.

**The signs, applied to Emeka's account.** All seven were present. He could not state his position in figures without constructing it; the explaining had become routine; he was avoiding his wife's brother, who had lent him the school fees and had asked him twice about the repayment; his arrangements were conducted in the gaps of the day; he had withdrawn from the association's meetings and two occasions; his sleep had reduced and his temper had altered; and he had rehearsed the discovery for about a year. His account of the last item: *I used to lie in the bed and decide what I would say to her if the bank called, and I had one version in which I had already told her that morning, and it was the only way I could sleep.*

The material that produced the change was, in the case, three items in ten days. The client's finance department informed the company that the certification for the previous quarter would be delayed a further period of two months and that the matter was with a new officer. The lender who held the verbal facility telephoned on the same day about the interest. And Grace's discovery, on the following Sunday, was not of the money: she had taken the vehicle to the market and the school had telephoned Emeka's number twice, and she had answered the second call from the passenger seat and had learned from the school's assistant that the fees were outstanding, and she then asked Emeka a direct question about the household's accounts, and he told her a partial version, and she said the sentence he recorded in the case: *Emeka, I have been carrying this house for four years. I know exactly what is happening. I do not know why you think I do not.*

The work, conducted over about twenty months, followed the order of Section 42.6.

**The statement.** Conducted over four days, and its effect in Emeka's account: *I put everything on two sheets in figures. I had known most of it. What I had not known was the total, which was two hundred and forty-one thousand and some. I wrote the two sentences the book says to write, and the second one, about my own part, took me about an hour. What I wrote was that I had borrowed eleven times in two years and had never once written down the total, and that I had done it because I was afraid of being seen to be a man whose business was failing, and that I had been right about the fear and wrong about the price.*

**The one person.** A quantity surveyor eleven years older than Emeka, who had contracted in the same city for twenty-six years, whom Emeka had known for eight and had never disclosed anything to. The material: the two sheets, read out, in the surveyor's office, on a Wednesday evening. The surveyor's response, recorded in the case: *He read the two sheets and then he was quiet for about a minute and he said, you have described my own 2009. He told me for about forty minutes what had happened to him: the client who did not pay, the building society, the house he lost, the year in which he drove a taxi, and the two people who helped him. He said, I am going to tell you three things and you will not like the first. The first is that your client is not going to pay you for six months and possibly not at all, and you already know that and you have known it since November. The second is that your wife is stronger than you are and you have been insulting her for four years. The third is that you are going to have to reduce your life and that when you have done it you will be a better builder and you will not miss one thing that you are going to lose.*

**The household.** The disclosure to Grace was conducted in the following week over two sittings with the two sheets and the surveyor's notes. Grace's account, recorded separately: *I knew the shape of it. I did not know the amount or the second facility. What I want to record is what I said to him, which I had been holding for a long time: that we had been conducting this house on my business's money for four years while he was calling himself the provider, and that I had let him do it because I could see what it cost him, and that this was my part in it. And when I said that, he sat down on the floor of the parlour, which I have never seen him do in fourteen years.*

**The professionals.** A debt and insolvency adviser was engaged in the third week, on the surveyor's recommendation. The adviser conducted the arrangements with the two facilities, negotiated the machinery's instalment schedule, and advised on the house. A lawyer reviewed the guarantee and the verbal facility. The client's position was pursued through a formal demand and, in the ninth month, through the association's arbitration route, and it settled at about seventy per cent of the certified work with payment over eight months. And Emeka attended his doctor in the fourth week, was commenced on treatment for hypertension, and was referred for counselling on the alcohol use, which he described as follows: *The doctor asked me how much I was drinking and I told him the truth for the first time in my life and I said, about a bottle a night, and he wrote it down and said, right, and I sat there and nothing happened. I have been carrying that for eleven years and nothing happened.*

**The reductions, in layers.** The first layer, conducted in the first month, contained the association's dues, two subscriptions, the second vehicle, the hospitality, and the machinery that was not in use, which was sold. The finding: the layer produced about eleven per cent of the household's requirement and no disclosure outside the household. The second layer, conducted over the following four months, contained the school fees, which were renegotiated to a termly arrangement with the school's bursar and which were conducted at the same school after a conversation Emeka described as *the hardest fifteen minutes of the year*; the extended family's contributions, which were reduced with a stated arrangement and a three-month notice; and the building committee's position, which Emeka resigned. The third layer, conducted in the sixth month after the reductions' other layers and with the adviser's arrangement, contained the house, which was sold and the position moved to a smaller rented accommodation for a period of two years; the main vehicle, which was sold and replaced with one of about a third of the value; and the arrangement with the lender, which was settled at a figure over eleven months.

**The reconstruction.** Conducted over about two years and described by Emeka at the review in three items. **The first:** the work. The company's turnover reduced by about forty per cent and its position became profitable in the second year, and its composition altered: the principal client's share reduced from sixty to about twenty-five per cent and eight smaller clients were added. Emeka's account: *I had been chasing one big job for nine years. When it went, I had to go and find eight small ones, and I now know that the eight were the business and the one was the risk. I did not know that in 2019 and no one could have told me. I had to lose it to learn it.* **The second:** the household. The position at the true level, conducted with Grace's business as a stated and joint arrangement rather than as a concealed support, and the arrangement recorded: *We have one account for the house and one for the work and we do the figures on the last Sunday of the month, and it takes about twenty-five minutes, and in the first month of it I could not do it and I sat there with my hands shaking and Grace did the adding. In the eleventh month I did the adding and she was watching me and she said, you are enjoying this. It is twenty-five minutes and it is the thing I look forward to in the month.* **The third:** the health. The blood pressure controlled, the alcohol stopped for the first eight months and thereafter occasional, and the sleep restored. His account: *I have not had a drink at home in about a year and a half and I did not make a decision about it. The doctor told me in the fourth month that the pressure was down and I noticed that I had stopped needing it, and I think the reason is that there was nothing left to carry at the end of the day.*

**The finding the case records as its own conclusion.** Emeka, at the review: *Two years ago I was a man who had a name and no money and one big client and a wife who was carrying my house while I was being the provider. Today I am a man who has two vehicles fewer, one house fewer, one client fewer, one title fewer, and one flask of my own blood pressure tablets on the table and one wife who knows every figure in my business, and I do the accounts with her on the last Sunday of the month. I would not go back. And I want to record one more thing: my son, who is eleven, watched me sell the vehicle and he asked me why, and I told him the truth in four sentences, and I have been told by a man who knows the family that he is now telling his friends at school that his father is a builder who does not lie about money. I did not teach him that. I lost it in front of him and he learned it.*

Five features of this case deserve isolation.

**The position was successful according to every external measure.** Emeka had a name, work, a house, vehicles, the children in school, and standing in his community and his association, and the case is included because the chapter's material is not addressed to men in visible difficulty. The specific finding: his position's collapse was not produced by a failure of competence but by the mechanism of Section 42.2's second cost, and his competence was in fact the reason the pretence was sustained for so long.

**The pretence was financed by the wife's concealed labour.** Grace had been conducting the household's monthly expenditure from her business's income for four years, and Emeka had never stated it. This is the fourth domain of Section 42.1 in its most frequent form, and the case is included because the finding is reported across the case material of this book: the man's account of himself as the provider is frequently maintained by a party who is not told that she is the provider.

**The seven signs were all present and each was treatable as an ordinary difficulty.** The sleep, the temper, the withdrawal, the explaining, and the rehearsed discovery. The case is included because it records the reader's own likely position: the signs of Section 42.4 are present in the man's material for a period measured in years, and they are accounted for as the ordinary conditions of a man's life.

**The disclosure to the one person and the household altered the position before an arrangement was constructed.** The surveyor's three statements, including the statement about Grace, and Grace's account of her own part. The case is included because the finding is the chapter's central claim about the mechanism: the position's facts were unchanged at the disclosure and the man's conduct of them was altered immediately, and the case material records the same finding in every domain of this book.

**The position at the true level was smaller and better.** Fewer clients, fewer vehicles, one house fewer, one title fewer, and a business that was profitable rather than a business whose appearance consumed it. The case's conclusion, in the man's own voice, is the chapter's closing statement, and its final item — the son who learned from the loss — is the material of Chapter 43 in its preliminary form.
