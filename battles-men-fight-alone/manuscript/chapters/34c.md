## 34.3 The Five Dangerous Silences

Five categories of silence carry a specific risk, and each requires a different response. They are the chapter's practical subject.

### Silence One: The Silence About a Risk to the Man's Own Life

The most urgent category by a substantial margin, and the one the chapter addresses first and most directly.

**The material.** Thoughts that the man would be better off absent; the wish not to wake up; the sense that others would manage better without him; the specific plan; the rehearsal; the note that has been written and not left; the giving away of things; the arrangement of affairs; the purchase; the drive; the risk taken with the man's own life on the road or in the water or by the medication. The material is frequently not expressed as the wish to die, which is the reason men in this category are missed: the man's own account is frequently of tiredness, of being unable to see a way through, of a weight, of not being able to continue, or of a specific and unshakeable conviction that his family would be better without him.

**The specific risk.** Chapter 27.3 supplied the research on isolation and suicide; Chapters 30.7 and 31.4 supplied the elevated risk in specific groups of men. The relevant and urgent point is that men's suicide rates are substantially higher than women's while their reported depression and help-seeking rates are lower, which means that a substantial proportion of the material in this category is never presented to any service and is held, if it is held anywhere, by the man.

**The requirement.** The silence in this category must not be maintained, and the requirement is professional contact rather than a plan. The position is stated plainly because it is the position of the research and of clinical practice: a man who is carrying thoughts of ending his own life, or who has formed the conclusion that his household would be better without him, requires an assessment by a professional — a general practitioner, a mental health service, a crisis line, an emergency department — and requires it before anything else in this book is conducted. Section 34.5 supplies the plan and the routes.

**What the man should know.** The material in this category responds to treatment. The belief that the state is a permanent assessment of the man's situation, rather than a condition that has altered the man's perception of his situation, is a symptom of the condition and not a fact about the man's life. This is the single most important sentence in the chapter, and it should be read as a statement about the evidence and not as encouragement.

### Silence Two: The Silence About an Addiction

**The material.** The drinking, the substances, the gambling, the sexual conduct, the screens, the spending, and the use of any behaviour as the principal means of managing the man's state. The material of Chapter 23 applies in full, including the secrecy that the behaviour requires and the household's experience of a man whose conduct has altered with no explanation.

**The specific risk.** The progression described in Chapter 23.1, and the recurrence of the emergencies listed in Section 34.5. This category also carries a specific feature: the silence is frequently the behaviour's mechanism, and the man's management of the concealment is what maintains the arrangement.

**The requirement.** The professional assessment, the disclosure to the household in the form Chapter 23 specifies, and the arrangements described in that chapter. Where the man is in acute risk — withdrawal, overdose, the drink-driving, the violence, the inability to stop — the requirement is immediate medical attention.

### Silence Three: The Silence About Violence and About Harm

**The material.** Two directions, and both require treatment.

The man's own violence: toward a spouse, a partner, a child, or anybody else; the conduct he has concealed; the incident he has minimised; the pattern he has not named. The specific risk is the escalation and the household's position, and the requirement is professional intervention, with the safety of the people in the household as the first consideration.

The violence done to the man: the abuse he experienced as a child; the assault; the conduct of a relative; the treatment he has received in a household, an institution, or a period of his life. The silence in this category is frequently of decades, and its feature is that the material is held with the man's own account of why it was his fault or why it did not matter. The material of Chapters 21 and 30 applies, and the professional arrangement is generally required, because the disclosure to a household carries a specific risk of the man's household being drawn into the original arrangement.

**The requirement.** In both directions, professional contact with a service that has the competence for the material: a domestic violence service, a child protection service, a sexual assault service, a counselling service with the relevant experience, or, where a person is at present risk, the emergency route.

### Silence Four: The Silence About a Medical Condition

**The material.** The man's diagnosis that he has not told his household; the symptoms he has not had investigated; the treatment he has discontinued; the medication he has not taken; the condition he regards as inconsistent with his position.

**The specific risk.** Chapter 32.7's case is the illustration, and the mechanism is the one described in Section 32.2: the man's answer requirement prevents the question, and the condition progresses in the interval. The category's other specific risk is the men's mortality data, which consistently show that men present later with conditions that are treatable earlier.

**The requirement.** The medical route first, and then the disclosure of the fact to the household, in the form of Section 34.6. The position on the household's entitlement is settled in this chapter and in Chapters 18 and 20: the household cannot make its arrangements while it is uninformed, and the withholding is frequently presented as protection while it functions as an assumption about the household's capacity.

### Silence Five: The Silence About a Financial Collapse

**The material.** The debt that the household does not know about; the business that is failing; the guarantee that has been called; the obligation that cannot be met; the arrangement the man has been managing without disclosure.

**The specific risk.** The material of Chapters 20 and 25. The collapse, when it arrives, is frequently larger than it would have been had it been disclosed earlier, because the man's private management of the position generally involves additional obligations, higher-cost borrowing, and arrangements constructed to conceal. The household's discovery, when it occurs, is the arrangement described in Chapter 20.5: the loss of the position, together with the loss of the household's trust in the man's account of the household's affairs.

**The requirement.** The disclosure of the position in the form of Section 34.6, with the accounting, the arithmetic, and the options; and the professional route for the specific categories: debt counselling, insolvency advice, legal advice on guarantees, financial counselling, and, where the material involves a guarantee or a business partnership, appropriate legal advice obtained before further commitments are made.

## 34.4 The Signs That Silence Has Become Dangerous

The following checklist is for the reader's own position and for his assessment of a man he is concerned about. It is not a diagnostic instrument; it is a list of indicators that practitioners use to prompt a fuller assessment, and any single item from the first group is sufficient to warrant an assessment.

### The Indicators in the Man Himself

**Group One — requiring immediate action:**
- Thoughts that others would be better off if the man were not present.
- The wish not to wake up, or a wish to be absent.
- Any plan, rehearsal, or preparation relating to the man's own death.
- Risk-taking with the man's life: the road, the water, the medication, the confrontation.
- The giving away of possessions or the settling of affairs without a stated reason.

**Group Two — requiring prompt assessment:**
- Sleep that has not restored the man for a period of months.
- The withdrawal from arrangements the man previously maintained.
- The escalation of drinking, gambling, spending, screens, or risk.
- The collapse of the man's patience with people he is ordinarily patient with.
- The conclusion that his situation is unendurable and that nothing can be altered.
- The avoidance of a specific person, place, or subject that the man is unable to explain.
- A physical presentation that a practitioner has investigated and found no cause for.
- The man's own statement that he cannot continue.
- The cessation of the man's interest in matters that previously occupied him.

**Group Three — the man's own material:**
- The material is operating somewhere other than in a decided arrangement.
- The maintenance of the silence has become an activity in the man's life.
- The silence is no longer protecting what it was intended to protect.
- The man has not stated the silence's purpose in any form, including to himself.

### The Indicators in the Man's Household and Setting

**What the household sees:**
- A change in the man's temper, availability, or presence with no explanation.
- The man's management of a subject, or a room that changes when a matter is raised.
- The household's construction of an account of the man's state that it does not test.
- The administration of the man by the household: the children's selections, the spouse's arrangements, the occasions organised around him.

**What the organisation sees:**
- The man's withdrawal from settings he previously maintained.
- The performance of performance: the work maintained while the man's engagement has emptied.
- The deferral of decisions that the man previously took promptly.
- The team's management of the man: the matters not raised, the subject avoided, the meeting deferred.

**What the practitioner sees:**
- The presentation of a physical complaint with the material unmentioned.
- The consumption data: alcohol, sleep, appetite, weight, work hours.
- The enquiry's own answers: the man who declines every question about the state and answers all the questions about the details.
