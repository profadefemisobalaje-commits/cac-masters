## 42.3 How the Pretence Is Maintained

The four mechanisms, and the section is placed here because a man's pretence is generally conducted by these four instruments and each can be identified in his own conduct.

### Mechanism One: The Script

**The material.** The pretence requires a consistent account, and the man composes it. The fact stated in the same form to each party; the figure stated consistently; the answer to the recurrent question prepared; the account of the business, the employment, the project, or the marriage rehearsed; the details that are added in order to be convincing and that must then be remembered.

**The specific features.** The script is generally short, and it contains a small number of load-bearing items: a figure, a date, a client, a project, a plan, a state of affairs. The case material records the finding that the script's load-bearing items are the ones that eventually fail, and that the man's own memory of them degrades: *I had told three people three versions of the same figure and I could not remember which one I had told my wife.*

**The identification.** The reader should write the recurrent questions and the answers he has prepared: the income, the position, the business, the project, the marriage, the health, the household's arrangements.

### Mechanism Two: The Avoidance of the Meeting Points

**The material.** A pretence survives by keeping its parties apart. The specific forms: the correspondence collected; the call taken outside the room; the two relatives not invited at once; the colleague not brought to the house; the account statement not opened; the appointment not made; the occasion not attended; the question not asked; the domain's specific meeting point avoided — the household budget meeting, the clinic, the professional body's register, the association's meeting, the extended family's gathering.

**The particular finding.** The avoidance is the mechanism that produces the vigilance cost of Section 42.2, and its specific feature is escalation, because the number of meeting points increases with the pretence's age. The finding from the case material: the man's own record of the meeting points he has been avoiding is the most useful single instrument of the chapter's exercise, because the list is generally longer than he expects and each item is an arrangement he is conducting.

### Mechanism Three: The Borrowing Against the Future

**The material.** The pretence is financed, and it is financed from the future: the facility, the instalment, the arrangement with a supplier, the family's assistance, the sale of an asset, the deferred maintenance, the unpaid contribution, the borrowing from a friend, the second employment conducted in the hours that were the household's, the contract accepted at a loss, the asset pledged.

**The specific feature.** The mechanism defers the position's discovery and increases the position's size, which is Cost Two of Section 42.2. Its most consequential form in the case material is the facility secured against the household's principal asset, which converts a financial difficulty into the household's loss of the house, and its second most consequential form is the guarantee: the man's own guarantee given to another party, and the man's request that a relative or a friend guarantee his facility, which is the mechanism that draws parties into the man's position and that Chapter 38.6's case records in the person of the brother-in-law.

**The identification.** The reader should record every arrangement entered in the previous twenty-four months that deferred a position, and the party who is exposed by it.

### Mechanism Four: The Maintenance of the Appearance

**The material.** The appearance is the pretence's instrument — the standing, the vehicle, the house, the schooling, the occasions, the contributions, the clothing, the office, the gifts, the hospitality — and it is maintained because it is the account from which the man's income, credit, and standing are derived.

**The specific feature.** The mechanism has an economic logic that the case material records as the pretence's most effective justification: the appearance produces the credit and the business, and the credit and the business produce the income, and the man therefore presents the argument — which is frequently true — that the appearance is his instrument rather than his vanity. The finding the chapter supplies in response: **the appearance is a legitimate instrument up to the point at which its maintenance requires the concealment of the position.** Chapter 25.6's three prohibitions apply, and the specific case material finding is that businesses and practices conducted from a reasonably presented and accurately understood position outperform those conducted from an appearance whose maintenance consumes the position.

**The identification.** The reader should record: the items of appearance, their annual cost, and the specific items whose maintenance requires a fact to be concealed.

## 42.4 The Seven Signs That It Is Failing

A pretence fails before it is discovered. The following signs are the ones the case material records, and the reader should note that the signs are the man's own and that he has generally been treating them as ordinary difficulties.

### Sign One: The Man Cannot State His Position in Figures

The sign that is present in every case in the material and that the man reports as the moment at which he knew. The specific form: the man is asked an ordinary question — what the household's monthly requirement is, what he owes, what the income is, what the school fees come to, what he has in the account — and he cannot answer without constructing it.

**The instruction.** This sign alone should conduct Section 42.6's first step, and it is the reason Chapter 38's statement is the chapter's first instrument.

### Sign Two: The Explaining Has Become Routine

The man finds himself explaining, in the ordinary course of the week, matters that require no explanation: the late arrival, the call, the visitor, the absence, the appointment, the amount, the delay, the arrangement. The finding: the explaining is the script's maintenance, and its routine presence is the sign.

### Sign Three: The Man Is Avoiding a Named Party

The specific party whose questions he will not face: the wife's account, the colleague, the relative, the accountant, the professional, the creditor, the man who would ask the right question. The finding: the party's name is generally known to the man and he has been arranging the avoidance for a period measured in years.

### Sign Four: The Arrangements Are Conducted in the Gaps

The letters opened in the vehicle; the calls taken outside; the account's statement kept elsewhere; the appointment conducted in the working day; the arrangements conducted at hours when the household is not present; the specific arrangement of the man's day around the pretence's requirements. The finding: the man's timetable contains the pretence, and the reader should draw it.

### Sign Five: The Man Has Withdrawn from the Occasions

The family's gathering, the association's meeting, the community's occasion, the school event, the marriage's social arrangements, the friend's visit. The withdrawal is conducted on the ground of work, of fatigue, of the position's demands, and its actual mechanism is the avoidance of the setting in which the pretence may be punctured.

### Sign Six: The Sleep, the Drinking, or the Temper Has Altered

The material of Chapters 22, 23, and 34. The finding from the case material: a pretence's cost is borne by the sleep first, then by the drinking or the eating, and then by the man's temper in the household, and the sequence is generally reported in that order.

### Sign Seven: The Man Has Begun to Think About the Discovery

The sign that the position is failing rather than merely inconvenient: the man's own arrangements of the discovery — what he would say, whom he would tell first, what he would do, where he would go. The finding: the man's rehearsals of the discovery are frequent, and their presence in his thinking is the clearest available sign of the position's state. The case material records the specific form: the man who has conducted the discovery in his thinking for a year and who has not conducted it in fact.

::: keypoint
A pretence is maintained by four mechanisms: the script, the avoidance of the meeting points at which the parties would compare accounts, the borrowing against the future, and the maintenance of the appearance from which the man's income and standing are derived. It fails before it is discovered, and the seven signs are that the man cannot state his position in figures; his explaining has become routine; he is avoiding a named party; his arrangements are conducted in the gaps of the day; he has withdrawn from the occasions; his sleep, drinking, or temper has altered; and he has begun to rehearse the discovery in his own thinking.
:::
