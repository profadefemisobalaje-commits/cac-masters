### Clause Four: The Control

**The common form.** *A man controls his household. A man is the head of the family. A man's word is the decision.*

**What it actually requires.** In the case material, the clause as held requires that the man's determination prevail in the matters of consequence, that his household's arrangements be conducted by him or by his direction, that his judgement not be publicly contradicted, and that the household's difficulties be referred to him for resolution.

**The distinction, and this clause requires the most careful examination in the chapter.** The obligation of responsibility is authentic: the man is accountable for the household's position, and the material of Parts III to V establishes that a man's conduct of the household's affairs is real work with real consequences. **What the clause adds is the conversion of accountability into authority.** Accountability is a relation to consequences: the man answers for the household's position. Authority is a relation to persons: the man's determination governs the household's members. The two are not the same thing, and their confusion produces the specific material of this book: the man who is accountable and who therefore believes he must decide; the household that is required to receive the arrangement rather than participate in it; the wife who manages the household and is not consulted about its conduct; the children who are conducted.

**The consequence.** The case material records four consequences. **First: the household's information is reduced**, because a member who is conducted does not report, which is the material of Chapter 31.1's second effect in its household form. **Second: the man's load is increased**, because the arrangement in which every decision refers to him is the arrangement of Chapter 33.2's second effect. **Third: the household's capacity is not developed**, which is the finding most frequently reported by the household members themselves: *I stopped deciding anything, because there was no point.* **Fourth: the man's equality as a party is lost**, which is the finding of Chapters 18.6, 20.6, and 26.5: the household's substantive arrangements — the marriage's content, the children's confidence, the disclosure function — require a different relation between the parties than the one the clause's control element supplies.

**The revision.** The accountability is maintained, and its conduct is specified: the man answers for the household's position; the household's members participate in the arrangements that concern them; the decisions are distributed according to the matters and the competences; the man states his determination as a position rather than as an order; and the household's members' capacity is developed rather than protected from requirement. The material of the four arrangements in Chapters 38.6 and 40.6 supplies the form.

### Clause Five: The Achievement and the Status

**The common form.** *A man is measured by what he achieves and what he owns. A man does not stop. A man makes his mark.*

**What it actually requires.** The clause requires that the man's value be computed from his position, his income, his property, his title, his reputation, and his standing in the community; that the computation be continuous; and that any reduction in the position be a reduction in the man.

**The distinction.** The obligation of meaningful work is authentic and the material of Parts VI and VII: the work is real, its products are real, and a man's satisfaction in what he has built is a legitimate good. **What the clause adds is the identification of the man with the position**, which is Chapter 25.5's material in its clause form. The consequence: the man who is not his position cannot be reduced by the position's reduction, and the man who is his position has no remainder.

**The consequence.** Three outcomes are recorded in the case material. **The first is the perpetual insufficiency:** because the computation is continuous and comparative, the position is always short of the available comparison, and the man's satisfaction is always deferred to the next item. **The second is the exposure to the collapse:** the material of Chapter 38, in which the man's identity is destroyed with his position and in which the reconstruction is therefore not a reconstruction. **The third is the release at the end:** the material of Chapter 40.3's fourth loss, in which the man discovers that his own account of his life is composed of the position's items and contains nothing of his own.

**The revision.** The work and the achievement are maintained, and the computation is altered: the man's measure is his whole conduct, as Chapter 25.5 established — the conduct in his household, his obligations, his work, his conduct with the people who cannot benefit him, and his own life — and his position is an item among them rather than the whole of them. The specific form: the man's statement of his value contains items that the audience cannot see.

### The Three Additional Clauses

The case material records three further clauses that appear frequently enough to require their own treatment.

**The clause of the comparison.** *A man must be at least the equal of the men around him.* Its actual requirement: the man's conduct is conducted in relation to a comparison group, and the choice is made by the comparison's demands rather than the man's material. Its consequence: the house that is larger than required, the vehicle, the occasion, the schooling, the gifts, the borrowing, and the arrangements of Chapter 38.3's second error.

**The clause of the endurance.** *A man continues. A man does not stop. A man carries it.* Its actual requirement: the man's continuation regardless of the position's actual arithmetic, and its consequence is the material of Chapters 33, 37, and 36: the man who continues until the position produces his collapse. Its revision is Chapter 37's material in its clause form: the continuation is maintained for the obligations that warrant it, and the arithmetic is conducted.

**The clause of the invulnerability to feeling.** *A man does not become attached to that extent; a man does not let it affect him.* Its actual requirement: the reduction of the man's attachment to his household, his children, his friends, and his work to a level at which the losses do not reach him. Its consequence is the material of Chapter 39.3: the man who is not moved by his own household's life, and whose account of his losses is that nothing has happened to him.

## 41.3 How the Definition Is Enforced Without an Enforcer

The section supplies the mechanism, and it is the material that makes the chapter's work possible: a definition that is enforced by an institution is altered by altering the institution, and a definition that is enforced by the man himself is altered by the man.

### Mechanism One: The Internal Examination

**The mechanism.** The man conducts a continuous examination of his own conduct against the definition's clauses and records his compliance. The case material supplies its forms: the man who reports that he has not cried since 1996 and states the year as an item of his own conduct's record; the man who describes a night's sleep as *a weakness*; the man who recites the number of years he has not taken a day of leave, as in the case of Chapter 37.7.

**The finding.** The examination's principal feature is that the man does not agree with the definition; he **is** the definition in his own account of himself. The material of Chapter 39.2's sixth stage: the man has become the exterior, and the interior's examination is conducted by the exterior's rules.

### Mechanism Two: The Audience's Monitoring

**The mechanism.** The audience of Chapter 25.2: the wife's household, the extended family, the community, the colleagues, the staff, the neighbours, and the men of the man's cohort, all of whom hold the definition and who record the man's position against it. The mechanism's forms: the remark about another man's conduct; the account of a man who *let his wife decide*; the observation that a man *has changed* since the difficulty; the question about what the man is building.

**The finding.** The audience's authority is imposed by the man and not by the audience, which is the material of Chapter 25.6's third prohibition. The audience's remark produces the man's response because the man assents to the audience's standing as a judge, which is the mechanism on which the definition's enforcement depends.

### Mechanism Three: The Consequence That Was Real

**The mechanism.** The man's own experience, as Source Seven established: the occasions on which the definition's violation produced an actual cost. The disclosure that was used against him; the expression that was met with ridicule; the assistance that was not given; the financial disclosure that altered his household's regard; the request that was refused; the departure that occurred when he became less available.

**The finding.** **The definition's enforcement is strongest at this mechanism and its examination must be conducted here**, because the man's evidence is real and the chapter's instruction is not that the evidence should be disregarded. The instruction is that the evidence should be re-examined in the form the chapter supplies in Section 41.4's eighth test: the specific form of the reasoning by which a man constructs a general rule from a specific event, which is generally a single event, frequently an event of his adolescence, and almost never the whole of the available evidence.

### Mechanism Four: The Substitution of the Man's Own Record

**The mechanism.** The man's account of his own worth, conducted without the definition's assistance. The material of the internal ledger in Chapter 33.5 and of the measure in Chapter 25.5: the man's own record of what he has carried, what it cost him, and what it produced, held in his own accounting.

**The finding.** The mechanism's presence is the chapter's objective and it is the reason the exercise in Section 41.9 requires the construction of the man's own statement. The case material's finding: **a man who has constructed his own record cannot be reduced by the audience's accounting, because he has an accounting.** The audience's remarks continue; the man's assent to them does not.

### The Two Instruments of the Examination

The section closes with the two instruments that the chapter's remaining sections provide, and the reader should hold them together.

**The first instrument: the eight tests.** Section 41.4's tests by which a clause is examined. Their function is analytical: they establish what the clause requires, whether it is possible, what it produces, and what it omits.

**The second instrument: the substitution.** Section 41.6's exercise, in which each clause is replaced by specific conduct in the reader's actual circumstances. Its function is constructive: a definition is not altered by its analysis. The case material is unambiguous on the point — a man may state the clause's defect accurately and conduct it in the following week — and the substitution is what replaces the statement with conduct.
