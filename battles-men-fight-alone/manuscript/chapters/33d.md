## 33.4 Boundaries Without Coldness

The correction of the role begins with the distinction between the obligations a man owes and the demands that have attached themselves to him. The chapter supplies four tests, and they are applied to each demand before the man answers it.

### The Four Tests

**Test One: Whose responsibility is this in principle?**

The question is not whether the man can address the matter but whether it belongs to him. Its applications: the relative whose children's fees are unpaid is, in principle, responsible for those fees; the association's officer is responsible for the association's accounts; the man whose colleague has a difficulty is responsible for the conduct of a colleague and not for the colleague's marriage; the grown nephew who needs a place to stay is responsible for his own arrangements, in principle, though not always in capacity.

The test's function is the reversal of the arrangement's default, in which every demand belongs to the man because it was brought to him. Applied consistently, it identifies the demands that are genuinely his and the demands that are somebody else's responsibilities which he has been meeting.

**Test Two: Whose capacity is this?**

The second test addresses the fact that other adults are generally present and their capacity is unexamined. The questions: who else in this family could meet this demand; has anybody asked them; what is the reason they have not been asked; and what does the man's arrangement tell them about their own obligations?

The test's practical value is substantial, and it is the finding that most frequently changes men's arrangements: in a large proportion of cases, the other parties can help, they have not been asked, and they have learned that the man will manage. The material on the delegation of the load in Section 33.6 develops the point.

**Test Three: Is this mine to do, or mine to make possible?**

The distinction between the man's own act and the arrangements the man can create. Where the demand is genuinely the man's to meet, the third test asks whether he should meet it personally or construct the arrangement: the referral, the introduction, the guaranteed loan instead of the gift, the family's joint contribution, the institution's route, the person who is better placed.

The test's function is the multiplication of the man's capacity. A man who can cause a matter to be addressed by the right person is worth considerably more to his family than a man who addresses everything himself, and the second arrangement is the one that consumes him.

**Test Four: What will this cost my household, and has that cost been counted?**

The final test and the one the role's default omits entirely. The questions: what does this demand reduce or postpone in my household; who in my household will pay for it; has that payment been counted; and has my household been consulted?

The test's function is not the automatic refusal of demands. Its function is that the household's cost becomes visible in the man's own decision, which is the arrangement that the household is entitled to and which the man has generally never conducted.

### The Four Answers Available

Once the tests have been applied, the man's response is one of four, and the fourth is the one the role excludes.

**1. The full assistance.** The demand is his, the capacity is his, and the household's cost is counted and accepted. The response is conducted with the amount, the date, and the arrangement stated, and the practice of stating it is worth adopting: an assistance whose terms are unstated is the arrangement that produces the ledger of Chapter 20.

**2. The partial assistance.** The man meets the part he can meet, and states the remainder: what he will do, what he will not, and what is required of the person asking. The response is generally the most useful of the four and it is the one that the role's default makes unavailable: a man who has always met everything whole cannot offer a part.

**3. The arrangement instead of the payment.** The referral, the introduction, the guarantee instead of the gift, the employment instead of the money, the contribution made conditional, the other party brought in. The response requires more of the man's attention than the payment and considerably less of his resources, and it is the form whose record in the case material is the most favourable for both parties.

**4. The refusal.** The demand is not his, or the capacity is not his, or the cost to his household is not one he will impose, and the answer is no. The refusal's form matters and the chapter supplies it in Section 33.5.

### The Refusal and Its Form

The refusal is available and it is expensive, and the chapter records the position accurately: a man's refusal may cost him the family's account of him, a relationship, or his standing in a community, and the cost is real and should not be minimised. The material on the refusal's form is in the practice literature on boundaries, and its essential features are three.

**The refusal is short.** The lengthy explanation, the justification, the apology, and the account of the man's own difficulties convert the refusal into a negotiation and supply the other party with the material to argue. The forms that work are one or two sentences.

**The refusal does not argue about the demand's legitimacy.** The man should not dispute that the need is real. The refusal concerns his capacity, his household's position, or the responsibility's location, and the form is: *I am not able to do this one. Here is what I can do* — with the alternative supplied where one exists.

**The refusal is consistent.** The man who refuses once and complies the following week has taught the family that the refusal is a negotiation. The consistency is what establishes the arrangement, and men who have done it report that the family's demands adjust within a period of months to the boundary that has been established, and that the family's account of the man adjusts more slowly and generally in a less hostile direction than the man feared.

## 33.5 Assistance and Rescue

The chapter's ethical material, and the section treats it because the role's most damaging form is not the excess of a man's generosity but a pattern in which his help maintains the difficulty it is intended to solve.

### The Distinction

**Assistance** is the conduct that supplies what the person requires in order to address his own matter: the money that closes a gap, the introduction that produces work, the advice that changes a decision, the temporary place to stay while arrangements are made, the support during an illness, the assistance with a procedure.

**Rescue** is the conduct that removes the matter from the person and places it with the man: the debt paid without any change in the arrangement that produced it; the position found for a person who will not perform it; the relative whose recurrent difficulty is met every time it recurs; the man who takes on the consequences of somebody else's conduct in order to spare the person the consequence.

### The Two Markers of Rescue

**The matter recurs.** The most reliable marker. Where the same difficulty arrives repeatedly and the man's intervention has not altered the arrangement that produces it, the assistance is functioning as a rescue and the recurrence is its evidence.

**The man's involvement is required for the matter to be addressed at all.** Where the person cannot address the difficulty without the man, the person's own capacity is not being used, which is the arrangement's mechanism. The exception is genuine incapacity — a child, a person with a disability, a person in an acute crisis — in which the assistance is appropriate and the arrangement is different.

### Why Rescue Is Difficult to Refuse

Four reasons, and each requires a response.

**The person's need is real.** It generally is. The distinction is not between a real need and an imagined one; it is between the assistance that addresses the need and the rescue that conceals the arrangement producing it.

**The alternative appears to be the person's harm.** The person may indeed be harmed in the short term by the refusal: the fees unpaid, the arrangement lost, the consequence imposed. The material on this point is derived from the practice literature on enabling and its finding is consistent: the short-term harm of the consequence is generally less than the long-term harm of the arrangement's continuation, and the man's judgement of the comparison is frequently distorted by his own discomfort at the person's distress.

**The family expects the man's intervention.** Which restores the arrangement of Section 33.2 and which is the reason the family's own capacity has not developed.

**The man's own position.** The role's returns, described in Section 33.2, are the reason the rescue is difficult to decline: the rescue is what supplies the standing, the identity, the company, and the avoidance.

### The Corrected Form

The assistance that addresses the arrangement rather than the episode, and its four forms are as follows.

**The assistance made conditional on the change.** The payment of the fees on condition that the enrolment, the attendance, or the arrangement is corrected; the support on condition that a treatment arrangement is entered; the money supplied in a form that addresses the source — the capital, the training, the tool — rather than the shortfall.

**The assistance supplied in the arrangement rather than to the person.** The family's joint contribution; the loan with terms rather than the gift; the payment made to the institution rather than to the person; the employment of the relative in the man's own business with a stated duty.

**The assistance supplied with a limit and a stated duration.** The place to stay for three months; the support until the end of the term; the payment this once. The limit's function is that the arrangement has a date, which places it in the material of Chapter 24: a matter that has a time and a boundary is a matter that can be conducted.

**The assistance replaced by the man's construction of the person's own capacity.** The training, the introduction, the reference, the instruction, the recommendation, the teaching of the arrangement. This is the slowest form and it is the only one that alters the position permanently, and it is the form the case material in Section 33.7 illustrates.
