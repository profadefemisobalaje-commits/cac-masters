## 45.4 Reconnecting with Purpose and Belonging

The two previous sections addressed the release of exclusive conduct and the reconciliation with limitation. This section addresses the two structures on which a man's life is conducted after both have been undertaken: purpose and belonging.

The section treats them together because the case material finds that they fail together. A man with purpose and no belonging conducts a project in isolation and frequently abandons it. A man with belonging and no purpose is comfortable and unproductive, and the discomfort of the second condition is frequently more dangerous than the difficulty of the first, because it is not experienced as a problem. Purpose supplies the direction and belonging supplies the company; a life requires both.

### Purpose: The Four False Versions

The word purpose is used in the culture with a vagueness that prevents the reader from examining his own. The section therefore begins by stating the four things that purpose is frequently taken to be and is not.

**The first false version is the grand purpose.** The belief that purpose is a single, large, world-altering contribution, which the ordinary man either possesses or lacks. The version is transmitted by the literature of achievement and it produces two outcomes: the man who has not identified his grand purpose concludes that he has none, and the man who has identified one frequently neglects the obligations of his ordinary position in order to pursue it.

**The second false version is the purpose that is discovered.** The belief that purpose is a hidden object which the man must find, and that its discovery will resolve his uncertainty. The version produces the search that never concludes, and the case material records men who spent a decade in the search and never conducted the work that was in front of them.

**The third false version is the purpose that is the man's own.** The belief that purpose consists of the man's fulfilment, his self-expression, and his own development. The version is the material of Chapter 40's permissions in their distorted form, and its difficulty is that it removes the obligations by which purpose is conducted in a life that includes other people.

**The fourth false version is the purpose that arrives with the position.** The belief that purpose is supplied by the man's role: the promotion, the title, the appointment, the ownership of the enterprise, the leadership of the household. The version is the material of Part IX's loneliness, since a purpose that is contingent on a position ends with the position, and the case material records the collapse of men whose purpose was conducted entirely through their roles.

### Purpose: The Working Definition

The section supplies a working definition in the form employed in Chapter 41.6, and it states the definition's components.

**Purpose, in this chapter's sense, is the answer to the question: what is my position for, and what will be different because I conducted it?**

The definition has three components.

**The first component is a position.** Purpose is not a general statement about the man's life. It is an answer about a position that he actually occupies — a household, a trade, a profession, an association, a community, a body of work — and the case material's finding is that the answer becomes usable only when it is attached to a position. The man who asks what he should do with his life is frequently conducting the search instead of his work. The man who asks what his position is for is conducting an answerable question.

**The second component is a contribution that is not the man's own benefit.** The contribution may be small, and it must be identifiable. The household that is stable. The trade that is conducted honestly. The apprentice who is taught. The association that is administered well. The community's water supply that works. The children who are able to speak about what they carry. The finding is that men who locate their purpose in a contribution are substantially less vulnerable to the collapse of their positions than men who locate it in their roles, since the contribution can be conducted from a smaller position.

**The third component is an outcome that can be observed.** The purpose must produce something the man can see. The case material's finding is that men who conduct purposes that cannot be observed lose their direction within a year, and that the observation may be small: the account that balances, the boy who returns, the workshop that is clean, the record that is accurate, the household that has its evening meal.

The definition's test is stated in three questions: **What is my position? What is it for? What would be different, and how would I know?** A man who cannot answer all three has a sentiment rather than a purpose, and the sentiment is not the material.

### The Four Foundations of a Working Purpose

The section supplies four foundations, and the reader should note that they are not sequential. They are domains, and the case material finds that a purpose conducted on one foundation alone is fragile.

**Foundation One: The household.** The purpose that consists of the household's stability, its children's capacity, its elders' care, and its ordinary arrangements. The foundation is the one most men hold already, and the case material's finding is that it is frequently held without being stated, and that the stating of it is itself useful: the man who states to his household that his position is for them conducts that position differently, and the household's response to the statement is frequently the material the man had not had.

**Foundation Two: The work and the trade.** The purpose that consists of conducting a competence well: the product that is sound, the service that is honest, the apprentice who is trained, the record that is accurate, the standard that is maintained in the trade. The foundation is the one that survives the loss of position, since a man who has located his purpose in the conduct of his competence can conduct it at a smaller scale.

**Foundation Three: The community and the association.** The purpose that consists of a contribution to a body larger than the household: the association, the trade body, the school, the sports club, the community's arrangements, the professional institution. The foundation is the one that supplies the belonging described in the section's second half, and it is frequently the foundation that men in the second half of life most require.

**Foundation Four: The transmission.** The purpose that consists of supplying to a later party what the man has acquired: the apprentice, the junior colleague, the younger men of his community, the next generation of his household, the men described in Chapter 44.5 who are in transition. The foundation is the one the case material finds most reliable in the second half of a man's life, because it is conducted from whatever position the man holds and it does not depend on his continuing capacity.

### Belonging: The Three False Versions

The section turns to belonging, and it begins in the same form.

**The first false version is belonging as popularity.** The man's estimate of his belonging is conducted by the number of parties who know him, greet him, invite him, or speak well of him. The version is the material of Chapter 25.3's imagined audience, and its difficulty is that it supplies no party who would conduct the tests of Section 45.1.

**The second false version is belonging as the household's dependence.** The man's belonging is conducted by being required: the household that cannot function without him, the business that depends on him, the association that would collapse without him. The version is the material of Chapter 33's carrying other people's problems, and its difficulty is that it is an arrangement in which the man is valued for his function and not for himself, and its ending is frequently the retirement or the illness described in Chapter 31.

**The third false version is belonging as the group's approval.** The man's belonging is conducted by his conformity to a group: the peer group, the professional body, the association, the community's body of opinion. The version is the material of Chapter 43.4's propositions, and its difficulty is that the belonging is conditional on the man's not stating what he carries.

### Belonging: The Working Definition

The section supplies a definition, and it is deliberately narrow, because the narrow definition is the one that can be built.

**Belonging, in this chapter's sense, is the condition in which a man has parties who hold his accurate position, whose estimate of him is not conditional on the position's favourable appearance, and to whom he has obligations that he conducts.**

The definition has three components, and each one is required.

**The first component is the accurate position.** The parties know the man's actual position: his income, his difficulties, his history, his limits, his states. The component is the material of Chapter 42.5's second support, and the finding is that belonging without it is an arrangement of appearances, and appearances are what a man's isolation is made of.

**The second component is the unconditional estimate.** The parties' estimate of the man does not depend on his continuing to appear successful. The component is the one that the case material finds decisive, and it is the component that the disclosure tests. The finding is that men frequently discover, on disclosure, that the parties' estimate was never conditional, and that the belief in its conditionality was the man's own construction.

**The third component is obligations that are conducted.** The man has obligations to the parties that he actually conducts, and the component is the one that distinguishes belonging from support. A man who is supported by a party to whom he has no obligations is a recipient. A man who is supported by a party to whom he has obligations is a member, and the case material's finding is that men accept support far more readily when they have something to conduct in return.

### The Construction of Belonging

The section now addresses the reader who has few parties or none, and it states the construction in the order in which the case material finds it effective.

**The first step is the man's own conduct as the instrument.** Belonging is not found; it is built by the man's conduct toward others over a period. The case material's finding is that the specific conduct is the one described in Chapter 44.5's five functions in their reciprocal form: the man becomes present to a party, supplies information, accompanies, states his limit, and continues. The parties who receive that conduct are frequently the parties who become available to him, and the finding is that this is the ordinary mechanism by which men who have lost their parties construct new ones.

**The second step is the setting.** Belonging is conducted in settings, and the settings are ordinary: the association, the trade body, the club, the sports team, the reading group, the community's arrangements, the parents' meeting, the professional body's committee, the place of work's ordinary occasions, and the community's gatherings. The step is the material of Chapter 43.7's fifth design principle in its adult form, and the case material's finding is that a man's belonging is frequently constructed by his accepting a small office in an ordinary setting: the treasurer's role, the committee's secretary, the organiser of a repair, the coach of a junior team.

**The third step is the repetition.** Belonging requires frequency, and the frequency is what converts an acquaintance into a party. The finding is that men frequently conduct a single approach, receive a lukewarm response, and conclude that the party is unavailable, and that the conclusion is drawn from a sample of one. The step's practice is the ordinary one: the same setting, the same day, for a period long enough for the man to become known.

**The fourth step is the disclosure.** The step is the one that converts a setting into a belonging. The man states his accurate position to one party in the setting, in the form of Chapter 43.6's script and at the pace of Chapter 45.2's practice. The case material's finding is that the disclosure is frequently the point at which men report that a setting became a belonging, and that it is frequently conducted after a year or more of ordinary association.

**The fifth step is the obligation.** The man accepts an obligation to the party or the setting and conducts it. The step is the component that distinguishes belonging from support, and its practice is ordinary: the man is the party who telephones on the anniversary, who visits the bereaved household, who collects the contribution, who keeps the record, who accompanies the man to the appointment.

### The Second Half of Life

The section closes with the material that the case material finds most relevant to the book's likely readers, who are frequently men in the second half of their lives.

The second half has three characteristics that alter the construction of purpose and belonging.

**The first characteristic is the closure of the future's options.** The man of fifty-five has fewer available positions than the man of thirty-five, and the case material's finding is that the closure is frequently experienced as a reduction of purpose, when it is in fact a condition of its clarification. A purpose that is conducted from a smaller position is a purpose that has been examined; a purpose that requires an expanding position is a purpose that is contingent on the market.

**The second characteristic is the accumulation of the material.** The man in the second half has conducted difficulties, acquired competences, and observed consequences that the man of thirty-five has not. The material of Chapter 44 is available to him in a quantity that the younger man does not possess, and the transmission of Chapter 44.5's mentoring is frequently the purpose that the second half supplies and the first half cannot.

**The third characteristic is the reduction of the parties.** The parties of a man's first half are frequently reduced by death, migration, divorce, and the ordinary drift that the case material records. The reduction is real and the construction is available, and the case material's finding is that the men who construct new parties in the second half are the men who conduct the four steps above rather than the men who wait for the parties to return.

## 45.5 Building a Healthier Vision of Manhood

The section now states the vision, and it does so in the book's own terms rather than in the culture's, because the reader has been conducting the material of Chapter 41's five clauses and Forty-five chapters of the architecture they constructed.

The vision is stated in six clauses, each of which is the corrected form of a clause that the book has examined. For each, the section states the obligation that is retained and the addition that is removed.

**Clause One: A man provides.** The obligation is retained in full: the man's household has what it requires, and the man is accountable for its arrangements. The addition that is removed is the anonymity of the provision — the arrangement in which the household does not know its own position, the man's income is not stated, and the provision is conducted as a private burden whose size is known only to him. The corrected form is stated in one sentence: **a man provides, and the provision is conducted with the household's knowledge, at its true level, and with the household's participation where the household is capable of it.**

**Clause Two: A man is composed.** The obligation is retained: the man regulates his states and conducts them appropriately in the setting, which is the material of Chapter 39.4's four competences. The addition that is removed is the concealment that has been extended from the setting to the man's whole interior: the arrangement in which he does not know his own states, does not state them to any party, and conducts composure as the absence of an interior life. The corrected form: **a man regulates his states, states them to the parties who have a right to know, and is composed in the settings that require it.**

**Clause Three: A man is capable.** The obligation is retained: the man acquires competence, conducts it honestly, and is accountable for its limits. The addition that is removed is the self-sufficiency that requires the competence to be conducted alone, and that therefore removes the instruments on which competence depends: the second opinion, the correction, the professional advice, the party who examines the decision. The corrected form: **a man is capable, states his limits, and conducts his competence with the instruments that competence requires.**

**Clause Four: A man is accountable.** The obligation is retained: the man is responsible for his household's position and answers for it. The addition that is removed is the conversion of accountability into authority over persons, which reduces the household's information and capacity, and which the case material of Chapter 18 records as the mechanism by which a household becomes an audience rather than a body. The corrected form: **a man is accountable, and his accountability increases the capacity of the parties he is accountable for.**

**Clause Five: A man's work is real.** The obligation is retained: the man's work produces something, and his achievement is a legitimate part of his standing. The addition that is removed is the identification of the man with his position, so that a reduction in the position is experienced as a reduction in the man. The corrected form: **a man's work is real, and the man is not his work.**

**Clause Six: A man endures.** The obligation is retained: the man conducts difficulty without collapse, and his endurance is a genuine competence that the case material of this book records with respect. The addition that is removed is the requirement that the endurance be conducted in silence, in isolation, and at the cost of his health, his marriage, and his capacity to ask. The corrected form: **a man endures, and he does not endure alone.**

### The Six Statements of the Vision

The clauses are stated in the form in which the reader can conduct them, and the form is the one supplied by Chapter 41.5's substitution exercise.

1. **A man provides, and the provision is conducted with the household's knowledge and participation.**
2. **A man regulates his states, states them to the parties who have a right to know, and is composed in the settings that require it.**
3. **A man is capable, states his limits, and uses the instruments that competence requires.**
4. **A man is accountable, and his accountability increases the capacity of those he is accountable for.**
5. **A man's work is real, and the man is not his work.**
6. **A man endures, and he does not endure alone.**

The six statements are the book's positive answer to the question it began with. They are not a new standard, and the reader should note that they are harder than the standard he inherited rather than easier: the provision with the household's knowledge requires a conversation that the old standard permitted him to avoid; the statement of his states requires a competence that the old standard did not teach; the use of instruments requires an admission that the old standard punished; the increase of others' capacity requires a discipline that the old standard did not require; the distinction between the man and his work requires a security the old standard did not supply; and the endurance without isolation requires the whole of the book's material.

### The Vision and the Household

The section states the vision's household form, because the reader's household will conduct its own version of the vision whatever the reader intends, and the case material finds that the household's version is transmitted by conduct rather than by statement.

The household's version is conducted in five arrangements.

**The first arrangement is the stated position.** The household's position is stated to the household, at its true level, at intervals. The arrangement is the material of Chapter 42.6's third step, and its finding is that the household's capacity increases with its information.

**The second arrangement is the stated difficulty.** The household's adults state their difficulties proportionately, at intervals, in the form of Chapter 43.5's practice. The arrangement is the one that supplies the next generation with the curriculum.

**The third arrangement is the repair.** The household's adults conduct their repairs in the household's observation, which is the material of Chapter 43.4's consequence channel and the single most valuable item in the household's curriculum.

**The fourth arrangement is the party.** Each member of the household has a party outside the household to whom they can speak, and the arrangement is stated rather than assumed.

**The fifth arrangement is the obligation.** Each member of the household has an obligation to the household that they conduct, and the arrangement is the one that supplies belonging rather than dependence.

### The Vision and the Community

The vision's community form is the material of Chapter 43.7's six principles and Chapter 44.5's five functions, and the section states it in a single clause because the reader has already received the material: **a community in which a man can be accurate about his position without losing his standing.**

The clause is the vision's public form, and it is testable by the audit of Chapter 43.8: the vocabulary that the community supplies, the permission it states, its response to the last disclosure, the consequence a young man can observe, and the repetition of the arrangement.

The book's confidence in this part of the vision is stated with the restraint that the case material requires. Communities change slowly, the work is conducted by ordinary arrangements, and the reader's contribution is small. The finding that justifies the confidence is the one recorded in Chapter 43.7's account of the association that changed its meetings: **the ten minutes cost nothing, and it is the only part of the meeting that anybody remembers.**
