## 35.7 Illustrative Case: The Man Who Asked the Wrong Question for Six Years

::: casebox
**Composite case.** Constructed for teaching purposes from patterns reported in primary care, vocational counselling, and debt advice practice.

**Godwin is fifty, a self-employed builder with a small contracting business, married to Ekaette for twenty-four years, with two children aged twenty-one and seventeen.**

The material began in 2018 with a contract: a supply and installation project for a private development of eleven houses, for which Godwin had committed his own capital, taken a facility from a microfinance bank, and given a guarantee. The agreement was verbal in its variations and was never documented in its full form. The developer's funding was disrupted in 2019, the work was suspended with about 60 per cent completed, and payments stopped.

Godwin's position at that point was: an outstanding facility of about eight months' income; a sum owed to two suppliers; eight staff and seven artisans to whom wages for about ten weeks were due; and a claim against the developer which he had concluded, incorrectly, had no documentation.

The six years that followed are the substance of the case, and Godwin described them in the terms of this chapter.

**Year one: the reduced request.** Godwin approached two relatives and a colleague. In each case the request was for advice: *an opinion on what I should do about the developer.* He received, in each case, an opinion. His description: *I asked three men for advice. Two of them told me that the developer was a criminal and that I should take him to court. One told me that I should let it go. None of them knew that I had a facility and that my people had not been paid for ten weeks. I did not tell them what I needed, and then I concluded that asking does not help.*

**Years one to four: the management.** Godwin paid the artisan wages from his household's reserves, then from a second facility at a higher rate, then from a rotation of payments in which the two suppliers and the facility were each deferred in turn. The business continued at about half its prior volume, taking small work to cover the week. He did not reduce the household's expenditure. His household's account of the period, as recorded by Ekaette in the family consultation, was: *He was working. He was working more than before, in the evenings and on Saturdays. I believed that the contracts were slow. I did not know that there was a facility until the third year, and I did not know about the guarantee at all until the fifth year.* The household's arrangements in the period were: the elder child's fees paid late twice; the household's car sold in year three with an explanation concerning a mechanical fault; and the family's annual obligation to Ekaette's mother reduced.

**Year four: the presenting complaint.** Godwin attended a general practitioner with insomnia, weight loss, and a raised blood pressure, and was treated for the blood pressure. He did not mention the business. His description of the consultation: *He asked me whether I was worried about anything and I said the business is slow, and he wrote the tablets and said to come back in a month. I told him the truth about everything except the number.*

**Year five: the questions asked of the wrong people.** Godwin approached a lawyer about the developer, who advised that a claim was possible and requested the documents, which Godwin did not produce, being embarrassed by the agreement's informality. He approached a second relative for a loan, requesting the sum in the form of an investment in the business, and was declined. He approached his employer of an earlier period for work. He described the pattern: *I have asked nine people for something in six years. I have asked a lawyer to fight my case. I have asked a relation to invest. I have asked my doctor for tablets. I have not asked one person to look at the whole of it and tell me what to do.*

**Year six: the arrangement.** The position came into a service through the microfinance bank's restructuring officer, after the third deferral, who required a full financial statement of the business and the household. Godwin described the preparation of that statement as the turning point: *It took four days and it was the first time in six years that everything was on one page. When I saw it, I began to understand what I had been doing. I had been carrying eleven different things and answering each one separately, and the whole of it was about twice what I had been able to think about. I did not need advice about the developer. I needed somebody to look at the whole of it.*

The arrangements constructed over the following fourteen months had five elements.

**The professional arrangement.** A debt counselling service, obtained through the bank's restructuring officer, which conducted the consolidated accounting and negotiated with the facility. The negotiation rescheduled the outstanding sum over thirty months and reduced the interest.

**The legal arrangement.** A lawyer was engaged on the developer's claim with the documents that Godwin reconstructed — the correspondence, the delivery notes, the site records, the payment history — and the claim was settled in the second year for about a third of the sum, which was applied to the suppliers.

**The household disclosure.** Godwin conducted the disclosure to Ekaette in year six, in the four-part form: the material, the reason for the silence, the effects the household had already seen — the late fees, the car, the reduced obligation to her mother — and what happened next, with the schedule present. He described the conversation: *She was angry about the car, not about the money. She had believed that I had been cheated on a vehicle repair and she said that if I had told her at the time, we would have kept the car and delayed the school fees and it would have been the same amount. I had not thought about it that way, because I had been making each decision separately.*

**The household's arrangements.** In the third month, Godwin and Ekaette conducted the household meeting with the two children, in which the business's position, the schedule, and the schooling plan were stated. The elder child, who had been told in the period that the fees' delay was a bank matter, learned the actual position six years after the first delay. The account he gave at the meeting is recorded because it is the material of Chapter 34.6's third part: *I have known for four years that something was happening, because he stopped asking me about the business. I did not know what it was and I had decided it was the school fees, and I had planned to leave the university after this year. I will not leave now.*

**The arrangements for himself.** Godwin joined a trade association in the second year and established a small group with three other contractors which met monthly. He described one element as decisive: *The other three have each been where I was. One of them lost a business in the same way in 2016 and he told me the thing I most needed to know, which was that the partnership agreement is a page and that I had been building for twenty-two years on handshakes. I now take the page to every client. The second thing he told me was that I should have gone to the bank in the second month of the suspension rather than in the fifth year, because the restructuring is available to a man who comes early and it is not available to a man who comes late. That is a sentence I could not have got from anybody in my family.*

Godwin's summary, at the end of the case, was: *I asked nine people for something in six years and I did not ask anybody for the right thing. I asked for advice because advice is what I could ask for without saying the number. I have been asked every week by somebody in the trade whether I have the facility, and I have told them the truth since the second year. What I did not know was that the question I could not ask — help me look at the whole of it — was the only one that mattered, and that the man who could answer it was sitting in the association's meeting every month.*
:::

Five features of this case deserve isolation.

**The requests were made and were not the requests the man required.** Nine approaches in six years, all of them in forms that could be answered without the position. This is Section 35.5's reduced request in its most complete form, and the case is included because the pattern is extremely common among men and because it produces the false conclusion that asking does not work.

**The material was held in eleven separate pieces with no consolidated account.** The facility, the two suppliers, the wages, the claim, the household's reserves — each addressed in turn, with the whole invisible. The case records the intervention accurately: the statement took four days and it was the first occasion on which the total had been visible. This is the inventory of Chapter 33.6 in a business context, and the same principle as Chapter 34.7's arithmetic page: the position cannot be addressed until it is on one page.

**The household's explanation of the period was constructed from six years of partial information, and it included a plan the man did not know about.** The car's mechanical fault, the fees' banking delay, the son's decision to leave the university. The case is included because the finding is the chapter's strongest practical argument against the concealment: the household's account of the period was assembled from the man's incomplete explanations, and it had produced arrangements — the son's plan, the household's expenditure — that the man was not aware of and that were made on an incorrect basis.

**The presentation to the practitioner did not include the position.** The doctor's question about worry and the man's answer about the business being slow. The case is included because the material on routine assessment in primary care is consistent with this finding, and because the presentation route is the one available to men who cannot construct the disclosure: the question is asked on a form, and the man answers as far as he can, and the arrangement is established from the point at which he answers further.

**The arrangement that changed the position was a peer arrangement in the man's own trade.** The three contractors, monthly, of whom one had been in the same position and supplied the two sentences the man most required: that the agreement is a page, and that the restructuring is available early and not late. The case is included because the peer arrangement is the source that men most frequently overlook and the one whose material is the most directly usable: the recognition and the technical knowledge are supplied by people who have done the work, which is Chapter 25.3's reference group in a form available to any man who joins his association.
