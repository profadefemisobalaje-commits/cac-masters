## 33.6 Distributing the Load

The chapter's final substantive section concerns the arrangements that convert the man from the family's only solution into one of several load-bearing points.

### The Six Arrangements

**1. The written inventory.** The measurement, and the arrangement on which all the others depend. The man writes down, for one month, every demand he meets, with the amount, the time, the person, the alternative available, and the cost to his household. The practice supplies the only accurate picture of the position the man will ever have, and the finding from the case material is consistent: the total is generally several times the man's own estimate.

**2. The family's own arrangement.** The demands that are carried by a family are, in the arrangement's present form, carried by its most competent member. The correction is the construction of a family arrangement — the family's meeting, the council, the association, the written contribution schedule, the committee for a specific matter — in which the obligations are allocated by the family rather than by the man's availability. The material is in Chapter 26.3, and its value here is that the family's other capacity is developed by being used.

**3. The referral.** The construction of the man's map of the available services and expertise: the institutions, the practitioners, the agencies, the associations, the professionals, and the people with the competence the man lacks. The map is written, and its function is that a proportion of the demands the man currently meets are demands that ought to be met by somebody else, and would be if the man knew who.

**4. The stated arrangement.** The terms of the man's assistance stated to the person: the amount, the date, the condition, the duration, and the requirement. The measure is described in Chapter 20 and its specific value here is that the arrangement prevents the ledger and the resentment: a demand that has been answered with stated terms is a matter that has been conducted, and a demand answered without them is a matter that accumulates.

**5. The arrangements of Chapter 27.** The man's own: the confidant, the group, the professional arrangement, and the place where he is not the provider. This arrangement's function is the one the role has removed: a recipient for the man's own material, which is the requirement that the whole of the previous parts of this book establishes.

**6. The household's arrangement.** The man's spouse and children know the inventory and participate in the decisions about the demands. The arrangement's function is that the household's cost becomes a decision rather than a consequence, and the finding from family practice is that the household's participation, where it has been established, is generally the measure that ends the unexamined accumulation.

### The Development of Other People's Capacity

The most valuable of the arrangements and the slowest, and it deserves its own treatment.

The family's other capacity is generally real and undeveloped, and its development follows a consistent course: the person is asked to do something that he can do; he does it; the man does not intervene; the person's standing in the arrangement increases; and the next matter is brought to him rather than to the man.

Four rules apply to the development.

**Start with the competent members.** The relative who holds a position, the one who has managed an arrangement before, the one who is reliable. Their participation supplies the arrangement's legitimacy and the demonstration to the others.

**Do not perform the task once it is assigned.** The rule most frequently broken, and its violation produces a double result: the task is completed by the man, and the person learns that the assignment was a formality.

**State the contribution in units.** The amount, the monthly sum, the specific duty, the period. A family's arrangement of unquantified contributions resolves into the man's contribution, and the arrangement of quantified contributions distributes the load.

**Develop the next generation deliberately.** The young men in the family should be given arrangements of their own: the duty, the responsibility, the contribution, the representation. The material of Chapter 43 applies, and the finding from the case material is that the young men who are not given arrangements frequently learn the role's wrong lesson as the man's beneficiaries: that the arrangement requires one person to carry it and that they are not that person.

**Composite case.** Constructed for teaching purposes from patterns reported in family practice, community affairs, and counselling.

**Ibrahim is fifty-seven, a pharmacist, the proprietor of two pharmacy outlets in a state capital, married to Fatima for thirty-one years, with four children between the ages of nineteen and twenty-eight, two of whom are at university.**

Ibrahim is the third of nine children of a man who died in 1998, and he is, in the family's arrangement, its principal resource. He was the first of the siblings to obtain a professional qualification, the first to own a business, and, since 2004, the person to whom the family's financial and procedural matters are brought.

The inventory that Ibrahim prepared, at a counsellor's instruction, in the second month of the case was four pages long and it contained about forty items. Its principal elements were as follows.

**The standing monthly commitments:** the maintenance of his mother's house and its staff; the fees of two of his brother's children at a private school; the medical costs of one of his sisters, who had a chronic condition; and the monthly contribution to a family development fund.

**The recurrent requests:** the transport costs of two siblings; the business capital of a brother, supplied three times between 2015 and 2021 and not repaid; the funeral contributions and the ceremony costs of the extended family's occasions, which were met whole by Ibrahim in eight of the previous ten years; and the support of an uncle's household.

**The guarantees and loans:** two bank guarantees given for a brother's business in 2013 and 2017, both called upon; and about eleven personal loans to relatives over fourteen years, of which two had been repaid.

**The procedures and matters:** the family's land dispute, in which Ibrahim held all the documents and conducted the negotiation; the school admissions of four relatives; the hospital arrangements of three; two police matters for a nephew; the burial arrangements of two family members; and the employment of five relatives in his business at various points, of whom two remained.

**The disclosures:** the confidences of his sisters about their marriages; his brother's debts, which only Ibrahim knew; his mother's preference concerning the family house, which only Ibrahim knew; and the family's account of a serious matter involving his eldest brother, which the family had discussed only with Ibrahim.

**The household's position.** The inventory's fourth page concerned Ibrahim's own household, and it was the page he described as the one that changed the case. It contained: the two children at university, whose fees had been paid late on five occasions in six years; the household's inability to complete the building of the family's own house, on which construction had begun in 2011 and had been suspended three times; the postponement of a medical procedure for Fatima for two years; and the fact, recorded in the inventory, that the household's monthly expenditure had been reduced by about a fifth in 2018 and that nobody had explained to the household why.

The case's turning point occurred in relation to Ibrahim's younger brother, Nasir, who had requested a sum for business capital for the fourth time. The inventory contained the first three requests, the amounts, the outcomes, and the fact that neither of the businesses had been established. Ibrahim's account of the matter: *I had the sheets in front of me and I understood that I was not helping my brother. I was the reason that nobody was helping him. His wife was working. His children were in the school that I was paying for. He had four hundred thousand from me over six years and three unfinished plans, and nobody in the family has ever asked him a question, because I am the one who is asked.*

The arrangements Ibrahim constructed over the following two years had six elements.

**The inventory.** Conducted monthly for the first six months and quarterly thereafter, with the household's participation. Its effect on Ibrahim's own understanding was described exactly: *I had believed that I was spending about fifteen per cent of my income on the family. It was thirty-eight per cent, and the number on the paper has never left me.*

**The household's arrangement.** At the third month, Ibrahim presented the inventory to Fatima and, in a household meeting, to the four children. He described the meeting: *My second daughter asked me one question. She asked me whether the family knew that our house was unfinished because of them. I said I did not think they knew. She said, then they are not wicked, they are just uninformed, and you are the one who has kept them uninformed.*

**The family's arrangement.** At the fifth month, Ibrahim called a meeting of the family's adult members — eight of the nine surviving siblings, his mother, and two senior uncles — and presented the inventory, not as a complaint but as an accounting, with the family's assets and the demands set out. He described the meeting's first hour as difficult and its outcome as the point of the case: *I told them I was not going to continue meeting the whole of it and that I was going to state what I would contribute. The argument was that I was the one who had. My uncle, who is seventy-nine, asked the family a question: how many of the nine of us have contributed to anything in ten years. Three of them put up their hands. He said, then the problem is not Ibrahim's.*

The meeting produced a written schedule: contributions in stated monthly amounts from seven of the nine siblings; the school fees of the two children transferred to their own father and to a fund established with monthly contributions from three siblings; the family's development contribution allocated by formula; and a standing committee of four for the family's procedures, of which Ibrahim was one and not the chair.

**The refusals.** Ibrahim issued three refusals in the first year, each in the short form of Section 33.4: the fourth request from Nasir, a guarantee for a cousin's import business, and a demand from an uncle concerning the family land. He described the first as the most difficult and the aftermath: *Nasir did not speak to me for four months. In the fifth month he came to the shop and asked me whether I could help him with the training for the business, and he has started. He did not apologise and I did not require it. The four months were the price and it was smaller than I had feared for eleven years.*

**The referral map.** Ibrahim compiled a written list of the institutions, practitioners, agencies, and professionals to whom the family's non-familial matters should be taken: the lawyer, the estate agent, the bank's small-business desk, the hospital's social worker, the cooperative, and two associations. His account: *Twenty years of answering questions that belonged to an office. The first thing on the list cost me one afternoon and it has removed about a fifth of the calls.*

**The arrangements for himself.** Ibrahim established two: a monthly arrangement with a counsellor, conducted initially in respect of his blood pressure, and a small group of four men — two of them professionals of his age — which met fortnightly in a form with a rule of confidentiality. His account of the group: *I have been the family's ear for twenty-five years. In that room I have an ear. The first time I said a sentence about my own household in front of four men I could not look at any of them, and the second time it was ordinary.*

Ibrahim's summary, three years after the meeting, was: *I did not stop carrying my family and I am not going to. I stopped being the only one who was carrying them and I stopped being the one who was asked about everything. My brother Nasir is running a small provision business and he has repaid about a third of what I gave him before, which he decided himself. My house in the village is finished. My wife had the procedure two years ago. The number on the inventory page has not been above twenty-two per cent since the meeting, and my mother told me last year that she is glad I called the meeting, because she had not known that the family's other seven were thinking about it at all.*

Five features of this case deserve isolation.

**The inventory was the instrument.** Four pages, about forty items, and a total roughly two and a half times the man's estimate. The case is included because the measurement is the intervention: the arrangement was sustainable in the man's account and unsustainable on paper, and the difference between the two is the reason the first arrangement in Section 33.6 is the one on which the others depend.

**The household's position was the element that had never been counted, and it was a person in the household who identified the mechanism.** The unfinished house, the postponed procedure, the reduced expenditure that nobody had explained. The daughter's question and her conclusion — *then they are not wicked, they are just uninformed, and you are the one who has kept them uninformed* — states the case's central proposition, and it is the reason the household's arrangement is placed second in Section 33.6.

**The family's own capacity existed and had not been asked.** Seven of nine siblings contributing, a fund established, a committee of four with the man as a member rather than the chair. This is Test Two of Section 33.4 in operation, and the case is included because the finding is consistent and it is the most encouraging material in the chapter: the family's capacity is frequently present and unexamined, and the man's arrangement is what has prevented its use.

**The refusals were short, they were consistent, and their cost was bounded.** Four months of silence and then a request for training. The case records the outcome precisely, including the man's decision not to require an apology and the fact that the refusal produced, in this instance, the younger brother's first actual attempt. The case is included because the refusal is the material men most fear and because its aftermath, in the case material, is generally smaller than the fear and occasionally better than the original arrangement.

**The man constructed his own arrangements last.** The counsellor and the group came after the inventory, the household meeting, and the family's arrangement. The order is included deliberately: the man who is everyone's solution will not construct arrangements for himself until the load is visible and distributed, and the sequence in the case is the one the chapter recommends.
