Then answer five questions.

- For each friendship, which of the six unstated terms do I rely upon: confidence, non-predation, reciprocity of effort, defence in absence, assistance in crisis, silence about my failures?
- Which of those terms has ever been discussed with the friend?
- If I were hospitalised for three months, what would each of these men actually do — and how do I know?
- What proportion of my support is supplied by one person?

### Part Two: The Betrayal Analysis (Sitting Two)

For the most significant betrayal by a friend or a brother in your life, complete the following.

- **The act, stated factually.** What occurred, when, and what it cost.
- **Which term** of the relationship was relied upon and not held.
- **The evidence that was available, and when.** What signals were present before the act? At what point did they become visible?
- **What the arrangement should have included.** The document, the statement of terms, the test, the disclosure to a third person.
- **What remains true about men.** List, specifically, the men who have kept faith, the assistance that arrived unrequested, and the relationships that have held. The list must be written, and it must be specific; the exercise is not complete without it.

Then complete the four findings from Section 26.5 in your own words, and write the wrong lesson down explicitly — *men cannot be relied upon; I am better off alone* — so that it can be examined rather than operated.

## 26.9 Common Questions Men Ask

**"Is it not weakness to be affected by the loss of a friend?"**

The loss of a friendship is a loss of the same kind as the losses in Chapter 13, and its effects on men's health and functioning are documented. What is missing is the social permission to treat it as such, and the absence of permission is a defect of the environment rather than of the man. The exercise in Part Two is designed to permit the acknowledgement, which is the condition of the work.

**"I have known him for thirty years. How do I reduce the relationship without a war?"**

By reducing the reliance rather than the relationship, which is the option that men most frequently overlook. The friendship can continue — the meetings, the occasions, the ordinary contact — while the confidences, the financial arrangements, the business involvement, and the dependence are removed. The reduction requires no announcement, and men report that the arrangement is sustainable for years, provided that the man does not simultaneously continue to extend the reliance.

**"Should I confront him?"**

Where the material is significant and the relationship matters, yes, and in the direct form from Stage Three: what I know, what it has cost me, and what I intend. The confrontation does not require his agreement and should not seek it. Where the material is a confidence breached or a defence withheld, the direct conversation is difficult and the alternative — silence plus withdrawal — produces the resentful ledger described in Section 26.4.

**"The dispute is with my brother, and it is over our father's property. Where do I begin?"**

With the documents, as the case in Section 26.7 illustrates: the will or the customary position, the title, the records, and a written account of the family's decisions. Then with the appropriate body rather than with the family's conversations. Then with the decision about the relationship, made in writing and reviewed annually. And throughout, with the protection of the next generation, which is the measure that determines whether the matter outlives you.

**"I have been betrayed twice by different men. Is the problem me?"**

Possibly, in the specific and useful sense that the selection is the man's own: the criteria in Section 26.6 are applied at the point of the decision, and a man who has twice extended a substantial reliance without a test of the small ones has not applied them. The finding is not a verdict on the man's character; it is an instruction about procedure, and the procedure — graded reliance, stated terms, more than one confidant — is available immediately.

**"How long does this take?"**

The practical work in this chapter is conducted over months: the audit in a fortnight, the disclosure or confrontation within the first month, the rebuilding plan over six months. The emotional material follows the practical work and requires one to three years, and the men who report the outcome consistently describe it in the terms given in Section 26.5: the incidence reduces, the man encounters the friend at an occasion, and the account has closed without his noticing.

::: summary
**Chapter 26 in brief.** Male friendship supplies four functions — company and activity, practical assistance, standing and membership, and disclosure — and it operates on six unstated terms: confidence, non-predation, reciprocity of effort, defence in absence, assistance in crisis, and silence about the man's failures. Men place a large weight on a small number of relationships, the reliance is practical as well as emotional, and it is entirely unexamined, which is why a betrayal is frequently the discovery that the two parties held different terms rather than the discovery of a wicked act.

Betrayal by a friend takes six forms: the disclosure of a confidence, which damages the man's safety and frequently his standing and is invisible in its effects; the pursuit of the man's interest, which is deniable and which damages the man's model of his own judgement; the failure of assistance in crisis, which frequently involves no decision and is produced by the friend's own incapacity and subsequent guilt; the financial betrayal, which is preceded by accommodations the man performed in the name of the friendship; the failure of defence in absence, which arrives through a third party and is resolved by silent reduction; and the accumulated betrayal, which cannot be stated as an accusation and which produces the general conclusion about trust.

Fraternal betrayal has five forms — the dispute over inheritance or property, the refusal of assistance at a point of obligation, the alliance against the man, the pursuit of his interest, and the disclosure of his history — and four specific difficulties: the relationship cannot be ended by the man's decision, the community does not recognise the injury, the man is frequently required to be the party who concedes, and the matter is frequently never resolved. Its handling is the establishment of the facts and the documents, the bringing of the matter to a body rather than to the family's conversations, the statement of the position once followed by continued presence, an explicit boundary reviewed annually, and the protection of the next generation.

A betrayed friendship passes through six stages: the act; the management of the discovery, in which the most common and most expensive option is to continue as though nothing had occurred; the confrontation, which should take the direct form and which does not require the other party's agreement; the assessment of the relationship, which is the stage that determines the outcome and is most frequently omitted; the outcome, which is repair, revision, or conclusion; and the consolidation, in which the man's understanding is fixed and the lessons are drawn.

The wrong lesson is that men cannot be relied upon; its cost is the removal of the arrangements that most protect men's resilience, and it produces the condition described in Chapter 27. The right lesson consists of four findings: the term that was relied upon without being agreed, the evidence that was available and when, what the arrangement should have included, and what remains true about men. Revised reliance operates on three rules: grade the reliance to the demonstrated record, state the terms of any material arrangement, and maintain more than one confidant and more than one source of assistance. Discerning friendship applies six observable criteria, and the construction of durable arrangements involves reinstating one relationship, adding one group, testing the terms in one friendship, and establishing one arrangement that would operate in a crisis.
:::

## Reflection Questions

1. Complete Part One. How many of your friendships supply the disclosure function, and how much of your support is supplied by one person?
2. For your closest friendships, which of the six unstated terms do you rely upon — and which has ever been discussed?
3. What was the term your most significant betrayal failed to hold, and what was your own contribution to the arrangement that made it possible?
4. Write down the wrong lesson explicitly. Where has it operated in your decisions in the last two years?
5. Complete the fourth finding: what remains true about men? Write the specific list, with names and events.
6. Which of the three rules of revised reliance do you currently violate — grading the reliance, stating the terms, or maintaining more than one confidant?
7. Which relationship will you reinstate, which group will you join, and which term will you state — and what are the three dates?

## Further Reading

- **On men's friendships:** the research literature on male friendship, including the findings on the smaller number and lower intimacy of men's friendships compared with women's, and the evidence on the functions that male friendships serve.
- **On social support and mortality:** the substantial literature associating social connection with mortality and health outcomes, including the consistent finding that men with fewer close relationships face elevated risks.
- **On trust and its violation:** the literature on trust within dyadic relationships, including the findings on the difficulty of repairing integrity-based violations, and the research on how trust is built by graduated reciprocal exchange.
- **On sibling and family conflict:** the research on sibling relationships in adulthood, including the findings on conflict over inheritance and family property, the effects on family structure and on subsequent generations, and the evidence on mediation and on family councils.
- **On mediation:** the practical literature on mediation in family, community, and business disputes, including the evidence on its effectiveness where the parties must maintain a continuing relationship, and the material on the form of mediated settlements.
- **Practical resources:** mediation services for family and commercial disputes; legal advice on property, succession, and partnership matters, obtained early; men's groups with an agreed rule of honesty; and the material in Appendices J and O.
