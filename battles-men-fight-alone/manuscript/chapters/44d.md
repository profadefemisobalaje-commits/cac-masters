## 44.6 Choosing Growth over Bitterness

The chapter's final substantive section addresses the alternative that the case material finds in men who have conducted the material badly: bitterness. Its treatment is placed last because bitterness is not a failure of the earlier sections in general. It is the specific outcome of a particular set of mechanisms, and those mechanisms can be identified, described, and interrupted.

The section begins with a definition, because the word is used loosely and its looseness prevents the reader from examining his own record.

**Bitterness, in this chapter's sense, is a settled account of the world in which the man's injuries are the central fact, the parties who caused them are permanently in debt to him, and the future is pre-emptively discredited on the evidence of the past.** The definition has three components, and each one is required. The injuries must be real — the case material does not apply the word to a man who is complaining about ordinary difficulty, and the misapplication of the word to such a man is a common cruelty. The account must be settled, which is to say that it is not being examined. And the future must be pre-emptively discredited, which is the component that distinguishes bitterness from grief, from anger, and from the ordinary resentment that a man conducts for a season and then leaves.

### The Anatomy

Bitterness has five components, and the case material records them in a consistent order.

**Component One: The injury.** Something was done to the man, or something happened to him, that was genuinely unjust, genuinely damaging, or genuinely undeserved. The first component is the material of Section 44.1's cost ledger, and the reader should note that the chapter does not treat the injury as the man's invention. The finding is that bitterness occurs most frequently in men whose injuries were real, and that the men whose injuries were imagined are generally conducting something else.

**Component Two: The unacknowledged cost.** The injury's cost was not acknowledged by the parties who caused it, by the institutions that should have addressed it, or by the community that observed it. The component is the one that the case material identifies as the most frequent cause of the bitterness's persistence, and its mechanism is specific: an unacknowledged cost remains an open account, and an open account requires attention.

**Component Three: The ledger of the owed.** The man constructs a ledger of what he is owed: the apology, the compensation, the recognition, the position, the years. The ledger is not irrational. It is a claim, and the claim is frequently valid. Its difficulty is that it is held by the man alone, it cannot be enforced, and every day in which it is not settled increases its amount.

**Component Four: The narrowing.** The man's attention narrows to the injury and to the parties who caused it. His reading, his conversation, his professional judgement, and his account of new events are conducted through the injury's categories. New parties are assessed by their resemblance to the parties who caused the injury, and new events are interpreted as repetitions of the original. The component is the material of Chapter 29.5's recurrence, and its effect is the loss of information: the man's capacity to perceive his present position accurately is reduced by the instrument he is using to perceive it.

**Component Five: The confirming arrangement.** The man's conduct produces the evidence that confirms his account. He is suspicious, and parties withdraw; he is contemptuous of the institutions, and they exclude him; he tests new relationships for betrayal, and they fail the test; he declines opportunities because they come from the wrong quarter, and his position does not improve. The component is the most consequential, because it converts the account into a self-fulfilling arrangement, and it is the mechanism that the case material records as the point at which bitterness becomes a system rather than a mood.

Figure 44.2 states the loop, and the reader should note its structure: every element of the loop is a response to the previous element, and the loop can be interrupted at any point.

### Why Bitterness Is Not a Moral Failure

The section states this explicitly, because the reader who recognises himself in the anatomy will frequently respond with shame, and shame is not the instrument that the material requires.

Bitterness is not a moral failure, for three reasons that the case material supports.

**The first reason is that the injury was real.** The man did not construct the injury in order to have an account. He was injured, and the account is his response to the injury rather than its cause. The reading of bitterness as a character defect is therefore a second injury, and the case material records its effect: men who are told that they are bitter rather than injured frequently withdraw from the parties who might have helped them.

**The second reason is that the ledger is a rational response to an unacknowledged cost.** A man who has been damaged and has received no acknowledgement has no instrument by which to close the account. The ledger is his attempt to keep the claim open in the absence of any institution that will hear it, and it is frequently the only form of justice available to him.

**The third reason is that the man has frequently conducted the alternatives and found them closed.** He reported the injury and was not believed; he sought the institution and was refused; he conducted a legal or professional process and did not obtain the outcome; he attempted a reconciliation and was rebuffed. The bitter account is therefore frequently the last of a series rather than the first, and the case material's finding is that the series should be examined before the account is addressed.

The section's position is nevertheless that bitterness is a position that costs the man more than it costs the parties who caused it, and that its cost is paid in the currency described in Component Four and Five: the reduction of his capacity, his relationships, and his future. The material's task is therefore not to establish that the man is wrong. It is to establish that the account's maintenance is being conducted at his expense, and that the expense is optional.

### The Exit

The exit from bitterness has five steps, and they are stated in the order in which the case material finds them effective.

**Step One: The injury is stated accurately and in full.** The statement is written, it is specific, and it includes the cost. The man states what was done, by whom, when, and what it cost him: the money, the years, the health, the relationship, the standing. The statement is not conducted for publication and it is not conducted for the parties who caused the injury. It is conducted for the man, and the case material's finding is that the written statement frequently produces a specific and unexpected result: the injury's size is either confirmed or reduced, and in both cases the man's attention is released from the task of keeping it in mind.

**Step Two: The claim is separated from the account.** The man distinguishes between what he is owed and what he will do if it is not paid. The distinction is the exit's mechanical centre, and it is stated as a rule: **the man's future is not a hostage to the acknowledgement he has not received.** The rule does not abandon the claim. It places the claim in one account and the man's conduct in another, and it permits the man to conduct his life while the claim remains open.

The case material records two practical forms of the rule. The first is the legal or institutional route: where a claim can be pursued, it is pursued, with a professional, and its progress is conducted as a matter rather than as the man's identity. The second is the abandonment of the claim where it cannot be pursued: the man states in writing that he will receive no acknowledgement, that the absence is a fact about the other party, and that he will not conduct his future as a demand for it. The second form is frequently experienced as a loss and is frequently the step that produces the release.

**Step Three: The ledger is closed by an act the man controls.** The ledger of the owed requires an act that only the other party can conduct, which is the reason it remains open. The exit requires an act that the man can conduct himself, and the case material records several forms: the formal complaint that is lodged and then released; the letter that is written and not sent; the compensation that is sought through an institution and accepted at its outcome; the forgiveness that is conducted privately and without the other party's knowledge; the decision to conduct the injury as information rather than as a claim.

The reader should note the position of forgiveness in this material. It is included as one form among several, it is conducted for the man's benefit rather than the other party's, and it is not required. The finding in the case material is that men who conduct the release in whatever form suits them are substantially better off than men who wait for an acknowledgement that does not come, and that the form is less important than the conduct.

**Step Four: The narrowing is reversed by deliberate breadth.** The man's attention is restored to the fields that the injury had occupied, and the means is deliberate rather than passive. The practices are ordinary: a new competence, undertaken for its own sake; a setting in which the man is useful to parties who do not know his history; a body of work that produces something visible; a relationship that is conducted without the injury's categories; and a reading or study that supplies the man with material outside his own account. The finding is that the reversal is produced by the new material rather than by the decision, and that the decision is required in order to begin.

**Step Five: The confirming arrangement is interrupted by a deliberate exception.** The man's conduct produces the evidence that confirms his account, and the interruption is conducted by a specific exception: one party who is given the benefit of the doubt, one institution that is approached without the previous expectation, one opportunity that is accepted from the quarter the man distrusts, one test that is not administered. The exception is small, it is stated in advance as an experiment, and its result is recorded. The case material's finding is that a single favourable exception does not overturn the account and that a series of three or four, recorded, frequently does.

### The Distinction Between Bitterness and Grief

The section closes with the distinction, because the two are frequently confused by the man himself and by the parties around him.

Grief is the response to a loss. It is conducted over time, it is frequently severe, it comes in waves, and it does not narrow the man's capacity to perceive his present position. A grieving man may be uninterested in his future for a period and he does not generally construct a ledger of what he is owed.

Bitterness is the response to an injury that has not been acknowledged. It is conducted over years, it is steady rather than wave-like, and it narrows the man's perception to the injury's categories.

The distinction matters because the treatments are different. Grief requires time, acknowledgement, the conduct of the loss, and the material of Chapter 30: it is not resolved by an act of will, and a man who attempts to conduct it as a decision will fail. Bitterness requires the five steps above, and a man who waits for it to resolve with time will generally find that it has not.

The case material also records that the two frequently coexist. A man may be grieving a loss and conducting a bitter account of the injury that caused it: the death of a household member in circumstances that involved another party's negligence; the end of a marriage that involved a betrayal; the loss of a position that involved an injustice. The material's instruction in these cases is that the grief is conducted first, with the material of Part VIII, and the bitterness is conducted afterwards, because the bitter account is frequently performing a function that the grief has not yet been permitted to perform.

## 44.7 Illustrative Case: The Man Who Kept the Account

The case is composite and illustrative. It is constructed from the case material of this book and is presented as an example rather than as a report of a particular person.

### The Injury

Emmanuel Otu was fifty-one years old and had spent twenty-two years in the accounts department of a state-owned transport corporation. In the year of the injury, a new chief executive was appointed, and within eight months the corporation's procurement records for a three-year period were referred to an anti-corruption body. Emmanuel had signed the payment vouchers for the period, as had his immediate superior, a man named Okon, who was two grades above him and who had approved the contracts that the inquiry concerned.

The inquiry's finding, issued fourteen months later, was that the contracts had been awarded to a company connected to the chief executive's brother-in-law and that the payments had been made on documentation that did not support them. Emmanuel was not found to have benefited. He was found to have signed the vouchers without adequate verification, and he was dismissed. Okon was dismissed and prosecuted; the chief executive resigned; the brother-in-law was not charged.

Emmanuel was fifty-two at the time of the dismissal. He had eleven years of pension contributions, a wife named Grace who taught at a primary school, four children of whom two were in university, and a house that was three years from being paid for.

### The First Year

Emmanuel conducted the first year in the pattern that Chapter 38 describes, with one difference that the case material treats as significant. He did not stabilise. He conducted a legal challenge to the dismissal, which he pursued for two years and lost; he conducted a series of approaches to former colleagues, of whom two responded and neither offered work; he conducted a period of five months in which he left the house each morning and returned in the evening without employment, which his wife did not learn about until the sixth month.

His account of the dismissal, given to his family and to his former colleagues, was specific and accurate: he had signed the vouchers, the verification had been the responsibility of a department that had not conducted it, he had raised the matter twice in writing, and his letters had been ignored. The account was true. The case material records that it was corroborated by two documents that Emmanuel produced at the inquiry.

The case's finding at the end of the first year is that Emmanuel had sustained a real injury and had conducted it with a degree of accuracy that most men do not achieve. His difficulty was not the account's truth. It was what he did with it over the following nine years.

### The Nine Years

Emmanuel did not return to employment at his former level. He conducted a series of small engagements: bookkeeping for two schools, a period as the treasurer of a cooperative, a part-time arrangement with an audit firm that lasted fourteen months. The income was approximately a third of his former salary, and the household conducted a substantial reduction in its arrangements, which Grace managed with a competence that the case material records as one of the factors that made the household's survival possible.

The bitterness was not immediate. In the third year, Emmanuel's account was still specific and still accompanied by an activity: he was pursuing the legal challenge, he was writing to the professional body, he was assisting two former colleagues who had been dismissed in the same inquiry. The case material records the third year as the period in which the conversion was still available.

The change occurred in the fifth year, and it had three identifiable components, which the case material treats as the case's central material.

**The first component was the exhaustion of the routes.** The legal challenge was dismissed at the end of the fourth year, and the professional body declined to review the dismissal at the beginning of the fifth. Emmanuel's response, recorded in his own account, was the sentence *there is nobody left to tell.* The sentence is the material of Component Two of the anatomy, and its effect was the beginning of the ledger's closure — not through acknowledgement, which did not arrive, but through the absence of any further party to whom the claim could be addressed.

**The second component was the narrowing.** In the fifth and sixth years, Emmanuel's attention contracted to the dismissal. He read the inquiry's report repeatedly. He conducted a correspondence with two former colleagues that consisted entirely of the case's material. He declined two engagements, one because the client had been connected to the corporation and one for reasons he could not state. He began to assess new acquaintances by their resemblance to the chief executive, and he stated to Grace, in the sixth year, that he had concluded that *people in positions always protect each other*.

**The third component was the confirming arrangement.** By the eighth year, Emmanuel's conduct was producing the evidence that confirmed his account. He had withdrawn from the cooperative, whose officers he had begun to suspect of the irregularities he had been dismissed for failing to detect. He had declined a position as the treasurer of a community association, on the grounds that the association's accounts would not bear examination, which was true and was not the reason he declined. He had ceased to attend the occasions at which he would meet his former colleagues, and his account of the absence was that they had abandoned him. The case material records that two of them had attempted to maintain the relationship and had been rebuffed.

### The Measure

The case's finding on the nine years is stated in the household's own record, which the case material obtained from Grace's account.

**The financial measure.** The household's income in the ninth year was approximately forty per cent of its income in the year before the dismissal, and its expenditure had been reduced accordingly. The house had been retained. Two of the four children had completed university. The third had deferred for two years. The measure is not the case's principal finding, and it establishes that the household survived materially.

**The professional measure.** Emmanuel had not practiced at his former level and had not obtained a comparable position. Two engagements in nine years had been conducted at a level below his competence, and both had ended in the man's withdrawal rather than in dismissal.

**The health measure.** Emmanuel had been diagnosed with hypertension in the seventh year and had not sought treatment until the ninth, when he was admitted for four days following an episode that the case material records without clinical detail. The measure is the one that the case material treats as the bitterness's most concrete cost.

**The relational measure.** Emmanuel's relationship with Grace had survived and had narrowed. Her account, recorded in the ninth year, was: *he does not talk about anything else. He is not cruel. He is not present either.*

**The measure that the case material treats as the principal one.** Emmanuel's own account, recorded in the ninth year, was that he had been *waiting for eleven years for somebody to say that they had been wrong about me.* The sentence is the ledger of the owed in its clearest form, and the case material's finding is that it is the sentence that the exit's second step addresses.

### The Interruption

The interruption was not a decision. It was a letter.

In the tenth year, the anti-corruption body published a review of its earlier cases, and the review contained a paragraph that stated that a number of officers in the corporation had been dismissed for failures of verification that had been the responsibility of a department whose own procedures had subsequently been found deficient. The paragraph did not name Emmanuel. It did not constitute an acknowledgement, and it did not alter his position.

Emmanuel read the paragraph and, in the case material's account, conducted an act that he had not conducted in eleven years. He wrote to the review's author, stated his case in two pages, and asked for a correction to the record that named him. The correction was issued four months later. It stated that his dismissal had been reviewed and that the finding against him had been set aside.

The finding did not restore his position, his pension, or his eleven years. The case material records that Emmanuel's response to the correction was not elation. It was a period of two weeks in which he slept, and then a sentence to Grace that the material treats as the case's turning point: *I have been keeping an account with a bank that was never open.*

### The Exit

Emmanuel conducted four of the five steps over the following two years, and the case material records them in the order in which they occurred.

**He conducted the statement.** He wrote the account of the injury in full, in eleven pages, and he did not show it to anybody for eight months. When he eventually showed it to Grace, her account was that it was *shorter than what he had been saying for years, and more accurate.*

**He separated the claim from his future.** The correction had been issued; the pension was not restored, and he pursued it for a period through the pension authority without success. In the second year, he stated in writing that he would not receive the pension, that the absence was a fact about the authority, and that he would not conduct his remaining years as a demand for it. The statement is the material of the exit's second step, and the case material records it as the point at which the man's attention began to move.

**He interrupted the narrowing.** He accepted a position as the treasurer of the community association that he had declined three years earlier. He conducted it for four years, and the case material records that the association's accounts were, in fact, in order.

**He interrupted the confirming arrangement.** He contacted the two former colleagues who had attempted to maintain the relationship and stated, in specific terms, that he had withdrawn and that the withdrawal had been his. One of the two responded within a week. The other did not respond, and the case material records that Emmanuel's response to the silence was, in his own words, *one account closed and one not, and I am not going to wait for the second.*

**He did not conduct the fifth step in full.** The case material records that Emmanuel continued to distrust institutions and that he stated the distrust openly and specifically rather than conducting it as a general account of the world. The distinction is the one the case material treats as the case's most useful finding: **the exit does not produce a man who trusts. It produces a man whose distrust is specific, stated, and does not govern his conduct toward parties who have not earned it.**

### The Finding

The case's finding is stated in three parts.

**The first part is that the injury was real and the bitterness was not a moral failure.** Emmanuel's account was accurate, his legal challenge was reasonable, and his dismissal was, on the review's own finding, unjustified. The case material does not treat his nine years as a defect of character, and the reader who recognises his own position in the case should note the point.

**The second part is that the cost of the bitterness was paid by the man and by his household, and not by the parties who caused the injury.** The chief executive had resigned and had continued his career elsewhere. The brother-in-law had not been charged. The corporation had been restructured. The nine years were conducted in the man's own house, at his own expense, and the expense was measured in income, health, and presence.

**The third part is that the exit was available at every point and was conducted by small acts.** The correction was not the exit; it was the occasion. The exit was conducted by a letter, a written statement, a decision about a pension, an accepted position, two telephone calls, and a sentence to his wife. The case material's finding is that this is the ordinary form of the work, and that the extraordinary form — the reconciliation, the public acknowledgement, the restored position — is neither available nor necessary.
