## 38.6 Rebuilding With the Household

The household is the party through which the reconstruction is conducted, and this section supplies the arrangements.

### The Household's Position

The findings from the practice material, and the reader should apply them to his own household.

**The household generally knows that something has occurred.** The reduced expenditure, the man's state, the telephone calls, the visitors, the changes in the household's arrangements, and the man's conduct are all observed. The household's account of what has occurred is constructed from these observations, and it is frequently inaccurate and frequently worse than the position.

**The household has its own losses.** The financial security, the plans, the house, the standing, the schooling, the occasions, the relationships with the extended family, and the future the household was arranging. The losses are the household's own and they require their own arrangements; the man who addresses the financial position and does not recognise the household's material will find that the reconstruction does not hold.

**The household's members have their own conduct in the period.** The spouse who manages, the children who select their disclosures, the household's own arrangements and their costs, and, in the case material, the household member who has been carrying the position for longer than the man has known.

**The household's trust is a separate matter from the household's finances.** The material of Chapter 20.5: the discovery of the concealment produces the loss of the household's trust in the man's account of the household's affairs, and its restoration is not produced by the financial position's improvement. The restoration is conducted by the arrangements of this section and by the man's conduct over a period of years.

### The Four Arrangements

**Arrangement One: The disclosure.** The four-part form of Chapter 34.6, conducted with the household, with the statement of Section 38.4 present: the position in figures and facts; the man's own part in it, without defence; the consequences that the household has already observed and attributed elsewhere; and what happens next, with the stages and the dates.

**The instruction.** The disclosure should be conducted before the reconstruction's decisions are taken, since the household's arrangements depend on the position. The practice material's finding is consistent: the household's response is oriented to the plan, and the disclosure without the plan produces the household's anxiety, while the disclosure with the plan generally produces the household's participation.

**Arrangement Two: The household's own arrangements.** The household's requirements, at the level the position now permits; the changes, stated specifically; the members' own losses acknowledged; and the arrangements the household requires, established with the household's participation rather than supplied by the man. The material of Chapters 18.6 and 20.6 applies, at the level the position allows.

**Arrangement Three: The household's role in the reconstruction.** The specific contributions the household can make: the spouse's own income or capacity where it exists, the children's arrangements, the reductions that the household decides rather than those the man imposes, the household's own network, and the household's participation in the negotiations and the institutions' processes where that is appropriate. The practice material records the finding plainly: the men who conducted the reconstruction with the household's participation rebuilt faster, and the households reported the position as more manageable, than the men who conducted it as a private project.

**Arrangement Four: The household's own people.** The spouse's and the children's own arrangements: their confidants, their professionals where required, their groups, and their rest. The material of Chapters 27 and 37 applies to the household members as it does to the man, and the specific finding is that the household's members are frequently carrying more than the man knows, and that their arrangements are the reconstruction's support rather than its addition.

### The Two Errors

**The error of the protective concealment.** The man withholds the position from the household in order to protect it, on the basis that he will disclose it when the position has improved. The cost is the material of Chapters 34 and 35 in this setting: the household's arrangements are constructed on an incorrect account, its losses accumulate without explanation, and the disclosure's cost increases with the delay.

**The error of the reconstruction conducted as a private project.** The man informs the household of the position and conducts the reconstruction alone, treating the household as the party to be protected from the process. The cost is the household's exclusion from the arrangements that concern it and the household's own losses unaddressed, which is the arrangement most frequently reported in the case material of the men whose reconstructions did not hold.

::: casebox
**Composite case.** Constructed for teaching purposes from patterns reported in insolvency practice, family practice, and counselling.

**Yakubu is fifty-four, a mechanical engineer, married to Zainab for twenty-seven years, with three children aged twenty-four, twenty, and sixteen.**

Yakubu's position in 2020 was the result of eleven years of construction: a fabrication and engineering services company with about forty staff, a workshop on a site he had developed, a house in a suburb on which a mortgage remained, and a position in the state's engineering community that he described as *a name that people called on*.

The collapse occurred over eighteen months. The principal client, a state agency, suspended three contracts in 2019 with about eleven months of certified work unpaid. Yakubu met the company's obligations — the wages, the suppliers, the site's rent — from the company's facilities and from the household's reserves and, in the second year, from a facility secured against the house. In 2020 the agency's payments stopped altogether; the company's facilities were called; the house was under threat; the workshop was lost; the staff were reduced from forty to six; and, in the same year, Zainab's brother, whose guarantee of one facility had been given on Yakubu's request, was affected, which produced the family's involvement.

Yakubu's first period, which he described in the case in the terms of this chapter, contained five of the seven errors.

**The concealment.** Yakubu did not inform Zainab of the position for about ten months. His account: *She knew the contracts were suspended. She did not know that I had used the house and that her brother had signed. I believed I would get the money in the following quarter and that nobody would ever need to know.*

**The panic borrowing.** A facility at a substantially higher rate in the fourth month, taken from an informal lender, described by Yakubu as *the worst decision of my life and I knew it when I signed it, and I signed it because I could not face the wages on Friday.*

**The isolated major decision.** Yakubu accepted a payment arrangement with the agency that included a substantial reduction in the certified sums, negotiated alone over four months, and which he later identified as the error that reduced the creditors' recovery and his own position.

**The drinking.** His alcohol use increased from occasional to a nightly pattern of four to five units, described by him as *the only hour of the day in which I was not in that building.*

**The attempt to restore the position immediately.** In the eleventh month, Yakubu bid for and obtained a contract with a different agency, funded by a further facility, and the contract's margin was consumed by the facility's cost.

The two matters that produced the change were, first, the other lender's demand and the seizure of the workshop, which occurred in the twelfth month, and, second, Zainab's discovery of the position, which occurred when the bank's letter arrived at the house and she opened it. Her account, recorded in the family consultation: *I opened the letter and it was for the house. I read it three times. I was not shocked about the money. I was shocked that he had been carrying it alone, and that my brother's name was in it, and that I had been planning the school fees in my head for ten months.*

The reconstruction, conducted over about four years, had six stages, and Yakubu identified the stage order as the reason it worked.

**Stage one: the stabilisation.** Conducted with an insolvency practitioner engaged in the second month after the discovery, on the recommendation of a friend in the association, and with the household's disclosure conducted in the same month, in the four-part form, with the practitioner's statement in hand. The negotiations with the facilities, the other lender, and the suppliers were conducted by the practitioner. The position established at the end of the stage: the total in figures, the categories of the obligations, the protection of the house through a rearrangement with the mortgage holder, the arrangement with the other lender, and the removal of the workshop from the position.

**Stage two: the income.** Yakubu obtained employment as a maintenance engineer at about half his former income in the fourth month, and Zainab, who had trained as a teacher before the marriage, resumed teaching on a part-time arrangement in the sixth month. Yakubu described the stage: *I had been the proprietor of a company with forty staff and I went to work for another man as a supervisor at fifty-one. For the first three months I could not say the word. And the money came in on the twenty-eighth of every month and the household's position changed in the first month of it.*

**Stage three: the settlement.** The formal arrangement with the creditors was concluded in the second year, with a schedule of payments over four years. Yakubu's account of the stage: *I had avoided the practitioner for eleven months because I believed that the arrangement was the end of me. The arrangement is the thing that has kept the house and the name. I have paid on the twenty-fifth of every month for thirty-one months and I have not missed one, and I have a letter from the practitioner after each year.*

**Stage four: the standing.** Conducted over the following three years: the association's meetings resumed and attended, the professional registration maintained, the community's contributions made at the reduced level, the explanations given once where they were required, and no argument conducted with the audience. Yakubu described the period: *There were about nine months in which I did not go to the association, because I did not want to answer. Then a man I had not seen in two years called me for advice on a pump and I went to his site and I gave him the advice, and I have gone every month since. Nobody has asked me about the company. Two men have asked me about the practitioner.*

**Stage five: the relationships and the household.** The involved elements were the brother-in-law's guarantee, which Yakubu repaid in instalments over three years with the family's arrangement recorded in writing at the disclosure, and the household's own losses, which were addressed at a household meeting in the sixth month at which the family's circumstances were stated: the withdrawal of the youngest child from the private school at the end of that year, the eldest child's contribution, and the house's position. Yakubu described the meeting: *My eldest son said that he had known for a year and that he had been paying the household's electricity from his own salary without telling me. My wife said that she had been teaching for four months and had not told me because she did not want me to know that she had needed to. We had three people carrying the same thing in one house, each one hiding it to protect the others, and the meeting was the first hour in about two years in which everybody said what was actually happening.*

**Stage six: the new construction.** In the fourth year, Yakubu established two arrangements: a monthly supervision arrangement with a practitioner, from the alcohol and the position, and a small partnership with two former employees for the workshop's services on a reduced scale, conducted with written terms after the association's advice. His account: *The company is not coming back and I do not want it back in that form. I have three pages of terms with the two men and the terms were my own condition, after eleven years of doing everything on a handshake and a name. And what I have that I did not have in 2019 is a wife who knows the accounts, a son who tells me what he is paying, a group of men who have each been where I went, and a practitioner who has the whole of it. I would not have traded the company for those four things in 2019 and I would not trade them back for it now.*

Five features of this case deserve isolation.

**Five of the seven errors were present, and the man's own account identified each precisely.** The concealment, the panic borrowing, the isolated decision, the drinking, and the attempt to restore the position immediately. The case is included because the errors are registered by the man himself in retrospect, which is the reason Section 38.3 is written for the reader in the first period rather than for his judgment afterwards.

**The collapse removed the instruments through which it would have been addressed.** The company, the workshop, the house's security, the agency's relationship, the reserves, and — through the brother-in-law's guarantee — the family's standing. This is the definition of Section 38.1 in practice, and the case is included because the sequence of losses is the reason a partial method does not address a total collapse.

**The household was carrying the position and concealing its own conduct.** The wife's teaching, the son's electricity payments, and the man's concealment. The case records the household meeting's finding in the man's own words: three people carrying the same thing in one house, each hiding it to protect the others. This is the material of Chapter 34.6's mechanism in its strongest form, and the case is included because the arrangement's function is to end exactly this arrangement.

**The stages were conducted in order, and the man identified the reason.** The practitioner and the statement first, then the income — including the employment at half the former income — then the settlement schedule, then the standing, then the relationships, then the new construction. The case is included because the order is the chapter's principal instruction, and because the man's account supplies its mechanism: the settlement is what kept the house and the name, and the income flow was the condition of the settlement.

**The reconstruction was not the restoration of the former position.** The company was not reconstituted; the position at fifty-four is smaller, differently arranged, and, in the man's own accounting, preferable: the terms in writing, the household that knows the accounts, the group, and the practitioner. This is the material of Section 38.5's sixth stage, and it is the finding that most frequently distinguishes the men whose reconstructions hold from the men whose collapses continue.
