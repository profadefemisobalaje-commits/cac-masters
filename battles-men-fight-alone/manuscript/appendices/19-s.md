# Questions for Men's Discussion Groups and Book Clubs {.appendix letter="S"}

This appendix supplies the material for a group of men meeting to examine the book, and for the facilitators of such groups. It is written for the ordinary group — the association, the workplace, the community body, the reading circle, the group of friends — and not for a clinical setting.

## The Purpose of a Men's Group

Four findings from the case material govern the appendix.

**The first is that a group is one of the most effective conditions for men's statement.** The finding is that men who will not disclose a position to a professional will frequently disclose it in a group of peers, and that the disclosure is frequently the first of their adult lives.

**The second is that a badly conducted group is worse than none.** A group in which a man's disclosure is met with advice, diagnosis, ridicule, or a report outside the room teaches the man that his position is not safe, and the lesson is frequently durable. The group's conduct matters more than its content.

**The third is that the group needs a structure.** A group without a structure becomes an occasion for the exchange of opinions, which is frequently enjoyable and rarely useful. The structures below supply the alternative.

**The fourth is that the group needs a leader who is not a therapist.** The leader's function is the structure, the safety, and the referral, not the treatment.

## The Ten Sessions

The programme consists of ten sessions of ninety minutes, conducted at intervals of one or two weeks. Each session covers one part of the book, and each has the same five-part structure.

| Session | The reading | The theme |
|:---:|:----------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Part I, Chapters 1–4 | The silent world: what we carry, and what we were taught about carrying it |
| 2 | Part II, Chapters 5–8 | The mind: the states we do not name, and the fears that govern our decisions |
| 3 | Part III, Chapters 9–12 | Money: the position, the figures, and the concealment |
| 4 | Part IV, Chapters 13–16 | Love and relationships: the injuries, the silences, and the withdrawals |
| 5 | Part V, Chapters 17–20 | Family and fatherhood: what we transmit, and what we were transmitted |
| 6 | Part VI, Chapters 21–24 | Identity: the mirror, the weaknesses, the addictions, the loss of self |
| 7 | Part VII, Chapters 25–27 | Society: the judgement, the betrayals, and the parties we do not have |
| 8 | Part VIII, Chapters 28–30 | Loss, regret, and grief: what died quietly, and what we have not conducted |
| 9 | Parts IX and X, Chapters 31–37 | Leadership, carrying others, and the turn toward help |
| 10 | Parts XI and XII, Chapters 38–45 | Rebuilding, and the vision of manhood beyond the battle |

## The Session Structure

Each session has five parts and the times are stated.

**The opening (5 minutes).** The leader states the session's theme, the group's rules, and the time at which the session will end.

**The reading's material (15 minutes).** One member summarises the reading in five minutes, and the group identifies the three passages that were most significant. The summary is assigned in advance.

**The round (40 minutes).** Each member answers the two questions of the session. The round is conducted in order, each member speaks for a stated period, and no member is interrupted or answered. The leader's task is the timekeeping and the protection of the round.

**The examination (20 minutes).** The group examines one member's position in the form of the case material: what is the position, what is the arrangement, what is the instrument, and what is the first act. The member is chosen in advance and volunteers, and the group's task is the questions rather than the answers.

**The close (10 minutes).** Each member states one act he will conduct before the next session, with a date. The leader records them and opens the next session with them.

## The Session Questions

### Session 1: The Silent World

1. Who knows your accurate position, and how much of it do they know?
2. What did your household teach you about what a man does with what he carries, and how was it taught?

### Session 2: The Mind

1. Which state do you most frequently experience and least frequently name?
2. What decision have you not taken because of a fear, and what would you take if the fear were examined?

### Session 3: Money

1. Can you state your position in figures, and to whom?
2. What are you paying for that produces nothing except the account that others hold of you?

### Session 4: Love and Relationships

1. What have you not stated to the party closest to you, and what has the silence cost?
2. What withdrawal have you conducted, and since when?

### Session 5: Family and Fatherhood

1. What are you transmitting to the boys and girls who observe you, and how do you know?
2. What did your father transmit that you have kept, and what have you rejected?

### Session 6: Identity

1. Which of your weaknesses is concealed from everybody, and what does the concealment cost?
2. Who are you when your position is removed?

### Session 7: Society

1. Which party are you avoiding, and what is the question you expect from them?
2. Which friendship has lapsed, and what would restore it?

### Session 8: Loss and Grief

1. What have you lost that you have not conducted, and who knows about it?
2. Which regret is still governing a decision you take today?

### Session 9: Leadership and the Turn Toward Help

1. What are you carrying that is not yours to carry?
2. What have you refused that was available, and why?

### Session 10: Rebuilding and the Vision

1. What is your position for, and what will be different because you conducted it?
2. What is the one act you will conduct in the following thirty days, and what date is on it?

## The Group's Rules

The leader states the rules at the first session and at the beginning of every session. Five rules are required.

1. **What is said in the room stays in the room.** The material is not reported, and the leader states the one limit: where a member's safety or a child's safety is at risk, the leader will act.
2. **Nobody is advised unless he asks.** The group's task is the questions. The member who requires advice will ask for it, and the finding is that the advice supplied without the request is the most frequent cause of a man's withdrawal.
3. **Nobody is diagnosed.** The group is not a clinical setting, and a member's statement is not a condition.
4. **Each member speaks for himself.** The group does not discuss an absent member, and it does not discuss a member's wife, employer, or family in terms that the member would not use in their presence.
5. **The group ends on time.** The finding is that the ending is the condition of the members' attendance, since a man who cannot predict the session's length will not attend it.

## The Leader's Role

The leader's role has five functions.

**The first is the structure.** The leader holds the time, the order, and the five parts. The finding is that the structure is the safety, since a group without a structure drifts into the exchange of opinions in which the quietest member does not speak.

**The second is the protection of the round.** The leader stops the member who answers another's statement, the member who advises without a request, and the member who makes the group his audience. The interruption is conducted politely and consistently, and it is the leader's principal instrument.

**The third is the referral.** The leader identifies the member whose material exceeds the group's competence and directs him to the professional route of Appendix Q. The director should be conducted privately, with the sentence of Chapter 43.6: *I do not know how to help with this part, and I know who does, and I will go with you.*

**The fourth is the leader's own support.** The leader conducts the group and receives the members' material, and the finding of Chapter 44.5's fourth condition applies: the leader requires a supervisor, a peer, or a professional of his own.

**The fifth is the succession.** The leader establishes a second leader in the first three months, so that the group survives his absence. The finding is that groups conducted by a single leader frequently end when the leader moves, and that the groups which continue have two leaders and a written structure.

## The Difficulties

Five difficulties are recorded with their answers.

**The first is that the members do not speak.** The answer is the round's structure and the leader's first disclosure, which is proportionate and brief. The finding is that a group's depth is established in the first session by the leader's conduct rather than by his instruction.

**The second is that one member dominates.** The answer is the time limit, stated in advance and enforced without exception. The finding is that the dominating member is frequently the member who most requires the group, and that the enforcement is a service to him.

**The third is that a member discloses material that exceeds the group's competence.** The answer is the referral and the leader's limit, stated in the session: *this is beyond what this group can hold, and here is the route.*

**The fourth is that the group becomes a social occasion.** The answer is the structure and the close, which requires each member to state an act with a date. The finding is that the act is the difference between a group that produces change and a group that produces company.

**The fifth is that attendance declines.** The answer is the group's size and the interval. The finding is that groups of six to ten members meeting fortnightly are sustained, and that groups of twenty meeting monthly are not.

## A Note for the Member

The appendix closes with a note for the man who is considering joining a group.

The finding is that the men who benefit most from a group are not the men who are most comfortable in it. They are the men who would not ordinarily speak, and who speak once, and who discover that the room did not reduce its estimate of them.

The member's task is not to disclose everything. It is to state one true sentence in the first session, which is the act that Chapter 45 describes in its group form. The sentence is frequently the beginning of the arrangement that the book has described, and the group is frequently the setting in which a man first discovers that his position can be held by other people without his standing being reduced.
