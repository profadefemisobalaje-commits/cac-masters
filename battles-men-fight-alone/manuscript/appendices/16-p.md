# A 90-Day Life Rebuilding Action Plan {.appendix letter="P"}

This appendix is the operative instrument of Part XI and Chapter 38. It is designed for the man whose position has collapsed or substantially altered — the loss of a business, an employment, a marriage, a household, a position, or a health — and who requires a structure for the first ninety days and the year that follows.

## The Principles

Five principles govern the plan.

**The first is that stabilisation precedes reconstruction.** The finding of Chapter 38.3's seven errors is that men who attempt immediately to restore the position produce a further loss. The first thirty days are conducted for stability and not for recovery.

**The second is that the plan has three phases of thirty days, and the phases are not interchangeable.** Each phase is conducted before the next, and a man who is in the second phase on day ten will produce the collapse that the phase order is designed to prevent.

**The third is that the plan is conducted with a party.** The party of Chapter 42.5's second support. The finding is that the plan conducted alone is conducted for a period and abandoned, and that the party's function is to hold the plan rather than to supervise the man.

**The fourth is that the plan is written.** The plan is recorded in figures and dates, and its record is reviewed weekly.

**The fifth is that the plan is small.** Each week has one task. The finding is that men who attempt five tasks in a week conduct none, and that one conducted task per week produces twelve conducted alterations in ninety days.

## Phase One: Days 1–30 — Stabilisation

### The Week-by-Week Plan

| Week | The task | The measure |
|:---:|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | The position statement: income, obligations, essential expenditure, reserve, immediate risks (housing, food, school fees, health). | The four tables of Appendix D, Part Four, completed. |
| 2 | The immediate obligations: the rent or the mortgage, the food, the school, the health. State the position to the parties concerned and request the arrangement. | The three arrangements requested, with dates. |
| 3 | The one party: state the position in full to one party, and name the professional route for the domain that requires one. | The statement conducted; the professional engaged. |
| 4 | The household: state the position to the household, with the figures, and conduct the household's participation in the first reductions. | The household conversation conducted; the first reductions dated. |

### The Rules of Phase One

1. **No new obligation.** No loan, no guarantee, no contract, no commitment, no purchase on credit, no agreement to a request that carries a cost, for thirty days.
2. **No decision of consequence without the party's examination.** The decisions of consequence are listed in the week's record and examined before they are taken.
3. **The essential expenditure is protected.** The housing, the food, the health, and the children's schooling are conducted before any obligation to a creditor.
4. **The sleep is protected.** The finding is that the sleep is the first item that the collapse takes and the last that the man restores, and that its restoration is a condition of the plan's conduct.
5. **The record is daily.** Five lines: the position, the task, what was conducted, what was not, and what will be conducted tomorrow.

## Phase Two: Days 31–60 — Reconstruction

### The Week-by-Week Plan

| Week | The task | The measure |
|:---:|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------|
| 5 | The income: the statement of what the man can earn, in what form, and from which parties. The first approach conducted. | The income statement; the first approach made. |
| 6 | The professional route: the accountant, the lawyer, the debt adviser, the clinician, according to the domain of the collapse. | The engagement of two professionals, with appointments. |
| 7 | The reductions in three layers, from the items nobody sees to the items some see. | The first two layers conducted, with dates. |
| 8 | The third layer, conducted with the household's participation, and the renegotiation of the obligations that are capable of renegotiation. | The third layer conducted; three obligations renegotiated. |

### The Rules of Phase Two

1. **The income precedes the reconstruction.** A plan conducted without an income is a plan conducted on a hope, and the finding is that the income statement is the item that men delay most and require most.
2. **The obligation is negotiated before it is missed.** The creditor, the school, the landlord, and the lender are approached before the payment is missed, since the arrangement's availability reduces after the default.
3. **The reductions are conducted in layers.** The house and the school are conducted last, with the professional and the household.
4. **The professional's advice is recorded.** Where it is not followed, the reason is recorded.
5. **The plan is examined monthly by the party.** The party's function is to detect the drift that the man cannot see.

## Phase Three: Days 61–90 — Rebuilding

### The Week-by-Week Plan

| Week | The task | The measure |
|:---:|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------|
| 9 | The work: the arrangement that will produce the income in the following six months. The written plan, with dates. | The plan written; the first two engagements secured. |
| 10 | The standing: the accurate statement to the two or three parties whose estimate matters, and the repair of the relationships that the collapse has affected. | The statements made; the repairs begun. |
| 11 | The health: the medical appointment, the treatment, the sleep, the drinking, the exercise. | The appointment attended; the treatment begun. |
| 12 | The ninety-day review: the position restated, the plan's conduct recorded, and the following ninety days written. | The review conducted with the party; the next plan dated. |

### The Rules of Phase Three

1. **The rebuilding is conducted at the true level.** The finding is that men rebuild at the level of the previous position and produce a second collapse, and that the true level is the level the present income supports.
2. **The standing is rebuilt by conduct and not by account.** The finding of Chapter 38.5's fourth stage is that the standing is restored by the accurate statement and the conduct over a period of years, and not by the account.
3. **The health is not deferred.** The finding is that the health is the item most frequently postponed in the rebuilding and the item whose neglect produces the largest subsequent cost.
4. **The party's participation is retained.** The party of Phase One continues through Phase Three, and the finding is that the phase at which the party is released is the phase at which the man's position becomes uncorrected again.

## The Review

At the end of the ninety days, answer the following questions in writing, with the party present.

1. What is the position now, in figures, compared with day one?
2. Which of the twelve tasks were conducted, and which were not?
3. What did the unconducted tasks have in common? (The finding is that they cluster around a domain that the man is avoiding.)
4. What is the income, and what is the essential expenditure, and what is the margin?
5. What is the reserve, and how many months does it cover?
6. Which professional is engaged, and what is the next appointment?
7. What has the collapse taught, stated as an instrument rather than as an attitude?
8. What is the plan for the following ninety days, with the dates?

## The Cautions

The first caution is that the plan does not address a depression, a grief, or an addiction. Where those are present, the material of Appendices B, K, and M applies, and the plan is conducted alongside the professional route.

The second is that the plan's conduct is frequently interrupted by a further event. The finding is that the interruption does not invalidate the plan, and that the man's task is to return to the phase he was in rather than to begin again.

The third is that the plan does not restore the previous position, and the finding is that men who require the restoration frequently conduct the plan as a means to it and abandon the plan when the restoration does not arrive. The plan's object is a sound position at the true level, and the finding of the case material is that the true level is frequently a better position than the one that was lost, conducted for different purposes and by different arrangements.
