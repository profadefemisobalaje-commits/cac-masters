# A 30-Day Emotional Recovery and Self-Care Plan {.appendix letter="C"}

This appendix supplies a thirty-day plan for a man who has recognised the position described in Parts II, X, and XI and requires a structured beginning. It is not a treatment programme and it does not replace professional care. It is an arrangement of ordinary acts, conducted daily, that establishes the conditions in which recovery can proceed.

## Before Beginning

Three conditions should be met before the plan begins.

**The first is the professional route.** Where the reader's position includes a depression, an anxiety condition, an addiction, a significant physical symptom, or a recent loss, the plan is conducted alongside professional care and not in place of it. Appendix B supplies the recognition material and Appendix Q supplies the routes.

**The second is the statement to one party.** The plan is conducted with at least one party who knows that it is being conducted. The party's role is not to supervise the man and it is to know that the thirty days exist, so that the man is not conducting the arrangement alone, which would reproduce the book's subject in the form of a recovery plan.

**The third is the removal of one demand.** The plan requires approximately forty-five minutes a day and a small amount of sleep. A man who adds it to an unchanged position will abandon it by the ninth day. The reader should identify one demand that will be reduced, delegated, or deferred for the thirty days, and should record it.

## The Daily Core

The daily core is conducted every day of the thirty days. It takes approximately forty minutes and it is not optional.

| Element | Time | Conduct | Purpose |
|:----------------------------|:--------|:--------------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------|
| The waking record | 5 min | Three sentences: how I slept; what I notice in my body; what state I am in. | Establishes the day's position before it is conducted. |
| The body | 20 min | Walking, or the physical activity appropriate to your health. Outdoors where possible. | Regulation, sleep, and mood. |
| The statement | 5 min | One sentence to one party, or the record of the sentence if no party is available that day. | Maintains the arrangement of statement. |
| The regulation practice | 5 min | The practice of Appendix L, conducted twice. | Trains the capacity to reduce a state. |
| The evening record | 5 min | Three sentences: what the day cost; what it produced; what I will do tomorrow. | Closes the day and prevents its carry-over. |

## The Weekly Structure

Each week has a theme, one substantial exercise, and one review.

### Week One: Stabilisation

The first week's task is the establishment of the conditions. No substantial change to the man's position is attempted.

**The exercise.** Complete Sections 1, 2, and 5 of Appendix A, and record the totals. Identify the two items that will be addressed in the thirty days.

**The tasks.** Conduct a medical appointment if one is due or if a symptom is present. Record the sleep in hours each night. Remove the alcohol from the household for the thirty days, or state the quantity and record it. Identify the one party of the plan and state the plan to them.

**The review.** At the end of the week, answer three questions: which of the daily elements was conducted on every day; which was missed, and what interrupted it; what is the smallest alteration that would make the missed element conductable.

### Week Two: The Statement

The second week's task is the extension of the statement beyond the daily sentence.

**The exercise.** Conduct the position table of Appendix A, Section 9, and write the one-sentence statement of the principal difficulty. Then state it to the party of the plan, in the form of Chapter 43.6's reception: what happened, what it cost, what is required.

**The tasks.** Identify the professional who holds the domain of the principal difficulty, and make the appointment. Where the difficulty is in the marriage, conduct the first of the communication exercises of Appendix E. Where the difficulty is financial, complete Part One of Appendix D.

**The review.** Answer: what did the statement produce; what did the party do; what did I do with the party's response; and what is the next statement.

### Week Three: The Arrangement

The third week's task is the alteration of one arrangement, which is the material of Chapter 38.4's stabilisation and Chapter 44.2's fifth question.

**The exercise.** Identify one structural item that is producing cost and that is capable of alteration within the month: the working hours, the concealed obligation, the unrepaired relationship, the unexamined commitment, the drinking occasion, the sleep arrangement.

**The tasks.** Conduct the alteration, and state it to the parties affected. Record the alteration's date and its measure.

**The review.** Answer: was the alteration conducted; what did it cost; what has it produced; and what is the next structural item.

### Week Four: The Transmission and the Plan

The fourth week's task is the conversion of the thirty days into an arrangement that continues.

**The exercise.** Complete the wisdom account of Appendix R's first instrument, or Section 44.8's Instrument Three in its short form: the event, the cost, the instrument learned, and the party who will receive it.

**The tasks.** Supply one instrument to one other man, in the form of Chapter 44.5's five functions: presence, information, accompaniment, the limit, and continuity. Conduct the second administration of Appendix A's Sections 1 to 7.

**The review.** Answer the four tests of Chapter 44.4's closing material, and record the plan for the following ninety days, which is the material of Appendix P.

## The Difficulties That Will Occur

The material of this book records five difficulties that arise in a thirty-day plan, and their answers.

**The first difficulty is the collapse of the plan in the first week.** The most frequent cause is that the plan was added to an unchanged position. The answer is the removal of the one demand, conducted again, and the reduction of the daily core to the three elements that are genuinely conductable.

**The second difficulty is the man's sense that the plan is trivial.** The daily elements are small and they are not trivial: they are the establishment of the arrangement that the position has lacked. The correction is the record, which shows the accumulation.

**The third difficulty is the return of a symptom or a state in the third week.** The return is frequently a response to the statement's conduct rather than a failure of the plan, and its answer is the professional route rather than the plan's abandonment.

**The fourth difficulty is the household's response.** A household that has conducted a man's silence for twenty years frequently responds to the change with suspicion, and the suspicion is frequently the material of Chapter 42's costs. The answer is the conduct of the plan without requiring the household's approval, and the proportionate statement of what the man is doing.

**The fifth difficulty is the plan's completion.** A man who completes the thirty days frequently concludes that the work is done. The finding of the case material is that the thirty days establishes a condition and the arrangement continues for a year or more, which is the material of Appendix P.

## The Measures

At the end of the thirty days, record five measures.

1. **The conduct.** The number of days on which the daily core was completed, out of thirty.
2. **The statement.** The number of statements of difficulty made to a party who has a right to know, and the parties to whom they were made.
3. **The appointment.** Whether the professional appointment was made, attended, and what it produced.
4. **The alteration.** Whether the one structural alteration was conducted, and its measure.
5. **The score.** The Appendix A totals, compared with the first administration.

The case material's finding is that men who complete the daily core on more than twenty days and conduct at least one structural alteration report a change that they can identify in the following year, and that men who complete the plan as a reading exercise report that nothing occurred.
