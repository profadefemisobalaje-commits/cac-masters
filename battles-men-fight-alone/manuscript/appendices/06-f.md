# Marriage and Relationship Reflection Workbook {.appendix letter="F"}

This appendix is the working instrument for Part IV and Chapters 14, 15, 18, and 39. It is designed for a man who wishes to examine his marriage or partnership accurately, whether the position is stable, strained, or in the process of ending.

## How to Use This Appendix

Complete the workbook alone first, in one sitting of about an hour. Then, where the relationship is intact and the party is willing, conduct the joint section with the party. The workbook is not a test of the marriage and it is not a judgement of either party; it is a description of an arrangement, and the description is the condition of any change.

## Part One: The Arrangement as It Is

Answer each question in two or three sentences, without evaluation and without the language of blame.

1. What does the household's week actually consist of? Record the division of the labour, the money, the childcare, the extended family's obligations, and the time.
2. Who decides what, and how are decisions of consequence taken?
3. What do we talk about, and what do we not talk about?
4. When did we last have a conversation that was not about the household's administration?
5. What is the last thing I told my partner that was difficult for me?
6. What is the last thing my partner told me that was difficult for them, and what did I do with it?
7. What does my partner know about my work, my money, my health, and my fears?
8. What do I know about theirs?

## Part Six: The Household's Arrangements

The material of Chapters 18 and 42.1's fourth domain.

1. Who is actually conducting the household's provision, and does the household's account of itself match?
2. What does the household's expenditure consist of, and who knows it?
3. What obligations to the extended family exist, who agreed to them, and who pays for them?
4. What decisions have been taken in the last year without the other party's knowledge?
5. What does the household's reserve consist of, and who holds it?

## Part Seven: The Values and the Future

The material of Chapter 40.5's second-half audit in its marriage form.

1. What does the man want the marriage to be in five years, stated in four sentences?
2. What does he believe his partner wants, stated in four sentences?
3. What are the three matters on which the two accounts are furthest apart?
4. What is the one matter that is most urgent?
5. What is the one matter that is most consequential and least urgent?

## Part Eight: The Joint Section

Conduct this section with the party where the relationship is intact and the party is willing. It requires ninety minutes and it should not be conducted when either party is tired, hungry, or in the middle of a dispute.

**The rules of the joint section.** Each party answers in turn without interruption. No answer is evaluated. The purpose is the accurate position and not the resolution. Any matter that produces strong feeling is recorded and deferred to a subsequent conversation.

**The questions.**

1. What did you need from me in the last year that you did not receive?
2. What did I do in the last year that helped?
3. What do you carry that I do not know about?
4. What do you think I carry that I have not told you?
5. What is the one change you would ask of me, stated specifically?
6. What are you willing to conduct differently?

**The closing statement.** Each party states one thing they will do before the next conversation, and the date of the next conversation is fixed.

## Part Nine: Where the Relationship Is Ending or Has Ended

Where the marriage is ending or has ended, four sections of this workbook apply in modified form.

The first is Part One, which supplies the accurate account of the arrangement and is frequently useful in the professional processes that follow. The second is Part Three, which supplies the account of the injuries and the man's own contribution, and which is the material of Chapter 44.6's exit rather than of the marriage's repair. The third is Part Seven, which supplies the account of what the man wants in the following chapter. The fourth is the material of Chapter 30 and Appendix K, since the end of a marriage is a loss and requires a grief rather than a verdict.

The finding of the case material is that men who conduct these four sections within the first year of the end report a substantially different trajectory from men who conduct the marriage's ending as a continuous argument, and that the difference is not in the difficulty of the ending but in whether the loss is conducted.
