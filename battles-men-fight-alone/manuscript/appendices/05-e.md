# Healthy Communication Exercises for Men {.appendix letter="E"}

This appendix supplies the communication practices of Parts IV and V in exercise form. Each exercise is short, each is conducted with a party, and each is designed to be repeated until it is available without effort, which is the method of Chapter 43.5.

## The Principles

Six principles govern the exercises.

1. **The exercise is conducted when calm, not when the subject is live.** The rehearsal of Chapter 43.5's second stage applies to adults as much as to boys.
2. **The statement is specific.** A general statement cannot be received; a specific one can.
3. **The statement is owned.** The man speaks from his own position — *I noticed*, *I felt*, *I need* — and does not attribute the party's motives.
4. **The statement includes a request.** A statement without a request leaves the party without an instrument.
5. **The party's response is received without defence.** The first task is to understand what the party has said, and the second is to answer it.
6. **The exercise is concluded.** Each exercise has an ending, and the ending is stated.

## Exercise 1: The Daily Statement

**Purpose.** To establish the habit of stating a state in ordinary language.

**Conduct.** Once a day, for fourteen days, state one thing to one party in the form: *I noticed [the fact]; I felt [the state]; I need [the request, or nothing].*

**Examples.**

- *I noticed that I have been short with the children since Monday. I am worried about the review. I do not need anything from you this evening; I wanted you to know.*
- *I noticed that I did not sleep well. I am tired and I am not at my best. I need an hour this afternoon.*
- *I noticed that you have been quiet since your brother called. I am not sure what it is, and I am here if you want to say.*

**The measure.** The number of days on which the statement was made, and the party's response.

## Exercise 2: The Listening Turn

**Purpose.** To practise receiving a party's statement without solving, reassuring, or redirecting.

**Conduct.** In a conversation of twenty minutes, the party speaks for ten minutes on a matter of their choosing. The man's task is to listen, to ask one clarifying question, and to state what he has heard. He is not permitted to offer a solution, to describe a similar experience of his own, or to reassure.

**The form of the closing statement.** *What I heard is [summary]. Have I understood it?*

**The measure.** Whether the man conducted the four prohibitions, and the party's report of whether they felt heard.

## Exercise 3: The Request

**Purpose.** To convert a need into a specific request.

**Conduct.** Write three requests in the form: *I need [specific instrument] from you, by [specific time], and this is what it would change.* Then make one of the three within the week.

**The test.** A request that does not name an instrument, a party, and a time is not a request. It is a complaint, and the case material of Chapter 45.2 records that complaints are frequently answered with sympathy and instruments with action.

## Exercise 4: The Repair

**Purpose.** To conduct the sequence that Chapter 39.5 identifies as the most valuable practice in a household.

**Conduct.** Identify one act of the man's own that damaged a party. Then conduct the four-part sequence, in person:

1. **The act.** *I did [specific act], on [occasion].*
2. **The effect.** *I think it affected you by [specific effect]. Is that right?*
3. **The acknowledgement.** *There is no excuse for it, and I am not going to offer one.*
4. **The repair.** *This is what I will do, and this is when.*

**The prohibition.** The sequence may not contain the words *but*, *you also*, or *I was tired*. Any of the three converts the repair into a defence.

## Exercise 5: The Difficult Conversation

**Purpose.** To conduct a conversation about a matter that has been avoided.

**Conduct.** Prepare in writing, in six lines, then conduct the conversation within the week.

| Line | The content |
|:----------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1. The subject | The matter, stated in one sentence, without evaluation. |
| 2. The fact | What happened, with the date and the specific act. |
| 3. The effect | What it cost the man, stated as his own experience. |
| 4. The acknowledgement | What the man's own contribution was, where there is one. |
| 5. The request | What he requires, specifically. |
| 6. The limit | What he will do if the request is not met, stated without threat. |

**The conduct.** The six lines are spoken in order, without deviation, and the party is given the opportunity to respond after each. The conversation's purpose is the accurate statement and not the party's agreement.

## Exercise 6: The Household Conversation

**Purpose.** To conduct the household's position statement of Chapter 42.6's third step.

**Conduct.** A conversation of forty minutes, at a stated time, with the household's adults and, where appropriate, the older children.

**The sequence.**

1. The purpose is stated: the household's position, at its true level, so that the household can conduct it together.
2. The position is stated in figures: income, obligations, margin, reserve.
3. The concealed items are stated, with the man's acknowledgement that they were concealed.
4. The household is asked what it already knows and what information it holds that the man does not.
5. The reductions are stated, in the three layers of Appendix D.
6. The participation is requested: what each member will conduct.
7. The date of the next conversation is fixed.

## Exercise 7: The Stated Difficulty in Front of a Younger Party

**Purpose.** To conduct the transmission described in Chapter 43.6.

**Conduct.** One statement, proportionate, in the presence of a boy or young man, followed by an action.

**The form.** Two or three sentences, accurate, and directed at what the younger party can receive: *the work has been difficult this year and I have been worried. This is what I am doing about it. You are not required to solve it.*

**The prohibition.** The statement may not be used to recruit the younger party as a confidant, to obtain sympathy, or to make the party responsible for the man's state. The statement is conducted for the party's curriculum and not for the man's relief.

## Exercise 8: The Twenty-Minute Marriage Conversation

**Purpose.** To interrupt the arrangement of parallel responsibilities described in Chapter 15.

**Conduct.** Twenty minutes, once a week, at a stated time, with three questions asked in order and answered by both parties.

1. What was difficult for you this week?
2. What did I do that helped, and what did I do that did not?
3. What do you need from me next week?

**The rules.** No problem is solved in the twenty minutes; the conversation is for the position and not for the resolution. Each party speaks without interruption. The answers are not scored.
