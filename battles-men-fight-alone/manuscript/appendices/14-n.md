# Work-Life Balance and Burnout Prevention Toolkit {.appendix letter="N"}

This appendix supplies the material of Chapters 31, 33, 37, and 44 in practical form. It is written for the man whose work has expanded to fill the space that his rest, his household, and his health previously occupied, and for the man in a leadership position who is responsible for other men's workloads as well as his own.

## The Position

Four findings govern the appendix.

**The first is that the balance is not a division of hours.** A man may work long hours in a period that requires it and be conducting a sound arrangement, and a man may work short hours and be conducting an unsound one. The distinction is in whether the arrangement has rest, presence, and recovery inside it, and whether the man retains the capacity to reduce.

**The second is that the man's work frequently performs a function beyond income.** The case material records work used to manage a state: the grief that is conducted by working, the marriage that is avoided by working, the shame that is answered by achievement, the fear that is managed by activity. A balance programme that does not address the function fails, because the work is supplying something the man requires.

**The third is that the balance is frequently an institutional question rather than a personal one.** The workload is produced by a supervisor, a contract, a market, or an expectation. The man who attempts a personal solution to an institutional load will conduct the solution for a period and will return to the load.

**The fourth is that the man's rest requires a discipline.** The case material records men who take leave and conduct it as an extension of work, men who rest and feel guilty throughout, and men who cannot rest without a justification. The material of Chapter 37 applies, and it is the item that most men omit.

### Exercise 3: The Function

Answer the three questions.

1. What does the work supply beyond the income? (Structure, identity, standing, escape, control, an answer to a state.)
2. What would arrive if the work reduced by a third, and which party would receive it?
3. What has the work displaced, and is the displacement stated to the party it affects?

The finding is that the answer to the first question determines whether a balance plan is sufficient, and that men whose work is managing a state require the material of Appendix B, Appendix K, or Appendix F before the balance plan will hold.

### Exercise 6: The Leave

Where leave is owing, fix the dates within the week, state them to the household and the institution, and record the arrangement by which the man will not conduct work during the leave. The finding is that the leave conducted with the work's continuation is not a leave and does not produce the recovery.

### Exercise 8: The Rest Discipline

The discipline is stated in four rules and conducted for four weeks.

1. **One period a week with no productive purpose**, stated in advance, and protected from the household's and the institution's demands.
2. **The end of the working day stated**, by a time and by a conduct: the telephone, the messages, the work that is not conducted after it.
3. **The sleep protected**, by a fixed hour and the removal of the screen from the bedroom.
4. **The justification removed.** The man does not justify his rest to any party, including himself. The rest is a component of the position and not a reward for it.

### Exercise 9: The Guilt

Where the guilt is present, record what the guilt states, and examine it in the form of Appendix H's five steps. The finding is that the guilt is frequently the voice of the clause described in Chapter 41.2, which computes the man's value at his exhaustion, and that the clause is the material rather than the rest.

## Part Four: The Institution

Where the reader supervises or employs men, the appendix's institutional section applies, because the finding is that men's workloads are produced by arrangements and that the arrangements are conducted by the parties who hold authority.

### The Five Practices of the Supervisor

1. **The workload is stated.** The supervisor records the actual hours and the actual load of each party, and reviews it quarterly.
2. **The cover is real.** The party's absence should be covered by an arrangement, and the absence should not produce a penalty in the review.
3. **The error is information.** The workplace's response to error determines whether men report difficulty, which is the material of Chapter 43.3.
4. **The disclosure is received.** The supervisor conducts the reception of Chapter 43.6 when a man states a difficulty, and refers to the professional route where the material exceeds his competence.
5. **The supervisor's own conduct is legible.** The supervisor who works without rest transmits the standard, whatever the policy states.

## Part Five: The Cautions

The first caution is that the reduction of hours without the reduction of the load produces the same load in fewer hours, and the finding is that this is the most frequent failure of balance programmes.

The second is that the man's household may have constructed its own arrangements around his absence, and the finding is that the household's adjustment to his presence takes months and is frequently accompanied by difficulty, which is the material of Chapter 17.5.

The third is that the balance programme is not a substitute for the treatment of a burnout, a depression, or an addiction. The material of Appendices B and M applies.

The fourth is that the balance is not a permanent achievement. The finding is that the arrangement requires review at each change of position, and that the man who conducted the arrangement at forty frequently requires a different one at fifty.
