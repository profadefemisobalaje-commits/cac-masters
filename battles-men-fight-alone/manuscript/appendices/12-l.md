# Anger Management and Emotional Regulation Exercises {.appendix letter="L"}

This appendix supplies the regulation material of Chapters 3, 12, 18, and 39. It is written for the man whose anger is producing cost in his household, his work, or his health, and for the man whose regulation is conducted by suppression and who requires the alternatives.

## The Position

Three findings govern the appendix.

**The first is that anger is not the enemy.** Anger is a signal that a boundary has been crossed, a need has not been stated, or a state has not been conducted. The man whose anger is suppressed loses the signal; the man whose anger governs him loses the position. The appendix's subject is the conduct of the signal.

**The second is that anger is frequently the available expression of a state that the man cannot name.** The case material records anger standing in for fear, shame, grief, humiliation, exhaustion, and loneliness. The man who can name the underlying state frequently finds that the anger reduces without its being addressed directly, which is the material of Chapter 12.5.

**The third is that regulation is a competence and is trained.** It is not a matter of willpower and its absence is not a defect of character. The exercises below are rehearsed when calm, which is the method of Chapter 43.5.

## Part Two: The Regulation

### Exercise 4: The Physiological Practice

The practice is conducted twice a day when calm and is used at the third early sign.

1. **The pause.** Stop speaking. Place both feet on the floor. Count four seconds.
2. **The breath.** Breathe in through the nose for four counts, hold for four, out for six. Repeat four times.
3. **The body.** Release the jaw, the shoulders, and the hands deliberately.
4. **The statement to oneself.** *I am at the third sign. I will not speak for two minutes.*
5. **The exit.** Where the setting permits, leave for a stated period and state the return: *I need ten minutes; I will come back.*

The practice is trained twice daily for six weeks. Its purpose is not relaxation; it is the establishment of a conduct that is available at the third sign, which is the point at which the man still has the choice.

### Exercise 5: The Naming

At the third sign, name the underlying state aloud or in writing: *I am not angry; I am ashamed*, *I am afraid*, *I am exhausted*. The exercise is rehearsed in low-stakes situations first, since the capacity to name at the third sign is not available at the first attempt in a high-stakes one.

### Exercise 6: The Delay

The rule of the twenty-four hours applies to decisions taken in anger: no resignation, no message sent, no decision about the household, no confrontation. The rule is written and stated to the household in advance, and its statement is itself a repair, since the household's experience of the man's anger frequently includes decisions that were taken in it.

## Part Three: The Repair

### Exercise 7: The Four-Part Repair

Where the anger has produced damage, the repair of Appendix E, Exercise 4 applies, and it is conducted within seventy-two hours. The finding is that the repair conducted within three days is experienced as a repair and the repair conducted after three weeks is experienced as a reopening.

### Exercise 8: The Household Conversation

Conduct one conversation with the household, in the form of the following sequence.

1. The acknowledgement: what the man's anger has cost the household, stated specifically.
2. The pattern: what the man has recognised about his triggers and his early signs.
3. The practice: what he will do at the third sign, and what the household should expect.
4. The repair: what he will conduct after an occasion.
5. The request: what he requires of the household, which is generally to be told when the third sign is visible and to leave the room when he states the exit.

### Exercise 10: The Position

Where the anger is produced by the position rather than by the state, conduct the position statement of Appendix A, Section 9, and the alteration of Chapter 44.2's fifth question. The finding is that men frequently attempt to regulate an anger that is a correct response to an unmanageable arrangement, and that the regulation fails while the arrangement remains.

## Part Five: The Cautions

**The first caution** is that anger which has produced physical harm, threats, or the intimidation of a household is a matter for the professional route and, where a party is at risk of harm, for the appropriate authority. The exercises of this appendix are conducted alongside that route and not in place of it. Appendix Q supplies the routes.

**The second caution** is that the exercises are trained and not decided. A man who reads the appendix and resolves to be calmer will conduct the resolution for a period and will then conduct the pattern. The training is twice daily for six weeks and it is not optional.

**The third caution** is that the man's anger frequently has a history that the exercises do not address: a childhood in which anger was the household's currency, a trauma, a depression, a drinking pattern. The material of Appendices B and Q applies, and the finding is that the regulation exercises are substantially more effective when the underlying condition is treated.

**The fourth caution** is that the man's household may not believe the change for a period. The finding is that households which have conducted a man's anger for years require three to six months of consistent conduct before they alter their expectation, and that the man's task in the interval is the conduct without the requirement of the household's belief.
