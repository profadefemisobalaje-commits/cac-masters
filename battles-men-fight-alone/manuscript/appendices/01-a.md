# Men's Emotional Self-Assessment Workbook {.appendix letter="A"}

This appendix is the book's general instrument. It is designed to be completed before the reader begins the chapter-specific exercises, and again at three months and at twelve months. Its purpose is not diagnosis. It is to establish, in writing, the position from which the reader is starting, so that the changes described in the book can be observed rather than remembered.

## How to Use This Appendix

Complete the workbook in one or two sittings of not less than forty minutes. Answer from your conduct in the last four weeks rather than from your intentions, your history, or your better seasons. Where a question does not apply, write *not applicable* and state why. Where an answer is uncomfortable, record the discomfort in the margin and continue; the discomfort is data.

Score each item from 0 to 4 using the scale below, and write the total for each section.

| Score | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| 0 | Never, or almost never, in the last four weeks |
| 1 | Occasionally, but not in most weeks |
| 2 | In about half of the relevant occasions |
| 3 | In most relevant occasions |
| 4 | Consistently, in almost every relevant occasion |

For the sections marked **inverted**, a lower score is better; the scoring note under each section states the direction.

## Section 1: Naming States (0–32)

| Item | Score |
|:------------------------------------------------------------------|:---:|
| I can name what I am feeling with a word more precise than *fine* or *angry*. | |
| I notice a change in my state within the same day that it occurs. | |
| I can distinguish disappointment from shame when both are present. | |
| I can distinguish fear from anger in my own record. | |
| I can state where in my body a state is felt. | |
| I can state what preceded a state, rather than only that it occurred. | |

*Higher is better. A total below 16 indicates that the vocabulary described in Chapters 3 and 43.5 is not yet in use.*

## Section 2: Regulation (0–28)

| Item | Score |
|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:---:|

## The Three Questions

After scoring, answer three questions in writing.

**Question One: Which section has the lowest score, and what does that indicate about the arrangement?**

Record the section and one sentence about its mechanism. A low score in Section 2 indicates a regulation difficulty; a low score in Section 3 indicates a statement difficulty; a low score in Section 7 indicates an isolation that the reader may have attributed to circumstance.

**Question Two: Which single item in the whole workbook would produce the largest change if it were altered, and what would alter it?**

Write one item, one act, and one date. The case material's finding is that readers who conduct this question and act on the answer within a month make more progress than readers who attempt to address the lowest section in its entirety.

**Question Three: What did you decline to answer, and why?**

The unanswered items are frequently the most informative. Record them, and record the reason without judging it.

## Notes on Interpretation

Four cautions apply to the workbook's use.

The first is that the instrument measures conduct and not worth. A low score describes an arrangement that is producing a cost; it does not describe a man.

The second is that the workbook is not a clinical instrument. Where Section 2 and Section 5 both score low, and where the reader's sleep, appetite, interest, or capacity for work has altered for more than two weeks, the material of Appendix B and the professional route of Appendix Q apply.

The third is that the scores are not a target. A man who pursues the total rather than the arrangement will frequently score well and conduct nothing, since the workbook is self-reported and a man may answer from his understanding of the correct answer rather than from his conduct. The correct answer is frequently visible to the reader's household, and the reader who is willing should ask one party to complete Sections 3, 4, and 7 about him.

The fourth is that the workbook's value is in its repetition. A single administration produces a photograph. Three administrations produce a direction, and the direction is the material.
