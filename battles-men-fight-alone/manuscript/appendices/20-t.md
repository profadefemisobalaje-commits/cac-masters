# Personal Commitment: My Life Beyond Silent Battles {.appendix letter="T"}

This appendix is the book's final instrument. It is a document that the reader completes, dates, signs, and keeps. It is deliberately short, because it is intended to be encountered rather than read, and its value is in the encounter.

## How to Complete This Appendix

Complete the document in one sitting of about forty minutes, with the four tables of Chapter 45.7 in front of you. Write in your own hand. Where an item cannot be completed, write *not yet* rather than leaving it blank, and record the date on which you will return to it.

Then do three things with the document.

**The first is to sign and date it.**

**The second is to state it to one party**, in the form of Chapter 43.6's script, and to record the party's name.

**The third is to place it where you will encounter it** in three months: a drawer you open, a folder you use, the inside of a book you return to, the back of a diary. The finding of the case material is that the document's effect is produced by the encounter and not by the writing.

## Part Six: The Things I Will Not Do

I will not use my difficulty as a licence to reduce the position of any party in my household.

I will not conduct a resolution in place of an arrangement.

I will not wait for a crisis in order to state what is already known.

I will not require of myself a capacity that is not mine.

I will not conduct my position alone in order to protect my standing from a party whose estimate of me is not conditional on it.

## A Closing Note

This appendix ends the book's practical material, and it is placed last because everything else in the book is conducted in order to make this document possible.

The document's finding is stated in the form in which the case material records it. Men who complete this document and state it to a party report, at the twelve-month review, that the position they described has altered: not because the difficulty was removed, and because the difficulty was conducted by more than one party, with instruments, at a true level, and with a record.

That is the whole of the book's claim. It is not a claim about what a man can become. It is a claim about what a man can conduct when he stops conducting it alone.
