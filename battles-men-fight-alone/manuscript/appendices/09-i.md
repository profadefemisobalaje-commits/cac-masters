# Personal Values, Identity, and Purpose Discovery Exercises {.appendix letter="I"}

This appendix supplies the identity material of Parts VI and XII in exercise form. It is designed for a man who has conducted the position described in Chapter 21 and Chapter 24 and who requires an account of himself that is not derived from his function, his standing, or his history.

## The Method

The exercises proceed in four stages: the audit of the inherited definition, the identification of the man's own values, the construction of a working identity, and the statement of purpose. Each stage is conducted in writing, and each is conducted twice: once quickly and once after a period of reflection, since the first answers are frequently the culture's and the second are frequently the man's.

### Exercise 1.3: The Clause That Has Cost You the Most

Identify the clause that has produced the greatest cost in your adult life, and record the costs specifically: the years, the relationship, the health, the opportunity, the state you have not stated.

### Exercise 1.4: The Eight Tests

Conduct the eight tests of Chapter 41.3 against the clause identified above.

1. Does it require something of you that it does not require of others in the same position?
2. Does it compute your value at your exhaustion?
3. Does it require you to conceal information from a party who has a right to know it?
4. Does it convert a responsibility into a prohibition on stating it?
5. Does it treat help as a defect?
6. Does it make a reduction in your position a reduction in you?
7. Does it require you to conduct alone something that cannot be conducted alone?
8. Would you teach it, in its present form, to a boy you loved?

### Exercise 2.2: The Evidence

For each of the three values, record the last three occasions on which your conduct demonstrated it. Where the value has not been demonstrated in the last year, record that fact and record what has taken its place.

### Exercise 2.3: The Conflict

Identify the values that come into conflict in your ordinary life and record how you have resolved the conflict in the last year. The most frequent conflicts in the case material are provision against presence, loyalty against truthfulness, endurance against health, and independence against belonging.

## Stage Three: The Working Identity

### Exercise 3.1: The Six Statements

Complete each sentence with an accurate statement that is not derived from your function.

1. Beyond my work, I am ______________________________________________
2. Beyond my provision, I am __________________________________________
3. Beyond my history, I am ____________________________________________
4. Beyond my standing, I am ___________________________________________
5. Beyond my endurance, I am _________________________________________
6. Beyond my failures, I am ___________________________________________

### Exercise 3.2: The Witness

For each statement, record the party who could confirm it from observation. Where no party could confirm a statement, record that fact: the statement is a description of an intention rather than of a conduct, and the exercise's finding is that the statements without witnesses are the ones the man most requires to conduct.

### Exercise 3.3: The Account of the Man

Write a description of yourself in one paragraph, in the third person, as a party who knew you well would write it. The paragraph may not contain your position, your income, your title, or your history of difficulty. It may contain your conduct, your relationships, your competences, your values, and your ordinary habits.

### Exercise 3.4: The Correction

Compare the paragraph with the account you would give at an occasion. Record the three principal differences. The differences are the material of Chapter 41.5's substitution exercise.

## The Record

The appendix's product is a single page, written in the man's own hand, containing the three values, the six statements with their witnesses, the corrected account, the purpose statement, and the one act with its date.

The case material's finding is that men who write the page and keep it where they will encounter it report a different trajectory from men who conduct the exercises and file them, and that the difference is produced by the encounter rather than by the writing.

## The Cautions

The first caution is that the exercises are not a substitute for the professional route where the man's position includes a depression, a trauma, or a significant loss. The material of Appendix B applies.

The second is that the exercises are conducted over weeks rather than hours. A man who completes all four stages in an evening will produce the culture's answers, and the exercises' value is in the second and third answers.

The third is that the exercises do not produce a new identity. They produce an account of the identity the man has been conducting without stating it, and the account is the instrument by which the unnecessary parts are removed.
