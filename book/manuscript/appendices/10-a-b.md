# Selected International Energy and Natural Resources Treaties {.appendix letter="A"}

This appendix lists the principal treaties and other international instruments examined in this book, with their dates of adoption and entry into force and a short note of their relevance to energy and natural resources. It is a finding aid, not a substitute for the authoritative texts, which are available from the depositaries (principally the United Nations Treaty Collection and the depositary organisations named). Dates of entry into force refer to the instrument's general entry into force; readers should check the status of ratification of each instrument for any particular state, together with any reservations, declarations, amendments, or withdrawals.

## Using Treaties in Research and Practice

When working with any of these instruments, the researcher or practitioner should: (1) obtain the authentic text from the depositary; (2) confirm the status of the relevant state (signature, ratification, accession, succession, withdrawal) and the date from which obligations apply; (3) identify reservations, declarations, and objections; (4) check for amendments, protocols, and decisions of the treaty bodies (such as decisions of the Conference of the Parties); (5) consult the *travaux préparatoires* where relevant under article 32 VCLT; (6) consider how the treaty has been interpreted by courts, tribunals, and treaty bodies; and (7) consider the relationship between the treaty and domestic law, including whether domestication is required — as under section 12 of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999, which provides that a treaty does not have the force of law in Nigeria unless enacted into law by the National Assembly.

# Model Petroleum Exploration and Production Agreement {.appendix letter="B"}

The following model sets out the structure and key clauses of an exploration and production agreement of the licence-plus-agreement or concession type, with drafting notes. It is an **illustrative teaching model**, prepared for this book. It is not modelled on any particular state's form and must not be used in any transaction without adaptation to the applicable law and professional advice. Bracketed text indicates matters for negotiation or national choice.

## Parties and Recitals

**THIS AGREEMENT** is made on [date] between:

(1) **THE GOVERNMENT OF [STATE]**, acting through the [Ministry responsible for petroleum] (the "**Government**"); [and, where applicable, the national oil company, [NOC]]; and

(2) **[CONTRACTOR COMPANY]**, a company incorporated under the laws of [jurisdiction] (the "**Contractor**").

**WHEREAS** (A) all petroleum existing in its natural condition in the territory, territorial sea, exclusive economic zone, and continental shelf of [State] is vested in [the State / the people of the State, held in trust by the State] under [constitutional or statutory provision]; (B) the Government wishes to promote the exploration and development of petroleum resources in the Contract Area for the benefit of [the State and its people]; and (C) the Contractor has the financial capability, technical competence, and professional skills necessary to carry out Petroleum Operations.

*Drafting note.* The recitals should accurately reflect the constitutional or statutory ownership regime (Chapter 3). In Nigeria, for instance, section 44(3) of the Constitution vests the entire property in and control of all minerals, mineral oils, and natural gas in the Government of the Federation, and the Petroleum Industry Act 2021 provides that petroleum is vested in the Government of the Federation for the benefit of Nigerians.

## Article 1 — Definitions and Interpretation

Key definitions should include: Affiliate; Appraisal Well; Associated Gas; Barrel; Commercial Discovery; Contract Area; Contract Year; Crude Oil; Decommissioning; Development Plan; Discovery; Effective Date; Exploration Period; Good International Petroleum Industry Practice ("**GIPIP**"); Natural Gas; Non-Associated Gas; Petroleum; Petroleum Operations; Production Period; Work Programme and Budget.

*Drafting note.* The definition of GIPIP is a frequent source of dispute. A definition referring to "practices, methods, standards, and procedures generally accepted and followed by prudent, diligent, skilled, and experienced operators in the international petroleum industry in similar circumstances" is common; parties may refer to specific standards (such as those of the International Association of Oil and Gas Producers) to reduce uncertainty.

## Article 2 — Grant of Rights and Scope

2.1 The Government grants to the Contractor the exclusive right to conduct Petroleum Operations in the Contract Area during the term of this Agreement, subject to the Petroleum Law and the terms of this Agreement.

2.2 The Contractor shall bear all risks and costs of Petroleum Operations. [If no Commercial Discovery is made, the Contractor shall not be entitled to any reimbursement.]

2.3 [Title to petroleum passes to the Contractor at the wellhead / at the Delivery Point, subject to payment of royalty.]

## Article 3 — Term, Phases, and Relinquishment

3.1 The Exploration Period shall be [six] years, divided into an Initial Phase of [three] years and [one/two] Extension Phases, each conditional on fulfilment of the Minimum Work Obligations of the preceding phase.

3.2 At the end of each phase, the Contractor shall relinquish [25–50] per cent of the original Contract Area, excluding Development Areas and areas subject to an approved appraisal programme.

3.3 Upon declaration of a Commercial Discovery and approval of a Development Plan, the Contractor shall be entitled to a Production Period of [20–25] years for the relevant Development Area, [extendable by agreement].

## Article 4 — Minimum Work Obligations and Guarantees

4.1 The Contractor shall carry out, as a minimum, the following work: [Initial Phase: acquisition and processing of [x] km² of 3D seismic; drilling of [one] exploration well to a minimum depth of [x] metres or to [named formation]].

4.2 The Contractor shall provide a [bank guarantee / parent company guarantee] in the amount of [USD x] securing the Minimum Work Obligations; the amount shall reduce as obligations are performed.

4.3 If the Contractor fails to complete the Minimum Work Obligations, it shall pay to the Government an amount equal to the estimated cost of the unperformed work.

## Article 5 — Work Programmes, Budgets, and Operating Committee

5.1 The Contractor shall submit annual Work Programmes and Budgets to the [Regulator] for [review/approval] within [x] days before the start of each Contract Year.

5.2 [Where the NOC participates, an Operating Committee comprising representatives of the parties shall supervise Petroleum Operations; decisions shall be taken by [voting rules], with [specified matters] reserved to unanimity.]

## Article 6 — Discovery, Appraisal, and Development

6.1 The Contractor shall notify the Regulator of any Discovery within [30] days and submit an appraisal programme within [x] months.

6.2 If the Contractor considers a Discovery commercial, it shall submit a Development Plan containing: reservoir and production profiles; development concept and facilities; cost estimates; gas utilisation plan (including measures to eliminate routine flaring); local content plan; environmental and social management plan; health and safety plan; and preliminary decommissioning plan and cost estimate.

6.3 The Regulator shall approve the Development Plan [within x days], approval not to be unreasonably withheld.

## Article 7 — Royalty, Taxes, and Fiscal Terms

7.1 The Contractor shall pay royalty at the rates set out in [Annex / the Petroleum Law], [on a sliding scale by production or price].

7.2 The Contractor shall be subject to [petroleum income tax / corporate income tax and any resource rent tax] in accordance with the applicable fiscal legislation.

7.3 [Signature bonus of USD x payable within 30 days of the Effective Date; production bonuses on reaching specified cumulative production.]

7.4 [Annual surface rentals per km² of the Contract Area.]

*Drafting note.* Fiscal terms are increasingly set by legislation rather than negotiated contract (see Chapter 16). Stabilisation of fiscal terms (Article 18) should be considered alongside.

## Article 8 — State Participation

8.1 The Government shall have the right, exercisable through [NOC], to acquire a participating interest of up to [x] per cent in any Development Area, [carried through exploration / reimbursed from production].

## Article 9 — Domestic Supply Obligation and Gas

9.1 The Contractor shall supply [a proportion] of its share of crude oil [and natural gas] to the domestic market at [prices set by formula].

9.2 The Contractor shall not flare or vent natural gas except as permitted by the Petroleum Law for safety reasons or with the Regulator's approval, and shall pay any applicable flaring penalties.

## Article 10 — Local Content, Employment, and Training

10.1 The Contractor shall give preference to [nationals / national companies] in employment and procurement, in accordance with [local content legislation], and shall implement an approved training programme.

## Article 11 — Host Community and Social Obligations

11.1 The Contractor shall [establish / contribute to] [a host community development trust or community development agreement] in accordance with [applicable law] and shall consult affected communities in good faith throughout Petroleum Operations.

## Article 12 — Environment, Health, and Safety

12.1 The Contractor shall conduct Petroleum Operations in accordance with applicable environmental law and GIPIP, shall carry out an environmental and social impact assessment before commencing any significant operations, and shall prevent, minimise, and remedy pollution.

12.2 The Contractor shall be liable for pollution damage caused by Petroleum Operations [without prejudice to fault-based or strict liability under applicable law] and shall maintain insurance [and financial security] adequate to meet such liability.

## Article 13 — Decommissioning

13.1 The Contractor shall decommission all facilities in accordance with an approved decommissioning plan and shall make contributions to a decommissioning fund [from the start of production / when [x] per cent of reserves have been produced].

## Article 14 — Information, Data, and Confidentiality

14.1 All data acquired in the course of Petroleum Operations shall be the property of the State; the Contractor may use the data for the purposes of Petroleum Operations.

14.2 [This Agreement shall be published.] Subject thereto, the parties shall keep confidential information obtained under this Agreement, with exceptions for disclosure required by law, stock exchange rules, or transparency commitments (including the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative).

## Article 15 — Assignment and Change of Control

15.1 The Contractor shall not assign any interest in this Agreement, or undergo a change of control, without the prior written consent of the Government, [not to be unreasonably withheld], [and subject to payment of any applicable tax on gains].

## Article 16 — Force Majeure

16.1 Neither party shall be liable for failure to perform an obligation (other than an obligation to pay money) to the extent that performance is prevented by an event beyond its reasonable control which could not reasonably have been foreseen or avoided. The affected party shall notify promptly and use reasonable endeavours to mitigate. Periods for performance shall be extended accordingly.

## Article 17 — Anti-Corruption

17.1 Each party represents and undertakes that it has not made and will not make any payment or offer of anything of value, directly or indirectly, to any public official or other person in violation of applicable anti-corruption law, [and the Government may terminate this Agreement in the event of material breach].

## Article 18 — Stabilisation

18.1 [If, after the Effective Date, a change in law materially and adversely affects the economic benefits of the Contractor under this Agreement, the parties shall negotiate in good faith such amendments as are necessary to restore the economic equilibrium, [provided that this clause shall not apply to laws of general application adopted in good faith for the protection of health, safety, the environment, or human rights].]

*Drafting note.* Freezing clauses have largely given way to economic equilibrium clauses; the proviso reflects the concern, expressed in the UN Principles for Responsible Contracts (2011), that stabilisation should not impede legitimate regulation (see Chapters 14 and 15).

## Article 19 — Termination

19.1 The Government may terminate this Agreement upon [material breach not remedied within x days of notice; insolvency; failure to pay amounts due; failure to commence production by a specified date], subject to the dispute resolution procedure.

## Article 20 — Governing Law and Dispute Resolution

20.1 This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of [State] [and such rules of international law as may be applicable].

20.2 Disputes shall be referred first to [senior management negotiation for x days], then [to an expert in the case of technical disputes], and finally to arbitration under [the ICSID Convention / the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules / the ICC Rules], seated in [neutral seat], before three arbitrators, in the English language.
