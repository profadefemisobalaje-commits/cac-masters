# Sample Renewable Energy Power Purchase Agreement {.appendix letter="E"}

This appendix sets out the structure of a long-term power purchase agreement (PPA) for a grid-connected solar or wind project sold to a state-owned or regulated off-taker, with drafting notes on bankability. It is an **illustrative teaching model** and should be read with Chapter 9, section 9.5, Chapter 10, and Chapter 16. Note the regulatory context in Nigeria, where the Electricity Act 2023 allows states to regulate intra-state electricity markets and where the Nigerian Bulk Electricity Trading Plc has historically acted as bulk purchaser.

## Clause 1 — Parties and Recitals

The PPA is concluded between [Project Company] (the "**Seller**"), a special purpose vehicle that will build, own, and operate a [x] MW solar photovoltaic plant at [site], and [Off-taker] (the "**Buyer**"). Recitals record the Seller's generation licence, the grid connection agreement, the implementation agreement or government support arrangements (if any), and the Buyer's authority to purchase electricity.

## Clause 2 — Term and Conditions Precedent

The term runs for [20–25] years from the Commercial Operation Date ("**COD**"). Conditions precedent include financial close, permits, land rights, the grid connection agreement, government support agreement, and, where applicable, the provision of payment security by the Buyer. A long-stop date permits termination if they are not satisfied.

## Clause 3 — Construction and Commissioning

The Seller shall achieve COD by the Scheduled COD, subject to extension for Buyer risk events and force majeure. Commissioning tests (including capacity tests and grid code compliance tests) must be witnessed by the Buyer and the system operator. Delay liquidated damages may be payable by the Seller for late COD, and deemed energy payments by the Buyer where completion is delayed by the Buyer or by the unavailability of the grid.

## Clause 4 — Sale and Purchase

> 4.1 From COD, the Seller shall sell and deliver, and the Buyer shall purchase and accept, all Net Electrical Output delivered at the Delivery Point, together with all environmental attributes associated with it [unless otherwise agreed].

*Drafting note.* Variable renewable PPAs are typically "as generated" (take-and-pay for all output) rather than capacity-based. The treatment of environmental attributes (renewable energy certificates, carbon credits) must be explicit (Chapter 10, section 10.7).

## Clause 5 — Tariff

> 5.1 The Buyer shall pay for Net Electrical Output at the Tariff of [USD/kWh x] [indexed annually at [x] per cent / to US CPI for the foreign currency component], determined [through competitive auction / by the regulator].

*Drafting note.* Currency risk is a critical bankability issue. Tariffs may be denominated in foreign currency, in local currency indexed to the exchange rate, or partly in each. Nigerian practice has involved dollar-indexed tariffs, and the impact of naira devaluation has been a significant source of financial strain in the sector.

## Clause 6 — Curtailment and Deemed Energy

> 6.1 If the Buyer or the System Operator instructs curtailment of the Facility, or the Facility is unable to deliver due to unavailability of the Grid, other than for reasons attributable to the Seller, emergency, or [x hours per year of permitted curtailment], the Buyer shall pay for the Deemed Energy that would have been delivered, calculated by reference to measured irradiance [or wind speed] and the Facility's performance model.

*Drafting note.* In weak grids, curtailment risk is high; lenders require deemed energy protection. The calculation methodology must be robust and verifiable.

## Clause 7 — Metering, Billing, and Payment

Metering at the Delivery Point; monthly invoicing; payment within [30] days; interest on late payment; and a dispute procedure in which undisputed amounts must be paid.

## Clause 8 — Payment Security

> 8.1 The Buyer shall provide and maintain [a standby letter of credit / a payment escrow] equal to [x] months of estimated payments. [The Government shall provide a guarantee / put-call option agreement under the Implementation Agreement.] [Partial risk guarantees from multilateral development institutions may support the Buyer's obligations.]

*Drafting note.* Off-taker creditworthiness is the single most important issue for many renewable projects in emerging markets. Instruments such as World Bank Group partial risk guarantees and the liquidity facilities developed for African IPPs have been used to mitigate it.

## Clause 9 — Operation and Dispatch

The Seller shall operate the Facility in accordance with Prudent Utility Practice, the Grid Code, and the instructions of the System Operator; provide forecasts of output; and maintain availability. Provisions address planned outages and the provision of ancillary services (where required, such as reactive power).

## Clause 10 — Change in Law

> 10.1 If a Change in Law results in an increase in the Seller's costs or a reduction in its revenues exceeding [threshold], the Tariff shall be adjusted to place the Seller in the same economic position as if the Change in Law had not occurred. A "Change in Law" includes the introduction, repeal, or change of any law, or change in the interpretation of any law by a competent authority, after [the bid date], [including changes in taxes specific to the electricity sector], but excluding [changes in general income tax].

## Clause 11 — Force Majeure

Distinguishes natural force majeure (extended time, no payment) from political force majeure (including expropriation, change in law, political violence, and governmental action), in respect of which the Buyer or Government may be required to continue payments or compensate the Seller.

## Clause 12 — Termination and Compensation

Events of default by each party, cure periods, and termination payments. Upon termination for Buyer default or political force majeure, the Buyer (or Government under the put option) must purchase the Facility at a price covering outstanding debt, equity, and a return; upon Seller default, the price may be limited to outstanding debt (or less).

## Clause 13 — Lender Protections

Consent to assignment by way of security; direct agreement giving lenders step-in rights and notice of default; and extended cure periods.

## Clause 14 — Environmental and Social Compliance

The Seller shall comply with applicable environmental and social law and [the IFC Performance Standards / Equator Principles], including land acquisition and resettlement requirements and grievance mechanisms (see Appendix G).

## Clause 15 — Disputes and Governing Law

Technical disputes to an expert; other disputes to arbitration under [ICC / LCIA / UNCITRAL Rules], seated in [London / a neutral seat / Lagos], with recourse to investment treaty protection where available (see Chapter 14 and Appendix I).

## Corporate PPAs: Key Differences

A corporate PPA between a generator and a commercial or industrial customer may be physical (requiring delivery through the network or private wire, subject to the regulatory regime for supply) or virtual/financial (a contract for difference against a market reference price, with no physical delivery). Corporate PPAs raise particular issues of credit support from the corporate buyer, shape and balancing risk, the allocation of guarantees of origin, and, in virtual PPAs, accounting treatment as derivatives.

# Mining Licence and Regulatory Compliance Checklist {.appendix letter="F"}

This checklist is designed to assist in reviewing the regulatory compliance of a mining project across its life cycle. It is general in form, drawing on the issues examined in Chapter 7 and Chapter 8, and must be adapted to the applicable law. Where Nigerian law applies, the principal statute is the Nigerian Minerals and Mining Act 2007, administered by the Ministry of Solid Minerals Development and the Mining Cadastre Office, together with the Nigerian Minerals and Mining Regulations 2011 and relevant environmental legislation, including the Environmental Impact Assessment Act.

## Using the Checklist

Each item should be supported by documentary evidence and, where possible, verified by site visit and interviews. Non-compliance should be classified by severity (critical, major, minor), with recommended corrective actions, responsibilities, and deadlines. The checklist is a tool for structured inquiry, not a substitute for legal analysis of the applicable statutes, regulations, and licence conditions.
