# Sample International Arbitration Clause and Procedural Documents {.appendix letter="I"}

This appendix provides model arbitration clauses and templates of key procedural documents for international energy arbitration. It should be read with Chapter 26. The model clauses of arbitral institutions are reproduced in substance from their published recommended forms; readers should always check the institution's current recommended wording before use.

## Institutional Model Clauses

**ICC.** The ICC recommends the following standard clause: "All disputes arising out of or in connection with the present contract shall be finally settled under the Rules of Arbitration of the International Chamber of Commerce by one or more arbitrators appointed in accordance with the said Rules." The ICC suggests that parties add the place of arbitration, the governing law, the number of arbitrators, and the language.

**LCIA.** The LCIA's recommended clause for future disputes provides: "Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this contract, including any question regarding its existence, validity or termination, shall be referred to and finally resolved by arbitration under the LCIA Rules, which Rules are deemed to be incorporated by reference into this clause." Parties then specify the number of arbitrators, the seat, the language, and the governing law.

**UNCITRAL.** The model clause annexed to the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules provides: "Any dispute, controversy or claim arising out of or relating to this contract, or the breach, termination or invalidity thereof, shall be settled by arbitration in accordance with the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules," with a note that the parties should consider adding the appointing authority, the number of arbitrators, the place of arbitration, and the language.

**ICSID.** For investment contracts with a state party, ICSID publishes model clauses by which the parties consent to submit disputes to the Centre for settlement by arbitration under the ICSID Convention, including provisions on nationality (for locally incorporated companies under foreign control, by agreement under article 25(2)(b)) and on the applicable law.

## A Tiered Dispute Resolution Clause for an Energy Contract (Model)

> **20.1 Negotiation.** Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement (a "**Dispute**") shall first be referred, by written notice, to senior executives of the Parties, who shall meet within fifteen (15) days and attempt in good faith to resolve the Dispute.
>
> **20.2 Expert determination.** Any Dispute concerning [measurement, quality, reserves determination, cost recovery accounting, or other technical matters specified in Annex X] not resolved within thirty (30) days of the notice may be referred by either Party to an independent expert appointed [by agreement / by the ICC International Centre for ADR]. The expert shall act as expert and not as arbitrator, and the expert's determination shall be final and binding save for fraud or manifest error.
>
> **20.3 Mediation.** [Any other Dispute not resolved within forty-five (45) days may, by agreement, be referred to mediation under [the ICC Mediation Rules / CEDR / other rules].]
>
> **20.4 Arbitration.** Any Dispute not resolved under Clauses 20.1 to 20.3 within [sixty (60)] days of the notice shall be finally settled by arbitration under the [Rules], by three arbitrators. The seat of arbitration shall be [London / Paris / Singapore / Geneva / Lagos]. The language shall be English. [The Parties waive any right of appeal on a point of law to the extent permitted by the law of the seat.]
>
> **20.5 Sovereign immunity.** [The State/NOC] irrevocably waives any immunity from jurisdiction and [to the extent permitted by law] from execution in respect of any arbitration and the enforcement of any award, [except in respect of assets used for diplomatic, military, or central bank purposes].
>
> **20.6 Continuing performance.** Pending resolution of a Dispute, the Parties shall continue to perform their obligations under this Agreement.

*Drafting notes.* (1) Ensure that pre-arbitral steps are clearly defined with fixed time limits, to avoid disputes over whether they are conditions precedent to arbitration (see *Emirates Trading Agency LLC v Prime Mineral Exports Pte Ltd* [2014] EWHC 2104 (Comm), enforcing a time-limited friendly discussion requirement). (2) Choose the seat carefully: it determines the curial law, the courts with supervisory jurisdiction, and the grounds for setting aside. Note the English Arbitration Act 2025, including the default rule on the law governing the arbitration agreement, and Nigeria's Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023. (3) Consider joinder and consolidation provisions in multi-contract projects. (4) Consider emergency arbitrator provisions and interim relief.

## Template: Request for Arbitration (Outline)

1. Names, addresses, and representatives of the parties.
2. The arbitration agreement invoked (with copy).
3. The contract(s) or other legal instrument(s) out of which the dispute arises.
4. A description of the nature and circumstances of the dispute.
5. The relief sought, including amounts claimed (to the extent possible).
6. Proposals concerning the number of arbitrators, nomination of arbitrator (if three), seat, applicable rules of law, and language.
7. Fulfilment of pre-arbitral steps.
8. Payment of the filing fee.

## Template: Procedural Order No. 1 (Outline)

1. Parties and representatives; tribunal; administrative secretary (if any) and their role.
2. Applicable rules; seat; language; place of hearings (including virtual hearings).
3. Communications and filing.
4. Procedural timetable (Annex).
5. Written submissions: number, sequence, and content (memorial and counter-memorial; reply and rejoinder).
6. Document production: scope and procedure, by reference to the IBA Rules on the Taking of Evidence in International Arbitration (2020) as guidelines; use of Redfern schedule.
7. Witness statements and expert reports; expert conferencing ("hot-tubbing").
8. Pre-hearing conference; hearing arrangements; time allocation (chess clock).
9. Post-hearing submissions and costs submissions.
10. Confidentiality and transparency; data protection and cyber security.
11. Tribunal secretary's role and fees; deposits.
12. Third-party funding disclosure.

## Template: Witness Statement (Structure)

1. Identity, position, and relationship to the parties.
2. Source of knowledge, distinguishing personal knowledge from information and belief.
3. Chronological narrative of relevant facts, with references to exhibits.
4. Statement of truth, date, and signature.

## Template: Expert Report (Structure)

1. Expert's qualifications, independence, and instructions.
2. Issues addressed and documents relied upon.
3. Methodology and assumptions.
4. Analysis and conclusions (for example, quantum by discounted cash flow, with sensitivity analysis).
5. Declaration of independence and duty to the tribunal.

## Investment Treaty Arbitration: Additional Checks

Before commencing an investment treaty claim, counsel should check: (a) the nationality of the investor and any planning or restructuring concerns (see *Philip Morris Asia v Australia*, PCA, 2015, on abuse of process); (b) the definition of investment; (c) temporal scope and any sunset clause (relevant, for example, to the twenty-year sunset clause of the Energy Charter Treaty following withdrawal); (d) cooling-off and local remedies requirements; (e) fork-in-the-road and waiver clauses; (f) denial of benefits; (g) for intra-EU disputes, the implications of *Achmea* (2018) and *Komstroy* (2021); and (h) enforcement prospects, including immunity from execution.

This appendix summarises the principal legal and institutional frameworks for petroleum, mining, and electricity in selected jurisdictions, as a starting point for comparative research (Chapter 24). The tables are necessarily simplified; laws and institutions change frequently, and readers must verify the current position from primary sources.
