# Preface {.front}

## Purpose of the Book

This book has been written to provide a comprehensive, advanced, and integrated text on international energy and natural resources law, suitable for doctoral study and for professionals working at the highest levels of the field. Its title — *Becoming a Doctor of International Energy and Natural Resources Law* — reflects its aspiration: to take the reader from a sound foundation in the principles of the field to the frontier of current scholarship, and to equip the reader with the analytical and methodological skills needed to contribute original knowledge to it. The title describes a journey of learning and research, not a credential; as stated throughout, a doctorate can be conferred only by an authorised institution following supervised research and examination.

## Scope and Approach

The book addresses the full range of energy and natural resources — oil, natural gas, coal, minerals (including critical and transition minerals), electricity, nuclear energy, renewable energy, hydrogen, carbon capture and storage, water, and marine resources — and the full range of legal disciplines that govern them: public international law, international economic law, environmental and climate law, human rights law, constitutional and administrative law, contract and commercial law, tax and finance law, and the law of dispute resolution. It is organised in eight Parts and thirty chapters, each comprising eight numbered sections, followed by twenty appendices of practical and research materials.

The approach is at once doctrinal, comparative, and contextual. Doctrinal, because a command of the authoritative sources — treaties, legislation, cases, and awards — is the foundation of legal expertise. Comparative, because energy and resource law is practised across jurisdictions, and because much can be learned from the experience of different legal systems. Contextual, because energy and resource law cannot be understood without an appreciation of the economic, technical, political, environmental, and social realities in which it operates, and of the gap that often exists between law in the books and law in action.

The book gives sustained attention to Africa, and to Nigeria in particular, not as an afterthought but as an integral part of the analysis. Africa holds a large share of the world's critical minerals and renewable energy potential and significant hydrocarbon resources; it is home to most of the world's people without access to electricity; and it has been the setting for many of the field's most important legal developments and controversies, from the litigation over oil pollution in the Niger Delta to the reform of mining codes and the creation of continental free trade. Nigeria's recent reforms — the Petroleum Industry Act 2021, the Electricity Act 2023, the Climate Change Act 2021, and associated fiscal and institutional changes — are examined in depth.

## Pedagogical Design

Each chapter follows a consistent structure designed to support advanced learning:

- **Learning objectives** identify the knowledge and skills the chapter seeks to develop.
- An **introduction** situates the chapter within the book and the field.
- **Eight numbered sections** provide substantive analysis, drawing on primary authorities and comparative perspectives.
- **Figures and tables** summarise complex frameworks and relationships.
- **Case studies** examine leading cases, awards, and events in depth.
- A **practical exercise** — contract drafting, treaty interpretation, regulatory compliance review, simulated arbitration, judicial decision analysis, legislative drafting, environmental liability assessment, or comparative research — develops professional skills. Exercises involving invented parties or facts are expressly labelled **hypothetical**.
- A **summary** recapitulates the chapter's key points.
- **Doctoral research questions** identify open questions suitable for advanced research.
- **Further reading** directs the reader to authoritative scholarship.

## A Note on Currency and Verification

Energy and resource law changes rapidly. The law is stated as understood at the time of writing in 2026. Recent developments — pending legislation, ongoing litigation, arbitral proceedings, and evolving international negotiations — have been described with appropriate caution, and the reader should verify the current status of every authority before relying on it. The book adopts British spelling and, for legal citation, conventions broadly consistent with the Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities (OSCOLA).

## A Note on Neutrality

Energy and resource law raises questions on which reasonable people disagree: the pace of the transition away from fossil fuels, the balance between investor protection and the right to regulate, the role of the state in resource industries, and the obligations of companies towards communities. The book seeks to present competing positions fairly and to ground its analysis in law and evidence, while not shrinking from evaluation where the authorities and evidence support it. Readers are encouraged to form their own judgements and to test them against the materials presented.
