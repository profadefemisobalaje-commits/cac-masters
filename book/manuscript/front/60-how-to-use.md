# How to Use This Book {.front}

## Intended Readers

The book is written for several audiences, each of whom may use it differently.

- **Doctoral candidates and advanced postgraduate students** may use it as a comprehensive foundation for their studies, as a guide to the literature and authorities, and as a source of research questions. Part VIII and Appendices N, O, P, and T are designed specifically to support the design, conduct, and writing of doctoral research.
- **Academics and teachers** may use individual chapters or Parts as the basis for advanced courses and seminars, and the practical exercises as the basis for assessments, simulations, and moot proceedings.
- **Practitioners** — lawyers, in-house counsel, and advisers — may use it as a reference work on the legal frameworks, contractual structures, and dispute resolution mechanisms of the field, and the appendices as checklists and starting points for drafting.
- **Regulators, policymakers, and legislators** may use it to understand comparative approaches to regulation and law reform, particularly through Chapters 4, 9, 24, and 29 and Appendices J and M.
- **Arbitrators and judges** may use it as a guide to the substantive law and procedural practice of energy disputes, particularly through Chapters 14, 15, 26, and 27 and Appendices I and Q.

## Features of Each Chapter

**Learning objectives** at the start of each chapter identify what the reader should be able to do after studying it. Readers may find it helpful to return to them after completing the chapter as a check on their understanding.

**Numbered sections** (for example, 14.3) allow precise cross-referencing. Each chapter has eight sections.

**Figures and tables** summarise frameworks, processes, and comparisons. They are listed in the List of Figures and are numbered by chapter.

**Case studies**, set apart in shaded boxes, examine leading cases, awards, and events in depth. Readers are encouraged to read the primary sources — the judgments and awards themselves — alongside the case studies.

**Comparative and practice boxes** highlight the experience of particular jurisdictions and points of professional practice.

**Practical exercises** develop professional skills. They vary by subject: contract drafting, treaty interpretation, regulatory compliance reviews, simulated arbitration proceedings, judicial decision analysis, legislative drafting, environmental liability assessments, and comparative legal research. Most exercises use invented parties and facts, and **every such exercise is labelled hypothetical**. Exercises may be completed individually or in groups; many are suitable for role-play and moot formats.

**Summaries** recapitulate the chapter's key points and may be used for revision.

**Doctoral research questions** identify open questions suitable for advanced research. They are intended as starting points: a viable doctoral question must be refined through a review of the literature, consideration of method and sources, and discussion with supervisors (see Chapter 28 and Appendix N).

**Further reading** lists direct the reader to authoritative scholarship and primary sources. Where an edition may have been updated since writing, readers should consult the latest edition.

## Reference Apparatus

The front matter contains tables of treaties and conventions, cases and arbitral awards, and statutes and regulatory instruments, so that readers can locate the treatment of particular authorities; to avoid duplication, these tables are not repeated at the end of the book. The back matter contains the bibliography and indexes of legal concepts, of institutions and organisations, and a subject index.

## Citation and Verification

Citations broadly follow the Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities (OSCOLA). The law is stated as understood in 2026. Because energy and resource law changes rapidly, **readers must verify the current status of every authority before relying on it** — checking for amendments, repeals, appeals, annulments, and subsequent developments — using official sources and reliable databases. Appendix P offers guidance on legal research and verification.

## Important Notices

**This book does not confer a doctorate or any other academic qualification.** It is a scholarly resource designed to support advanced study and research. Doctoral standing can be achieved only through enrolment in a duly authorised programme, supervised original research, and successful examination in accordance with the rules of the awarding institution.

**This book is not legal advice.** It analyses law and policy for purposes of education and research. Readers facing a specific legal question should consult a qualified practitioner in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Hypothetical material is fictitious.** Invented states, companies, communities, and disputes used in exercises do not describe real events, and any resemblance to actual persons, companies, or disputes is coincidental.
