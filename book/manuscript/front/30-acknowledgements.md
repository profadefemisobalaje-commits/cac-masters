# Acknowledgements {.front}

A work of this scope depends on the contributions of many people and institutions, and it is a pleasure to acknowledge them.

We are grateful, first, to the scholars of energy and natural resources law, international law, environmental law, and international economic law whose research forms the foundation of this book. The further reading lists at the end of each chapter and the bibliography record only part of this debt. The work of the international organisations, courts, tribunals, regulators, and research institutes that publish their decisions, reports, data, and analyses openly has also been indispensable; open access to legal materials is a public good on which the study of law depends.

We thank the faculty, research staff, and graduate students of Triune Biblical University Global Extension USA whose discussions, comments, and questions helped shape the structure and content of the book, and whose engagement with earlier versions of its materials improved its clarity and usefulness. We are grateful to the practitioners, regulators, and arbitrators who shared their insights into how energy and resource law operates in practice, and to the colleagues in Nigeria, elsewhere in Africa, and around the world who drew our attention to developments in their jurisdictions.

We acknowledge the editorial, design, and production work that made the book possible: the preparation of the figures and diagrams, the typesetting, the compilation of the tables and indexes, and the careful checking of references.

Finally, we acknowledge the communities living alongside energy and resource projects, whose experiences — of benefit and of harm — give the law examined in this book its human significance, and whose voices have increasingly and rightly shaped its development.

Responsibility for any errors or omissions remains with the author. Readers who identify errors or wish to suggest improvements for future editions are warmly invited to communicate them to the publisher.
