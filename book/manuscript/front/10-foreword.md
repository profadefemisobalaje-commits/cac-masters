# Foreword {.front}

Few fields of law touch the lives of so many people as directly as the law governing energy and natural resources. The electricity that lights a classroom, the fuel that moves goods to market, the minerals inside the telephone in a student's pocket, the gas flared at a wellhead, and the revenues that fund — or fail to fund — schools and hospitals are all shaped by legal rules: by constitutions and statutes, by treaties and contracts, by the decisions of regulators, courts, and arbitral tribunals. Yet the field has long been studied in fragments. Petroleum law, mining law, electricity regulation, environmental law, investment law, and human rights law have often been taught in separate courses and written about in separate literatures, even though, in practice, every significant energy or resource project engages all of them at once.

This book brings these fragments together. It offers an integrated, advanced treatment of international energy and natural resources law, written for those who aspire to master the field at the highest level — doctoral candidates, advanced postgraduate students, academics, practitioners, regulators, judges, arbitrators, and policymakers. Its thirty chapters move from the jurisprudential and institutional foundations of the field through the law of petroleum, gas, and mining; electricity, renewable energy, and emerging technologies; international trade, investment, and contracts; environmental and climate law; human rights and resource justice; comparative law, geopolitics, and dispute resolution; and, finally, the methods of doctoral scholarship, the craft of law reform, and the future of the discipline.

Three features of the work deserve particular mention. First, it is rigorously grounded in primary authority. Throughout, the reader is directed to the treaties, statutes, cases, and awards that constitute the law, and is encouraged to verify every proposition against its source. Second, it is genuinely comparative and international while remaining attentive to Africa, and to Nigeria in particular. The Petroleum Industry Act 2021, the Electricity Act 2023, the Climate Change Act 2021, and the regional frameworks of ECOWAS, the West African Power Pool, and the African Continental Free Trade Area are examined alongside the law of the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, the Middle East, Asia, and Latin America. Third, it is designed for active learning. Each chapter contains learning objectives, case studies, practical exercises — contract drafting, treaty interpretation, regulatory compliance reviews, simulated arbitrations, judicial decision analysis, and legislative drafting — and doctoral research questions. The hypothetical exercises are clearly identified as such, so that readers may practise the skills of the profession without confusing simulation with reality.

The book appears at a moment of profound transformation. The energy transition, the race for critical minerals, the digitalisation of energy systems, the recognition of climate change as a legal and financial risk, the growing insistence on the rights of communities, and the fragmentation of the international order are reshaping every part of the field. Those who master its law will be called upon to help societies navigate these changes wisely and fairly. It is our hope that this book will help prepare them for that responsibility.

We commend this work to its readers with confidence, and with the reminder that it is offered as a scholarly resource. It does not itself confer any academic qualification, and it is not legal advice. Its value will lie in the use its readers make of it — in the rigour of their research, the integrity of their practice, and the quality of the laws and institutions they help to build.
