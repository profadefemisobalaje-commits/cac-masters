## 1. Course Aims

1. To give postgraduate students a coherent, research-informed **orientation to counseling psychology** as a distinct specialty within psychology and the wider helping professions.
2. To trace the **historical, philosophical and socio-cultural development** of the field globally and within Nigeria/Africa.
3. To survey the major **theoretical orientations** and to build the student's capacity for theory-informed **case conceptualisation**.
4. To introduce the foundational **counseling process, microskills and the therapeutic relationship**.
5. To develop awareness of **assessment, prevention, diversity, ethics, law, supervision and technology** in professional practice.
6. To prepare the student for **practicum, internship, research and the subsequent courses** in the programme.

## 2. Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

By the end of CPY 701, the student should be able to:

| CLO | Outcome | Bloom level | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLO 1 | Define counseling psychology and distinguish it from guidance, psychotherapy, clinical psychology and allied helping professions | Understand / Analyse | Weeks 1, 3 · Test |
| CLO 2 | Trace the historical and philosophical development of the field, including its emergence in Nigeria | Understand | Week 2 · Test |
| CLO 3 | Compare the major theoretical orientations and justify a formulation-driven choice of approach | Analyse / Evaluate | Weeks 4–5 · Assignment |
| CLO 4 | Demonstrate the core counseling microskills and structure a counseling session across its stages | Apply | Week 6 · Skills lab |
| CLO 5 | Conduct a culturally responsive intake, mental-status screening and case formulation | Apply / Analyse | Week 9 · Case report |
| CLO 6 | Design a prevention or psychoeducation intervention for a specified population | Create | Week 10 · Group project |
| CLO 7 | Apply ethical and legal decision-making models to practice dilemmas | Apply / Evaluate | Week 13 · Ethics portfolio |
| CLO 8 | Appraise counseling research, and use technology and supervision responsibly | Evaluate | Week 14 · Seminar paper |
| CLO 9 | Produce a personal professional-development and self-care plan | Create | Week 15 · Portfolio |
| CLO 10 | Integrate theory, skills, ethics and evidence into a coherent professional identity | Synthesise | Week 16 · Examination |

# 5. Week-by-Week Detail Sheets

### Week 0 · Orientation — Welcome to CPY 701
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W00` · ~6 min · 8 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** Locate the course within the Master's programme; understand the assessment scheme; know how to use the video series (notebook method, skills lab, seminar).
- **Coverage:** title & course identity · aims · CLOs · assessment weights · 16-week roadmap · study expectations · academic integrity.
- **On-screen artefacts:** 16-week roadmap graphic; assessment donut chart.

### Week 1 · Nature & Concept of Counseling Psychology
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W01` · ~12 min · 16 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Define counseling psychology from at least three authoritative sources; (2) differentiate guidance, counseling, psychotherapy and clinical psychology; (3) explain the specialty's core values and scope of practice; (4) describe the scientist–practitioner (and scientist–practitioner–advocate) model.
- **Coverage:** etymology and working definitions; APA Division 17 / Society of Counseling Psychology perspective; helping vs curing; the wellness, strengths, developmental and prevention emphases; client populations and settings; scope and limits of practice; counseling as art and science; the person of the counsellor previewed.
- **On-screen artefacts:** definition comparison matrix; "guidance ↔ counseling ↔ psychotherapy ↔ therapy" continuum diagram; Venn of overlapping helping roles.
- **Applied task:** Write your own 60-word working definition and defend it in one paragraph.
- **Key readings:** Gladding, *Counseling: A Comprehensive Profession*; Corey, *Theory and Practice of Counseling and Psychotherapy*; APA Division 17 resources.

### Week 2 · Historical & Philosophical Development
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W02` · ~13 min · 18 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Construct a timeline of the field from vocational guidance to the present; (2) identify the contribution of key figures; (3) explain how counseling psychology developed in Nigeria and Africa; (4) articulate the philosophical assumptions (humanistic, phenomenological, holistic) that undergird practice.
- **Coverage:** Frank Parsons and vocational guidance (1908); Clifford Beers and the mental-hygiene movement; the impact of WWI/WWII testing, veteran rehabilitation and the U.S. Veterans Administration; Carl Rogers' *Counseling and Psychotherapy* (1942) and client-centred revolution; formal recognition of the specialty (APA Division 17); the community-mental-health and prevention era; multicultural and social-justice turns. **Nigeria/Africa:** indigenous and communal helping traditions, missionary and colonial schooling, school guidance services post-independence, professional associations, the commencement of graduate training in counseling psychology (1994, University of Nigeria, Nsukka), the Counsellors Registration Council of Nigeria, and the National Mental Health Act 2022. **Philosophy:** determinism vs free will, person-in-environment, holism, development across the lifespan, respect for dignity and cultural plurality.
- **On-screen artefacts:** animated timeline; portrait cards of key figures; Nigeria milestone strip.
- **Key readings:** history-of-counseling chapters in Gladding and Corey; journal accounts of the growth of counseling psychology in Nigeria (e.g., *Journal of Professional Counselling and Psychotherapy Research*, 2022).

### Week 3 · Relationship to Other Disciplines & Helping Professions
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W03` · ~11 min · 15 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Contrast counseling psychology with clinical psychology, psychiatry, social work, nursing, religious/pastoral care and traditional healing; (2) identify the unique contributions of the specialty; (3) map a referral network and explain the ethics of referral.
- **Coverage:** training models and licensure differences; medical vs psychosocial frames; the counseling psychologist's emphasis on strengths, normal developmental challenges, brief and focal intervention, career/vocational concerns and multicultural competence; collaboration in multidisciplinary teams; task-shifting and community mental health in low-resource settings; working with traditional and faith healers; referral, counter-referral and boundary management.
- **On-screen artefacts:** professions comparison table (training, focus, methods, prescribing rights, settings); referral-pathway flow diagram.
- **Applied task:** Draw the referral map for a client presenting with depression + unemployment + family conflict in your local government area.

### Week 4 · Theoretical Orientations I — Psychodynamic, Humanistic & Existential
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W04` · ~14 min · 18 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Explain the key concepts, goals and techniques of each orientation; (2) evaluate their strengths, limitations and cultural fit; (3) begin a theory-informed case conceptualisation.
- **Coverage:** classical psychoanalysis and contemporary psychodynamic/object-relations thinking (unconscious processes, defence mechanisms, transference, attachment); Adlerian psychology (lifestyle, inferiority/striving, social interest, family constellation); Jungian notes; **person-centred therapy** — the three core conditions (congruence, unconditional positive regard, empathic understanding), non-directiveness, and its influence on all later practice; Gestalt (awareness, here-and-now, unfinished business, empty chair); existential (four ultimate concerns: death, freedom, isolation, meaninglessness; Frankl's logotherapy).
- **On-screen artefacts:** orientation profile cards (assumption of human nature · goals · techniques · evidence · cultural fit); "theory → conceptualisation → intervention" chain diagram.
- **Applied task:** Conceptualise a single vignette from two of the week's orientations and compare.

### Week 5 · Theoretical Orientations II — CBT, Constructivist, Systemic & Third-Wave
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W05` · ~14 min · 18 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Compare behavioural, cognitive, constructivist, systemic and third-wave approaches; (2) justify an integrative or eclectic stance with reasons rather than convenience; (3) summarise what common-factors research implies for practice.
- **Coverage:** behavioural foundations (classical/operant/social learning; systematic desensitisation, exposure, behaviour activation); REBT (Ellis — ABC model, irrational beliefs, disputation); cognitive therapy (Beck — automatic thoughts, schemas, cognitive triad, distortions, thought records); reality therapy/choice theory (Glasser — WDEP); **solution-focused brief therapy** (miracle question, scaling, exceptions); **narrative therapy** (externalisation, unique outcomes, re-authoring); **family systems** (Bowen — differentiation, triangulation, multigenerational; Minuchin — structure and boundaries; Satir — communication stances); third wave: DBT, ACT, mindfulness-based approaches, compassion-focused therapy; positive psychology (PERMA, character strengths). **Common factors:** alliance, empathy, expectations, client factors — Wampold's contextual model; integration vs eclecticism.
- **On-screen artefacts:** technique-to-theory matching grid; ABC thought-record worked example; systems circular-causality diagram.

### Week 6 · The Counseling Process & Core Microskills
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W06` · ~15 min · 20 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Sequence the stages of the counseling process; (2) demonstrate the microskills hierarchy in simulated practice; (3) structure, document and evaluate a single session.
- **Coverage:** stage model — relationship building → assessment and problem definition → goal setting → intervention/working → termination, consolidation and follow-up; Egan's skilled-helper three-stage model as an alternative frame; **microskills:** attending behaviour and SOLER, physical and psychological listening, observation of non-verbal cues, open/closed questioning, encouragement, paraphrasing, reflection of feeling and of meaning, summarising, concreteness, empathy (accurate and advanced), immediacy, self-disclosure, focusing, confrontation/challenge, influencing skills (interpretation, information-giving, directive, advice — and when to avoid advice); intentional interviewing; the microskills hierarchy; session structure and time management; verbatim vs process notes; deliberate practice, self- and peer-rating scales; common beginner errors (interrogating, reassuring too fast, problem-solving prematurely, "why" questions, over-talking).
- **On-screen artefacts:** session-stage flow; microskills ladder; good/poor response contrast panels; a 90-second model transcript.
- **Applied task:** Record a 10-minute role-play using five named microskills; complete a self-rating sheet.

### Week 7 · The Counseling Relationship, Diversity & Multicultural Counseling
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W07` · ~14 min · 18 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Explain and operationalise the therapeutic alliance; (2) recognise and manage transference, countertransference, resistance and rupture; (3) apply the awareness–knowledge–skills model of multicultural competence; (4) adapt practice to African/Nigerian contexts.
- **Coverage:** Bordin's pantheoretical alliance (bond, goals, tasks) and alliance as the best-evidenced predictor; rupture and repair; empathy, warmth, genuineness in practice; boundaries, power and the professional use of self; transference/countertransference and the counsellor's triggers; resistance and ambivalence (motivational interviewing spirit); **multicultural and social-justice counseling** — Sue & Sue's ARIC model, cultural identity development, acculturation, worldview differences, culture-bound syndromes, microaggressions, advocacy and the multicultural-social-justice counselling competence quadrants; **indigenous/African considerations** — communalism and extended-family involvement, respect for elders, spirituality and religious coping, stigma and help-seeking delay, somatisation, language and interpretation, collectivist goal-setting, decolonising assessment and diagnosis, gender norms, working with persons with disabilities, refugees/IDPs, and clients in conflict-affected settings; legal and cultural sensitivity where topics are contested.
- **On-screen artefacts:** alliance triad diagram; ARIC competence matrix; help-seeking pathway in a Nigerian community (formal vs informal).
- **Applied task:** Cultural formulation of one case — what must change in your usual approach, and why?

### Week 8 · Mid-Semester Assessment & Consolidation Seminar
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W08` · ~10 min · 12 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Integrate Weeks 1–7 into one conceptual map; (2) attempt a complete case conceptualisation under guidance; (3) diagnose personal learning gaps.
- **Coverage:** revision architecture for Modules A and B; a fully worked case conceptualisation (presenting problem → history → formulation → chosen orientation → goals → intervention plan → evaluation → ethical notes); test format, marking rubric and worked sample answers; seminar preparation and presentation skills; feedback loop.
- **On-screen artefacts:** concept map of Weeks 1–7; annotated model answer; self-check gap-analysis sheet.
- **Assessment:** Continuous assessment test (15%).

### Week 9 · Assessment, Diagnosis & Case Formulation
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W09` · ~14 min · 18 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Conduct a structured intake interview; (2) perform a mental-status examination and screen for risk; (3) use classification systems critically; (4) write a formulation and a professional report.
- **Coverage:** purposes and limits of assessment in counseling (vs forensic/medical assessment); intake and history-taking (presenting problem, precipitants, background, medical/psychiatric/substance history, family, education/work, spiritual/cultural, strengths and supports); mental-status examination domains; **ICD-11 and DSM-5-TR** orientation — dimensional vs categorical thinking, comorbidity, the risk of over-pathologising normal distress, cultural formulation interview; psychological testing and the counsellor's role (selection, administration conditions, interpretation limits, feedback, competence); non-standardised tools (genogram, ecomap, lifeline, scaling); **risk assessment** — suicide and self-harm (ideation, plan, means, intent, history, protective factors), homicide/violence, abuse and neglect, duty to warn and protect, emergency referral; formulation models (5 Ps: presenting, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating, protective; psychodynamic, CBT and systemic formulations); documentation standards and report writing.
- **On-screen artefacts:** MSE checklist card; 5-P formulation template; suicide-risk decision tree; sample report skeleton.
- **Assessment:** Case conceptualisation & formulation report (10%).

### Week 10 · Prevention, Development & Psychoeducation
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W10` · ~12 min · 16 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Distinguish levels of prevention; (2) design a developmentally appropriate psychoeducation intervention; (3) plan needs assessment, delivery and evaluation for a target population.
- **Coverage:** public-health framing — primary, secondary and tertiary prevention; universal, selective and indicated programmes; developmental/lifespan counseling (infancy and childhood, adolescence and youth, emerging adulthood, midlife transitions, older adults and retirement, end-of-life); developmental tasks and crises (Erikson); wellness, resilience and protective factors; strengths-based and positive-psychology interventions; psychoeducation design (audience analysis, objectives, methods, materials, plain language, local examples); group and school-based prevention (bullying, examination malpractice, substance use, teenage pregnancy, study skills, career readiness); community outreach, advocacy and social-justice action; programme planning cycle — needs assessment → logic model → implementation → process and outcome evaluation → sustainability; working with stakeholders (schools, churches/mosques, NGOs, ministries).
- **On-screen artefacts:** prevention pyramid; programme planning cycle; logic-model template.
- **Assessment:** Group prevention project (10%) — introduced here, presented Weeks 10–11.

### Week 11 · Counseling Across Specialization Settings & Populations
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W11` · ~15 min · 20 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Describe the distinctive demands of major practice settings; (2) select an appropriate focus and approach for a given population; (3) identify competence boundaries requiring referral or additional training.
- **Coverage:** **school/educational counseling** (learning difficulties, examination anxiety, guidance curriculum, teacher and parent consultation); **career & vocational counseling** (Parsons' trait-and-factor legacy, Holland's RIASEC, Super's life-span/life-space, Ginzberg, career construction and narrative career counselling, labour-market realities, entrepreneurship and the informal economy); **family, marriage & couples** (communication, conflict, infidelity, blended families, domestic violence — and when couples work is contraindicated); **rehabilitation and disability** (adjustment to disability, vocational rehabilitation, community-based rehabilitation); **health/hospital counseling** (chronic illness, adherence, pain, HIV, oncology, palliative care, liaison work); **trauma, grief & crisis** (bereavement models, complicated grief, PTSD, psychological first aid); **addiction and substance use** (screening, brief intervention, referral to treatment, harm reduction, family involvement, gambling and digital addictions); **workplace/EAP and industrial counseling** (stress, burnout, workplace conflict, harassment); **correctional/forensic**; **pastoral/faith-based**; **gerontology**; **private practice and telepractice** (business, ethics, marketing boundaries). Each segment closes with a short case vignette.
- **On-screen artefacts:** settings matrix (population · typical problems · approach · team · constraints); specialty map.

### Week 12 · Group, Family & Crisis/Emergency Counseling
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W12` · ~13 min · 17 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Explain how groups produce therapeutic change; (2) plan and lead a short psychoeducational or counseling group; (3) execute a structured crisis response and document it.
- **Coverage:** types of groups (psychoeducational, counseling, therapy, task/support, self-help) and when to use each; group development stages (forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning); **Yalom's therapeutic factors** (universality, instillation of hope, altruism, cohesion, interpersonal learning, catharsis, imitative behaviour, existential factors); leadership skills and co-leadership; screening, composition, size, structure and rules; confidentiality in groups; handling difficult members; family counseling foundations — systems thinking, circular causality, homeostasis, boundaries and triangulation, genogram construction, joining and reframing, structural interventions; **crisis and emergency response** — crisis theory, ABC/rapport–problem–alternatives–action models, RAPID psychological first aid, disaster and community mental health, mass-gathering and school emergencies, suicide-risk protocol and safety planning, post-incident support for responders, documentation and mandatory reporting, vicarious trauma after critical incidents.
- **On-screen artefacts:** group-stage wheel; therapeutic-factors cards; crisis protocol flowchart; safety-plan template.

### Week 13 · Ethics, Law & Professional Regulation
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W13` · ~14 min · 18 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) State the core ethical principles and how codes operationalise them; (2) apply a structured ethical decision-making model; (3) identify the legal and regulatory framework for practice in Nigeria.
- **Coverage:** moral foundations — autonomy, non-maleficence, beneficence, justice, fidelity, veracity; **ACA Code of Ethics** (2014) and **NBCC Code of Ethics**; BPS/APA parallels; informed consent (including for minors, groups and telepractice); confidentiality and its limits; records, data protection (Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023) and secure storage; competence and scope; dual/multiple relationships, gifts, barter and boundaries; working with colleagues and impairment; supervision and consultation obligations; advertising, fees, and social-media conduct; **regulation in Nigeria** — Counsellors Registration Council of Nigeria (CRCN), professional associations, the **National Mental Health Act 2022**, child-rights legislation and mandatory reporting, duty to warn/protect, liability and malpractice; ethical decision-making models (Rest's four-component model; Kitchener/Corey step models); technology and e-counseling ethics; a bank of dilemmas with model resolutions (client gifts, religious directive pressure, social-media friend requests, a client who discloses abuse, dual relationships in small communities, confidentiality vs family demand).
- **On-screen artefacts:** six-principles wheel; 8-step decision-making ladder; dilemma-response comparison; compliance checklist.
- **Assessment:** Ethics & professional-issues portfolio (5%).

### Week 14 · Research, Evidence-Based Practice & Technology
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W14` · ~13 min · 17 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Appraise counseling research critically; (2) translate evidence into practice decisions; (3) evaluate the benefits and risks of technology-mediated counseling.
- **Coverage:** the scientist–practitioner identity revisited; research designs relevant to counseling (RCTs, quasi-experimental, single-case/n-of-1, qualitative — phenomenology, grounded theory, narrative; mixed methods); outcome vs process research; what works — effect sizes, common factors, empirically supported treatments and manuals; limits of EBP transportability to African settings and the case for locally generated evidence; measurement-based care (routine outcome monitoring, ORS/SRS, session feedback); critical appraisal checklists; basic statistics literacy for counsellors (significance vs clinical significance, attrition, fidelity); writing and publishing, research ethics and IRB/NHREC; **technology:** teletherapy platforms and modalities, asynchronous text counseling, digital therapeutics and self-help apps, VR exposure, AI chatbots and large language models (capabilities, risks, informed consent, data privacy, de-skilling), social-media phenomena (addiction, cyberbullying, gambling, sexting/selfie culture, misinformation), the digital divide and low-bandwidth practice in Nigeria, competence and ethics for online practice.
- **On-screen artefacts:** evidence pyramid; appraisal checklist card; telepractice readiness matrix; research → practice loop.
- **Assessment:** Seminar paper on evidence-based practice (10%).

### Week 15 · The Counselor as Person & Professional
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W15` · ~12 min · 16 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Assess personal strengths, values and vulnerabilities for counseling work; (2) use supervision effectively; (3) construct a realistic self-care and professional-development plan.
- **Coverage:** the person of the counsellor — values clarification, personality, motives, the "wounded healer", personal therapy and its role; counsellor identity and development models (Rønnestad & Skovholt's phase model; Stoltenberg's Integrative Developmental Model; cognitive-affective maturation); **supervision** — purposes (formative, normative/ethical, restorative), models (developmental, systems, discrimination model: intervention/conceptualisation/personalisation × teaching/feedback roles), how to prepare for and use supervision, live vs recorded vs self-report supervision; **practicum and internship** — expectations, logbooks, hours, evaluation, ethics on placement; occupational hazards — burnout, compassion fatigue, vicarious/secondary traumatic stress, moral injury, imposter phenomenon, isolation in private practice; protective strategies — workload management, boundaries, peer consultation groups, reflective practice and journaling, spirituality, physical health, supervision, continuing professional development, professional associations and mentorship; career pathways — school counselor, health, NGO/community, corporate/EAP, private practice, academia and research, M.Phil/Ph.D. progression; building a professional portfolio and CV.
- **On-screen artefacts:** identity-development curve; supervision triad; burnout self-check; 30-day self-care plan template.
- **Assessment:** Personal professional-development & self-care plan (portfolio).

### Week 16 · Integration, Revision & Course Examination
- **Video module:** `CPY701-W16` · ~12 min · 15 slides
- **Learning outcomes:** (1) Synthesise the course into a single integrated practice model; (2) answer application-level examination questions with structure; (3) plan the next stage of professional training.
- **Coverage:** the grand integration map — person → theory → relationship → skills → assessment → formulation → intervention → prevention → ethics → research → supervision; how to answer "compare and contrast", "apply", and "critically evaluate" questions; marking rubric decoded; three worked past-style questions with model answers; common examination errors; capstone mini case presentations; course evaluation; what comes next — CPY 702 and the wider programme, practicum sequencing, certification and registration pathways; closing charge to the profession.
- **On-screen artefacts:** one-page integration map; question-answering scaffold (PEEL/PEA); rubric table; model answers.
- **Assessment:** Final examination (40%).

# 6. Video Production Plan

## 6.1 Two build modes

| Mode | What it produces | Runtime | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Master orientation video** | One film covering the whole 16-week roadmap, aims, CLOs, assessment and how to study | ~25–30 min | Course launch, LMS front page, orientation week |
| **B — Weekly module series** | 16 self-contained lecture videos (one per week above), plus the Week 0 orientation | ~3 h 36 min total | Full course delivery; uploaded week by week |

## 6.2 Slide and visual system
- **Canvas:** 1920 × 1080, 16:9, consistent safe margins; title slides, section dividers, content slides, diagram slides, quote slides, summary/recap slides, task slides.
- **Typography:** Inter (body/labels) + Poppins (display/headings); minimum on-screen body size 34 px for legibility on mobile.
- **Palette:** deep academic navy, warm gold accent, off-white surface, muted slate for secondary text; one semantic colour each for definition / process / warning / evidence.
- **Recurring furniture:** course code badge (top-left), week + module chip (top-right), progress bar, slide number, lower-third key-term captions.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA contrast, no colour-only meaning, burned-in or sidecar SRT captions, no reliance on animation to convey meaning.

## 6.3 Audio and pacing
- Professional narration at ~140–150 wpm, per-slide scripts written for the ear, not the eye.
- Roughly 40–60 seconds per slide; ~2 s of breathing room between slides.
- Chapters keyed to week sections so learners can jump; optional soft underscore under section dividers only.

## 6.4 Deliverables per module
1. Narrated MP4 (1080p, H.264 + AAC) with chapter markers.
2. Editable slide deck (PPTX) and print-ready PDF.
3. Narration script + SRT captions.
4. Slide images (PNG) for LMS thumbnail use.
