# About This Book {.front}

This volume is the complete lecture course for **CAC. 701 Fundamentals of Counseling Theories**, a First Semester core course of the Master in Children and Adolescent Counseling, carrying 1.6 units. It is written to be taught from and studied from without any other material in the room.

## What the course is for

Every other course in the programme will teach you something to do with a child or an adolescent. This one teaches you why doing it might work, and how you would know whether it did. A counselling theory is not a belief system and not a loyalty; it is a set of answers to four hard questions — what a child is, what maintains the difficulty, by what mechanism change occurs, and what the counsellor therefore does. Answered well, those questions make practice deliberate, teachable and defensible to the parent, the school, the court or the licensing board that asks what you did and why. Answered badly or not at all, practice becomes a series of intuitive moves that cannot be evaluated and cannot be revised when they fail.

The course moves through the four great lineages of counselling theory — psychodynamic and attachment, behavioural and cognitive, humanistic and existential, systemic and family — reading each against the same four questions, and ends where practice actually lives: in the integration of competing accounts into a formulation for one particular child, held to the standard of the available evidence.

## How the semester is organised

Sixteen weeks. Weeks 1 to 14 teach. Week 15 integrates: a comparative practicum in which the whole course is applied to a single extended case. Week 16 consolidates for examination. Each week is one lecture unit of about four notional hours — roughly ninety minutes of scheduled teaching, ninety minutes of seminar or practical work, and the remainder your own reading and writing. Each week has the same shape, and the shape is deliberate: outcomes you can be examined on; the place of the week in the argument; four key concepts each tied to something a counsellor does in a room; three lectures built from propositions rather than narrative; at least one diagram placed where the argument needs one; worked examples; a practical with a debrief; seminar questions; the assessed task and its criteria; the reading, with the core text marked; and one sentence of synthesis.

## A note on the child

The material is addressed throughout to work with children and adolescents, and that is not a decoration on an adult curriculum. A child cannot free-associate, may lack the vocabulary for a cognitive dispute, does not choose to attend, and is embedded in a family and a school that are themselves part of the problem and part of the solution. Theories built for adults fail in particular, predictable ways in a room with a nine-year-old. One purpose of this course is to teach you to see the failure coming.

## Standards observed in this volume

British spelling in the book's own prose; American spelling preserved in quoted titles, journal names and direct quotations. Citations in APA 7th edition; only works actually cited are listed; no quotation, statistic or page number has been invented. All case material is fictitious and labelled as such; photographs generated for teaching are captioned as such; standardised instruments are described and attributed but never reproduced.
