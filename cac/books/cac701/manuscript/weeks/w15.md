# The Comparative Practicum: One Case, Four Formulations, One Recommendation {.unit num="15" week="15" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Produce** four complete formulations of one extended case, each internally coherent, using the vocabulary of a different lineage without borrowing its rivals' concepts.
2. **Compare** the four on mechanism, not technique, and state what each reading contributes, forbids, and gets wrong.
3. **Recommend** one governing account with an eight-week sequence and a dated falsifier, defending the choice in the face of objection.
4. **Present and defend** the work to a panel, including the sentence "I do not know" where it belongs.
5. **Diagnose** the five common failure modes of comparative work and repair a specimen of each.
:::

### Where this week sits

This is the course's capstone practicum. Everything in weeks 2 to 14 is used on one child: Daniel, who arrived in week 1 aged nine with eleven days of absence, returned in week 5 as an escape-reinforced morning, in week 10 as a node in an enmeshed dyad with a disengaged father, and in week 13 as a five-P formulation with a falsifier. In this week's block you complete the comparison, make the recommendation, and defend it under interrogation. The output is assessed; the process is what the examination of week 16 will test.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=Mechanism_comparison}
Comparison at the level of why change would occur, not at the level of what is done. Two lineages may use the same technique for opposite mechanisms; the technique comparison is therefore nearly always uninformative. In the room: the question "what is doing the work, and how would we know?"
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Internal_coherence}
The requirement that each formulation uses only its own lineage's constructs — no attachment language inside a behavioural account, no "core belief" inside a systemic one. In the room: the discipline that makes comparison possible; a formulation built from every lineage's vocabulary cannot be compared to anything, including itself.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Contribution_and_prohibition}
For each reading, what it adds that the others cannot see, and what it would forbid that the others require. In the room: the honest form of integration, and the shortest route to identifying a cancelling pair (week 13).
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Defensible_recommendation}
A single governing account with a dated sequence, a stated falsifier, and a written answer to the strongest objection from each rival reading. In the room: the difference between a preference and a clinical decision.
:::

### Lecture I — The task, the standards, and the panel

**The task.** On the extended case supplied (Daniel, with the week 10 family-session transcript and the week 14 five-session outcome chart appended), produce: (i) four formulations of 250 words each; (ii) one comparison grid of the kind in Figure 15.1; (iii) a recommendation with an eight-week sequence and falsifier; (iv) a ninety-second family presentation (week 13). Present (iii) and (iv) to a panel of two tutors and two peers; answer six minutes of interrogation.

**The standards.** Each formulation must name a mechanism; each must be internally coherent; each must use at least one protective factor; none may exceed its word allowance, which is itself a test of selectivity. The comparison grid is completed in full — every cell — because the empty cell is where the thinking stopped. The recommendation must answer the strongest objection from each rival reading, not the weakest. The panel's interrogation is designed to find the place where the work is thin; its finding it is a success for the panel and information for you.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** Present with the chart on the table and the grid behind you. Panels, like families and courts, trust a practitioner who can show the work; they distrust fluency without artefacts.
:::

### Worked example 15.1 — The model answer, abbreviated

**Task.** Complete the four formulations' mechanism sentences and the recommendation for Daniel, in the form the panel expects.

**Solution.** *Attachment/psychodynamic:* the mechanism is a repeated experience of the caregiver surviving the child's distress without collapse or withdrawal, so that inhibition of the separation signal gives way to signalling and the internal working model of mother-as-sole-regulator loosens; change is evidenced when Daniel protests openly rather than somatising. *Behavioural/cognitive:* the mechanism is extinction of escape-maintained avoidance combined with disconfirmation of the catastrophic prediction, delivered as a graded return with parent contingencies altered; change is evidenced by minutes at the gate, then inside, then in class. *Person-centred/existential:* the mechanism is the child's experience of unconditional positive regard and accurate empathy in a room where nothing is demanded, so that regard no longer depends on being unafraid; change is evidenced when Daniel speaks of the fear in his own words without checking the adult's face. *Systemic/cultural:* the mechanism is altered structure — the parental subsystem taking the morning function from the mother–son dyad, the father positioned inside the plan, the school's coalition with the mother dissolved; change is evidenced by who is in the kitchen at 7.15 a.m.

*Recommendation:* systemic-attachment governing, behavioural delivering. Weeks 1–2 work with the mother's regulation and hold a whole-family enactment; weeks 2–3 place the father in the morning function; weeks 3–6 run the graded return with both parents and the school's welcome fixed; weeks 4–8 add non-directive child sessions whose material feeds the cognitive work. *Falsifier:* if by the end of week 4 the morning is no shorter and the chart has not moved below the pre-treatment mean by a reliable margin, the maintenance hypothesis is wrong; return to the family map rather than intensify exposure. *Objections answered:* the behavioural reading objects that weeks 1–2 delay the effective ingredient (fair: it is why the graded return starts by week 3 and not week 6); the person-centred reading objects that the contingency plan instrumentalises the child (fair: it is why his own room, with no demands, opens in parallel and why he is told the plan in his own words); the attachment reading objects that the father's positioning may be beyond this family's current capacity (fair: it is the first thing the enactment tests, and the first thing the falsifier will detect).

### Lecture II — Five failure modes, and their repairs

**1. Technique salad.** Techniques from four lineages listed with no mechanism and no order: "play, exposure, scaling questions, reframing". Repair: for every technique, one sentence naming what it changes and how you would know. If you cannot, delete it.

**2. Lineage loyalty.** The comparison is arranged so that the practitioner's own lineage wins, and the rivals are described in their weakest versions. Repair: write each rival reading in its strongest, most charitable form — the version its best advocate would present — and then choose. A choice made against straw is not a choice.

**3. False neutrality.** "All four have merit; a balanced approach is indicated." Repair: name a governing lineage for this week and say what the others contribute and forbid. Neutrality is abdication with good manners; the child gets one plan, not four.

**4. Unanswerable hedging.** Every claim qualified until nothing is claimed, and no falsifier offered. Repair: one dated falsifier, observable, in the family's hearing. A formulation that cannot be wrong cannot be improved.

**5. The child absent.** Four paragraphs of professional machinery and no sentence in Daniel's own words, no drawing, no statement of what he wants. Repair: begin the presentation with the child's own account, quoted, and let it constrain the plan. Week 12's cultural humility applies to age as much as to ethnicity: the child is not merely the object of the comparison.

::: safeguard
A comparative exercise is not a licence to compare a safeguarding threshold. Where a formulation's reading of the case touches possible harm to the child, the safeguarding obligation of week 1 governs and overrides lineage preference: no systemic, cultural or existential reading may be used to defer, soften or relocate responsibility for harm to a child.
:::

### Lecture III — Under interrogation: what the panel is looking for

The panel asks six kinds of question, and each has a legitimate answer.

*Why this lineage, this week?* — a mechanism answer, with the maintaining factor named. *"Because the mother is the sole regulator and every other move is delivered into an unheld child."*

*What would change your mind?* — the dated falsifier. If you have none, you have none; say so and construct one in the room.

*What does this cost the family?* — time, money, dignity, disclosure, travel, school reputation. An unasked cost is a hidden one and usually the reason for dropout.

*Where is the child's voice?* — a quoted sentence and what it changed in your plan.

*What is the cultural position from which you are recommending this?* — your own, stated (week 12). A recommendation delivered from nowhere is delivered from somewhere unexamined.

*What do you not know?* — the honest gap, named without embarrassment. Panels record the practitioner who says "I do not know, and here is how I would find out" as more trustworthy than the one who fills every silence.

::: skills
**Practical 15 — the practicum, run as a panel.** In fives, over the full block: two panellists from the cohort plus two tutors; presenter has five minutes on the recommendation and ninety seconds on the family presentation, then six minutes of interrogation using the six question types above; the fifth member records which of the five failure modes appeared, in the presentation or in the panel's own questions. Every student presents; every student panels twice; the record is shared at the end of the block, and the cohort's three most common failure modes are written on the board and carried into week 16's revision.
:::

::: assessment
**Assessed task.** The practicum portfolio: four formulations (250 words each), completed comparison grid, recommendation with sequence and dated falsifier, objections answered, ninety-second family presentation (recorded), and a 300-word reflection naming your own dominant failure mode and its repair.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Each formulation internally coherent, mechanism named, protective factor used | 30% |
| Comparison completed in full, at mechanism level, rivals in strongest form | 20% |
| Recommendation defensible: sequence, falsifier, objections answered | 25% |
| Presentation: child's voice, cost to family, positionality stated | 15% |
| Reflection: failure mode identified and repaired | 10% |

**Formative.** Bring your comparison grid, with one cell deliberately left empty, to the tutorial; the tutor interrogates only that cell.
:::

::: summary
**Synthesis.** Comparative practice is not a display of knowledge but a decision procedure: four coherent readings, compared at mechanism level, resolved into one governing account with a dated falsifier, presented in the child's words, defended against the strongest objections, and honest about what it does not know.
:::

### Seminar questions

1. Is lineage loyalty ever defensible — for instance, where a practitioner's competence lies in one model? Where is the line between competence and loyalty?
2. Which of the five failure modes is most common in your cohort, and what in the training produces it?
3. What does the child gain, concretely, from a comparative exercise, as against a single competent formulation?
4. Why must the strongest objection be answered rather than the weakest? Give an example where the difference changed a plan.
5. A panel member asks a question you cannot answer and the room waits. What do you do, in what words?
