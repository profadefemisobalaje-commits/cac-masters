# Clinical Onsets and the Adolescent Formulation {.unit num="13" week="13" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Describe** the onset epidemiology of mental disorders — the cumulative onset curve, comorbidity and heterotypic continuity — and state why adolescent services are prevention.
2. **Construct** a one-page adolescent formulation in five parts: presenting, developmental history and timing, body and brain state, maintaining mechanisms, context and culture.
3. **Distinguish** normative adolescent intensity from clinical presentation using pattern, grip, function and impairment rather than symptom presence.
4. **Recognise** the red flags that move a case to medical or statutory pathways, and execute the referral with accompaniment.
5. **Write** formulations that are falsifiable: each with a dated test that could prove it wrong.
:::

### Where this week sits

The developmental half of the semester built instruments; this week begins using them clinically. Most lifetime mental disorder begins in adolescence and young adulthood, which makes the adolescent counsellor the earliest pair of trained eyes most cases will meet — and makes the formulation, not the label, the unit of work. This week teaches the one-page formulation the rest of the programme assumes, the differential discipline between weather and climate, and the red-flag list whose absence is the commonest serious error in adolescent practice.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=Onset_curve}
The cumulative distribution of first episodes: roughly half of lifetime disorder onset by the mid-teens and three quarters by the mid-twenties, with adolescence the steepest segment of the curve (after Kessler et al., 2005). In the room: the sentence that makes every adolescent contact a prevention opportunity — and the reason "wait and see" is a decision with a cost.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Heterotypic_continuity}
The pattern by which an underlying vulnerability changes costume across development — the anxious toddler's inhibition, the child's somatic complaints, the adolescent's avoidance and substance, the adult's disorder — so that different ages present different symptoms of the same trajectory. In the room: why the childhood history is not background but the differential.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Formulation_five}
This volume's one-page form: (1) presenting, in the child's and referrer's words; (2) developmental history and timing — the two clocks (week 12) and the transitions; (3) body and brain state — sleep, puberty timing, substances, medical; (4) maintaining mechanisms — the loops, avoidances and contingencies holding the presentation now; (5) context and culture — household economy, social clock, faith, school, peer layer, adversity. In the room: the document that makes the case teachable, supervisable and falsifiable.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Red_flags}
The presentations that end counselling-as-usual and open medical or statutory pathways: growth faltering or weight loss with restriction; purging; psychosis-type experience (hallucinations, fixed strange beliefs, dramatic functional collapse); manic-type presentation (days of decreased need for sleep with elevated or irritable drive and risk); suicidal intent with plan; substance dependence with withdrawal; and any safeguarding disclosure. In the room: the list is memorised, because red flags do not announce themselves as red flags.
:::

### Lecture I — Onset, comorbidity and the cost of waiting

**Proposition 1. The curve is the argument.** With half of lifetime onset by the mid-teens and three quarters by the mid-twenties, the adolescent service is where prevention is cheapest and treatment is earliest; the median delay of years between onset and first contact — documented across health systems — is lived entirely inside this age band, as disorder consolidating, schooling failing and identities forming around symptoms.

**Proposition 2. Comorbidity is the expectation, not the exception.** Adolescent presentations arrive in bundles — anxiety with depression, depression with substance, conduct with trauma, all with sleep debt (week 3) — and single-diagnosis thinking mis-orders treatment: the bundle's shared maintaining mechanisms (avoidance, rumination, sleep, conflict) are usually the efficient target, and the formulation is the instrument that finds them.

**Proposition 3. Heterotypic continuity makes history diagnostic.** The trajectory changes costume: inhibition becomes somatic complaint becomes avoidance becomes substance; early-onset conduct with childhood adversity is a different pathway from adolescent-limited conduct with peer onset (week 11), and the two require different plans. The ten-minute developmental history — who raised whom, what changed when, what stopped — is therefore assessment, not courtesy.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** Every case file carries the one-page formulation by session three, and every formulation carries a dated falsifier. A formulation without a test is a story; this course grades documents, not stories.
:::

### Lecture II — The one-page formulation, part by part

**Part 1, presenting:** two sentences minimum — the child's own words and the referrer's — because the distance between them is often the case. **Part 2, developmental history and timing:** the two clocks (week 12), the transitions of weeks 2, 6 and 8, the losses and moves, and the childhood costume of the current presentation (heterotypic continuity). **Part 3, body and brain state:** sleep pattern and debt (week 3), puberty timing and its meanings (week 2), substances, menstrual and headache history where relevant, and the medical screen — because thyroid, anaemia and seizure presentations sit in every adolescent differential and are missed by rooms that never ask. **Part 4, maintaining mechanisms:** the loops named precisely — rumination's content, avoidance's list, the household's rankings, the feed's hours, the set's evenings, the sleep's revenge bedtime — each with its short-term payoff and long-term cost, because a mechanism is only a mechanism if it pays something now. **Part 5, context and culture:** household economy and who earns, the social clock and its owners, faith and meaning community, school climate and attendance pattern, peer layer map (week 9), adversity count (week 11) — the page's last part and the first part the room forgets.

**The falsifier** closes the page: one dated observation that would prove the formulation wrong, written in advance — attendance at week six, nights at week two, the solo accepted or declined — chosen so that it can actually fail. The discipline is not pessimism; it is the only mechanism by which a formulation improves rather than thickens.

### Lecture III — Differential discipline and the red-flag list

**Normative intensity versus clinical presentation** is decided by four questions, never by symptom presence: *pattern* — is this a level or a change (week 6)? *grip* — does it organise the day (week 6's body grip, week 7's climate)? *function* — what is the behaviour doing (week 7's self-harm grammar, week 4's heat)? *impairment* — what has stopped: school, friendship, sleep, play? Two of four positive opens the clinical pathway; the weather conversation continues beside it.

**The red flags, memorised and acted on:** restriction with weight loss or growth faltering, and purging — medical review first, this week; psychosis-type experience — the hearing of voices, the fixed strange belief, the collapse of function over weeks — psychiatric referral, stated to the family without the word "madness"; manic-type presentation — days of decreased need for sleep with driving energy, spending, sexual risk — psychiatric referral, because antidepressants and advice both fail it; suicidal intent with plan or means — week 7's pathway immediately; withdrawal phenomena and dependence — medical; and any safeguarding disclosure — the statutory pathway with accompaniment. The counsellor's role at a red flag is not termination but **accompaniment**: the referral is made with the child informed first, the child goes with an adult, and the counselling continues around the medical work, because the referral is a step in the case and not its end.

::: safeguard
Red-flag referral documentation carries: the flag observed and its date; the medical or statutory contact made and to whom; what the child was told and when; the accompaniment arrangement; the continuing counselling plan; and the review date. Where a family refuses a medical referral for a flagged presentation, the refusal is documented, the risk restated in writing to the family, and the service's escalation policy engaged — the child's life governs the room (week 7).
:::

### Worked example 13.1 — Ada's formulation, on one page

**Task.** Write Ada's complete one-page formulation at the week-13 review, with falsifier.

**Solution.** *(1) Presenting:* Ada: "the music stopped sounding like anything, and the nights got loud." Referral (school): "withdrawn, declining grades, choir abandoned." The distance between the sentences — anhedonia named in her own idiom versus a behavioural list — is the case's first datum. *(2) Developmental history and timing:* menarche at eleven and a half, earliest in her class (week 2's off-timing meanings); senior-school transition at fourteen with choir and class separation (week 6's transition); father absent since early childhood, mother migrated for work, auntie's household since age nine — belonging portfolio of two moves; no childhood mental-health contact; childhood costume: the quiet compliant child who sings — inhibition in a valued channel. *(3) Body and brain state:* sleep onset two a.m. with six-hour school nights (week 3's debt, counted at seven per week at intake, two at week 9); puberty development normal; no substances; no medical history, menstrual pain moderate — medical screen otherwise unremarkable, documented. *(4) Maintaining mechanisms:* rumination loop at two a.m. on the looking and the mother's absence (content named, payoff: the loop pretends to solve); avoidance list — choir room, front gate, assembly (payoff: shame avoided today); the comparison feed hour nightly (payoff: village; cost: verdict); knowledge-supply to the auntie stopped (payoff: no questions; cost: no witness). *(5) Context and culture:* household economy — auntie's trade, fees secure this year, SS3 assumption dated (week 10); social clock — school-then-trade, owned by auntie and economy; faith community present and re-entered; school climate — belonging niche restored via choir and one friendship; peer layer — one high-quality friendship, no clique, no romance, no wish yet; adversity count three (parental migration, transition, off-timing puberty) against protections four (auntie, Blessing, choir, faith). *Falsifier, dated:* if at the six-week review the anhedonia sentence persists unchanged despite sleep at midnight-or-earlier on five nights and two choir attendances with the solo, the formulation reorders from situational climate-on-weather to a depressive episode requiring the week 7 clinical pathway and medical review — and the reorder is written, not implied.

### Worked example 13.2 — Kelvin: adolescent-limited or early-onset?

**Task.** Kelvin (16): one arrest (phone snatch, "only held"), evening runs, cannabis "sometimes", repeating SS1, warm-but-stretched household, rankings uncle, set loyalty. Formulate the conduct presentation and choose the pathway.

**Solution.** *(1) Presenting:* Kelvin: "I am not a thief; I held a phone and the whole world ended." Referral (police liaison): "first offence, cooperative, family present." The distance: ownership absent, minimisation present, shame underneath both. *(2) History and timing:* no childhood conduct history — no cruelty, no fire, no theft before fifteen, school reports describe a sociable average child until SS1; the pathway question answers itself provisionally: adolescent-limited (peer-onset, post-transition) rather than early-onset life-course-persistent, which carries childhood onset and adversity accumulation — and the difference is the plan's spine, because the early-onset pathway demands intensive multi-system work while the adolescent-limited pathway demands that the label not become an identity. *(3) Body and brain:* sleep irregular on run nights; cannabis episodic, no withdrawal signs, no dependence features — monitored, documented, not yet a pathway; no red flags on the list. *(4) Maintaining mechanisms:* the set's evening economy (status via nerve, week 4's heat with audience); the rankings' school-identity damage (week 6) making school a place he is read aloud and found small, so offending supplies the only domain where he is not small; minimisation as shame-management, which blocks the ownership the magistrate's diversion programme requires. *(5) Context:* household warm, structure outsourced (week 8); apprenticeship hinge open with Saturdays in the uncle's shop (week 10); PYD conversion running — the under-14s league, four fixtures staffed of six (week 11); protections now five, risks four and falling. *The plan's spine:* adolescent-limited conduct is treated by **identity protection and role supply**, not by confrontation: ownership built through MI (week 14) toward the diversion programme's requirement, with the sentence "I held a phone" becoming "I took a phone and here is what I am doing about it" at his own pace; the league role deepened to under-tens coach next season (contribution with consequences); the school domain re-opened through the trade's warm standards rather than the rankings. *Falsifier, dated:* a second offence of any kind within twelve months, or league attendance falling below half of fixtures, reopens the pathway question toward the early-onset model and triggers multi-system referral — written now, so that the kinder formulation is a hypothesis on probation rather than a verdict in reverse.

::: skills
**Practical 13 — the one-page formulation.** From a supplied case record: write the five-part formulation in one page (a single sheet, timed at 40 minutes in examination conditions), including the two clocks, the medical screen line, the maintaining mechanisms with payoffs, the context part with adversity and protection counts, and one dated falsifier that could actually fail. The mark is for mechanisms with payoffs and a falsifier that bites — a formulation whose test cannot fail is decoration.
:::

::: assessment
**Assessed task.** A clinical case analysis of 1,300 words: the onset-curve argument applied to the case's timing; the five-part formulation complete; the four differential questions (pattern, grip, function, impairment) answered explicitly; the red-flag screen documented with flags present or absent named; the pathway decision (counselling, medical accompaniment, statutory) with its accompaniment steps; and the falsifier with its date.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Formulation five parts complete, one page | 30% |
| Differential questions answered explicitly | 20% |
| Red-flag screen documented | 20% |
| Pathway decision with accompaniment | 15% |
| Falsifier dated and capable of failing | 15% |

**Formative.** Write the red-flag list from memory. Check it. The item you forgot is the item that will arrive at two in the morning.
:::

::: summary
**Synthesis.** The onset curve makes adolescent services prevention: half of lifetime onset by the mid-teens, comorbidity the expectation, and heterotypic continuity making the childhood history part of the differential; the one-page formulation — presenting in two voices, developmental history and the two clocks, body and brain state with the medical screen, maintaining mechanisms with their payoffs, context and culture with adversity and protection counts — is the unit of clinical work, closed by a dated falsifier that can fail; normative intensity versus clinical presentation is decided by pattern, grip, function and impairment rather than symptom presence; and the red-flag list — eating and growth, psychosis-type and manic-type presentations, suicidal intent, dependence, safeguarding — is memorised and acted on with accompaniment, because the referral is a step inside the case and the child goes with an adult, not to one.
:::
