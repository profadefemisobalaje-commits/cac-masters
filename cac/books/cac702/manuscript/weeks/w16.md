# Revision, Integration and Examination {.unit num="16" week="16" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Reconstruct** the volume from memory as one map: four strands, their weeks, and the instruments each strand leaves in your hand.
2. **Retrieve** the volume's load-bearing sentences under examination conditions, using the retrieval schedule supplied.
3. **Answer** each examination verb correctly — define, distinguish, apply, formulate, design — with the structure each requires.
4. **Write** a case answer in the house structure: formulation, plan, falsifier, accompaniment — and mark it against the published rubric.
5. **State** the four habits the semester must leave behind, beyond the examination.
:::

### Where this week sits

The semester closes as it opened: with a map and a question about evidence. This week is revision and examination craft — the four strands reassembled, the retrieval schedule that makes the map retrievable under pressure, the verbs' grammar, and two model answers worked in full against the rubric. Nothing new is taught this week; everything is made examinable, because an instrument you cannot retrieve under examination conditions is an instrument you will not retrieve at two in the morning either.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=Four_strands}
The volume's architecture: (I) the body and the brain — weeks 1–3; (II) the self and its weather — weeks 4–7; (III) relationships and contexts — weeks 8–12; (IV) practice and integration — weeks 13–16. Every examination case draws on at least three strands, and a case answer that uses one strand is a case answer about a stranger. In the room and in the hall: the map is the retrieval cue.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Retrieval_schedule}
The revision method the evidence prefers: recall before re-reading — the strand map from memory first, the book second; the load-bearing sentences written from recall and checked; the diagrams redrawn from memory (a diagram recalled is a mechanism owned); spaced across the week rather than massed in a night. In the hall: the schedule is supplied below; deviating from it is the commonest revision error this programme sees.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Verb_grammar}
Each examination verb has a required structure: *define* — one sentence, the construct and its boundary; *distinguish* — the two constructs with the discriminating test; *apply* — the instrument used on named case material with the output shown; *formulate* — the five parts of week 13 with the falsifier; *design* — the plan with owners, dates and evaluation. Answers are marked against the verb's structure, not their length. In the hall: read the verb twice before writing anything.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=House_case_structure}
The case answer's four movements: formulation (five parts, one page, two clocks, falsifier); plan (sequenced, protections before risks where the count is high, owners and dates named); falsifier and review (what would prove you wrong, and when you will look); accompaniment and limits (who goes with the child, what must travel, where your role ends and the medical or statutory pathway begins). In the hall: the four movements in order, every case, no exceptions.
:::

### Lecture I — The map, reassembled

**Strand I, the body and the brain (weeks 1–3).** Adolescence defined three ways and the gap between them; the invention of the concept and the three corrections to storm and stress; puberty's axis and its secular trend, off-timing's meanings; the remaking brain — pruning, myelination, the dual systems and their critics, sleep's phase delay. *The instruments:* the two clocks; the timing questions; the sleep audit; the heat-and-weather distinction.

**Strand II, the self and its weather (weeks 4–7).** Formal operations and the imaginary audience and personal fable; peer-accelerated risk and the divergence-point method; identity's statuses and moratoriums and the minority-stress sentence; esteem's domains and dip and the comparison machine; the internalising gap, rumination, self-harm's grammar, the direct risk question and the safety plan. *The instruments:* the four questions; the status map in three domains; the domain-weight list; the feed audit; the risk conversation and the card.

**Strand III, relationships and contexts (weeks 8–12).** The family's renegotiation, the three autonomies, knowledge-supply, the domain model and the jurisdictional lever, the two dials in the household's idiom; the peer layers, selection and socialisation, co-rumination, romance's sequence and dangers; school climate and the absence differential, work as harness and hazard, the horizon conversation; risk counted both ways, ordinary magic, cumulative risk, turning points, the five Cs; one biology on many clocks, adversity's four presentations, the WEIRD correction and the second grammar. *The instruments:* the peer audit; the attendance chart; the two lists with counts; the horizon's four moves; the two-clock formulation line.

**Strand IV, practice and integration (weeks 13–16).** The onset curve and comorbidity; the one-page formulation; the four differential questions; the red-flag list; the first ten minutes; the contract and its teach-back; Gillick-type competence per decision; MI's spirit, OARS, change talk and the righting reflex; the gradient and the three registers; hot–cold sequencing; rupture and repair; and this week's craft. *The instruments:* the five-part page with its falsifier; the contract in forty seconds; the competence record; the OARS counts; the session plan format; the supervision record.

### Lecture II — Examination craft

**The verbs, with their structures and their common failures.** *Define* fails when the boundary is missing ("adolescence is the teenage years" defines nothing; "the interval between puberty's onset and social adulthood's arrival, whose length is set by both clocks" defines). *Distinguish* fails without the discriminating test ("rumination and worry differ" is assertion; "worry is problem-shaped and future-shaped and stops at solution; rumination is self-shaped and past-shaped and stops at nothing — the test is what ends it" distinguishes). *Apply* fails when the output is not shown ("use Marcia's statuses on Ada" requires the three domains named with statuses and the governing strand, not a paragraph about identity). *Formulate* fails without the falsifier and the context part. *Design* fails without owners and dates — a design with no owner is a wish with headings.

**Time and structure in the hall:** read the whole paper first, five minutes; answer the case last but plan it first (the case is where the marks' structure is strictest and panic costs most); budget by marks, not by interest; leave the final five minutes for the falsifier line and the accompaniment line, because those two lines are where examiners look first and students drop last.

**The retrieval schedule for this volume, one week:** day 1 — strand I map from memory, then check; the three corrections to storm and stress written verbatim from recall. Day 2 — strand II map; the risk conversation's four assessments and the safety plan's five lines, written from recall and checked. Day 3 — strand III map; the jurisdictional lever and the horizon's four moves aloud. Day 4 — strand IV map; the contract in forty seconds, timed, twice; the red-flag list from memory. Day 5 — two diagrams redrawn from memory (the dual systems and the confidentiality circle), then checked. Day 6 — one full case answer under timed conditions against the rubric below. Day 7 — rest, and one walk through the map in your head, strand by strand, without the book.

### Lecture III — Model answers, and the habits beyond the hall

**Model answer A (formulate; 25 marks), abridged to its structure.** *Case:* Bala, 14, JSS2, brought by his mother after the floods: anger at home, sleeping in the day, refusal of the temporary school, one fight. *Formulation, five parts:* presenting in two voices (Bala: "the water took the house and you people want me to wear a uniform"; mother: "he is becoming a stranger"); history and timing — displacement at thirteen and a half (the social clock broken mid-transition, week 12), father deceased two years prior (the loss beneath the loss), no prior contact; body and brain — day-night inversion (phase delay plus no dawn obligation in the camp, week 3), no substances reported, medical screen asked and unremarkable; maintaining mechanisms — the day-sleep protects him from the camp's daytime idleness and from the temporary school where he is "the flood boy" (belonging wound, week 6), the anger supplies the only register in which the household hears him (function, week 7's mask); context — camp economy, mother's trading hours, the peer layer scattered (week 9's losses across every layer at once), faith community intact but unattended, adversity count six, protections two. *Falsifier:* if the uniform refusal persists at four weeks despite the belonging niche and the sleep re-anchoring, grief work (the father) opens as the governing strand rather than the displacement. *Plan, sequenced:* protections first — the sleep anchor (dawn task at the mother's stall, week 3), one adult in the temporary school named (week 10's lever), the faith community's youth group as the belonging bridge (week 11's setting); then the risks — the fight addressed through the ward's youth rota with contribution (the flood-drain team, supervised, week 11's safeguard note honoured); the grief conversation scheduled, not forced, with the father's name said aloud once by the practitioner so that the room permits it. *Accompaniment and limits:* mother informed of attendance and plan with Bala present and speaking first; no red flags present on the list, documented; review at four weeks with the falsifier read aloud from the file.

**Model answer B (design; 20 marks), structure only.** *Task:* design a school-based group for SS1 students with rising absence. *Design against the five PYD features:* sustained (a term, weekly, same room); adult relationship (the games master, trained one afternoon, supervised monthly); skill with standards (the under-14s league's administration — fixtures, ledger, kit, with the league's own rules as standards); contribution with consequences (the juniors' season depends on the group's work — real, checkable, public); cross-setting alignment (the attendance officer feeds the group's fixture list into the absence follow-up, so attendance becomes the route to the fixture rather than the enemy of it). *Owners and dates:* games master (weekly), attendance officer (fortnightly feed), counsellor (monthly supervision of the group as a whole); evaluation counted and dated — attendance slope per member at weeks six and twelve, fixtures staffed per month, one voluntary telling per member per month to any adult in the design. *The sentence that would fail it:* any member's safeguarding concern places them outside unsupervised charge of juniors immediately (week 11's safeguard), and the design records that rule at the top, not in a footnote.

**The four habits beyond the hall.** *Count in both directions* — risks and protections, every case, forever. *Date your falsifiers* — a plan without a test is a story, and stories thicken while children wait. *Say the contract before the question* — in every room you ever hold, including the ones without walls: the walk, the stall, the pitch. *Accompany* — the child goes with an adult, not to one; in referrals, in protection work, in the family conversation, in the examination of your own practice before a supervisor. These four are the semester's residue; the marks are its receipt.

::: safeguard
Examination conditions include ethics: case material in answers is anonymised and composite; no real client is ever written into an examination script, and any answer drawn from a live case without consent and anonymisation is a conduct matter, not a mark matter. The habit stated here is the habit the profession enforces later at greater cost.
:::

### Worked example 16.1 — Marking a candidate answer

**Task.** Mark the supplied candidate answer to Model A's case against the rubric, and write the examiner's two sentences of feedback.

**Solution.** *Candidate's answer, in brief:* a fluent narrative of displacement and anger; the formulation's context part strong; the body-and-brain part absent (no sleep, no medical screen); no falsifier; the plan lists "counselling sessions and sensitisation of the mother"; accompaniment unstated. *Marking:* presenting 4/5 (both voices present, the second paraphrased rather than verbatim — one mark); history and timing 4/5 (displacement and loss placed, the clock-break named); body and brain 0/5 (the strand-I omission: the day-night inversion is the case's most movable mechanism and it is missing); maintaining mechanisms 3/5 (belonging wound named, the anger's function missed); context 5/5; formulation subtotal 16/25 with the two omitted parts costing nine; plan 2/5 (no sequence, no owners, no dates — protections not before risks because neither is identifiable); falsifier 0/5; accompaniment 1/5 (mother "sensitised", the child's voice and presence unstated). *Total 19/40 — a fail that reads fluently,* which is the examination's central lesson: fluency is not structure, and structure is what protects the child when the practitioner is tired. *Examiner's two sentences:* "You wrote about Bala; you did not examine him — the sleep, the body and the test are where his case lives. Re-sit with the five parts as headings and the falsifier as a date, and this becomes a good answer."

### Worked example 16.2 — The retrieval session, run in class

**Task.** Run the day-6 retrieval as a timed class exercise and record the strand-omission pattern across the cohort.

**Solution.** Forty minutes, books closed: (i) the four-strand map from memory, ten minutes; (ii) the red-flag list and the contract's three limits from memory, five minutes; (iii) one case answer in house structure, twenty-five minutes, case supplied at the door: *Amina, 15, SS1, three weeks of afternoon-only absence, a healed burn on the forearm she calls "the iron", and a boyfriend of twenty-six named by a classmate in the referral.* The case is chosen because it draws every strand at once — the absence differential (strand III), the burn's differential including self-harm function and including the household iron's truth (strand II's function question and strand IV's red-flag screen), the age-asymmetric relationship as a protection-law matter (strand III's adversity and strand IV's statutory pathway), and the two clocks (strand III's culture line). *Marking in pairs against the published rubric, then the cohort's omission pattern tallied on the board:* in this cohort, as in most, the context part and the falsifier are the dropped lines — the two lines that cost least to write and most to lose. *The closing sentence of the semester, said aloud once:* the map is not the territory, but a counsellor without the map will call the territory a mystery, and the child will wait while the mystery is admired.

::: skills
**Practical 16 — the timed case.** Under examination conditions (40 minutes, books closed), write the house-structure answer to the case supplied at the door in 16.2; then mark a peer's answer against the rubric and receive the mark on your own with one sentence of dispute permitted. The practical examination of this course is this exercise, observed. The mark is for structure under time — because time is the condition practice imposes, and the hall is merely practice's rehearsal.
:::

::: assessment
**Assessed task.** The final written examination: three hours, closed book. **Section A (40 marks):** four short scenarios, each requiring one instrument applied with its output shown (a status map; an attendance-differential read; a two-list count; a verb-precise definition pair). **Section B (30 marks):** two essays from four, each requiring a strand's argument with its corrections and its limits (storm and stress; the internalising gap; the family's renegotiation; the WEIRD correction). **Section C (30 marks):** one extended case in house structure — formulation five parts with falsifier, sequenced plan with owners and dates, accompaniment and limits, and the two-clock lines. Continuous assessment (60% of the course mark) comprises the weekly assessed tasks, the practicum portfolio and the supervised sessions; the examination carries 40%.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Section A: instruments applied, outputs shown | 40% |
| Section B: argument with corrections and limits | 30% |
| Section C: house structure complete, falsifier dated | 30% |

**Formative.** Write the four habits in your own words on the first page of your practice notebook, dated. Re-read them at your first supervision after qualification, and again at your hundredth case.
:::

::: summary
**Synthesis.** The semester reassembles as four strands — body and brain, self and weather, relationships and contexts, practice and integration — each leaving its instruments in the hand; retrieval before re-reading, spaced across the week, with diagrams redrawn from memory, makes the map available under pressure; the verbs have structures and answers are marked against them; the case answer runs formulation, plan, falsifier and accompaniment in order, every case; and beyond the hall the semester leaves four habits — count in both directions, date your falsifiers, say the contract before the question, and accompany — because the marks are the receipt and the habits are the residue, and it is the residue that will be in the room at two in the morning when the book is not.
:::

### Seminar questions

1. Which strand do you keep omitting from your memory map, and what will that omission cost your first caseload?
2. Read the verb twice: recall one examination answer of your own past that answered a different verb than the one asked.
3. The candidate's answer read fluently and failed on structure. Where in your current practice does fluency mask missing structure?
4. Name your falsifier habit's weakest point: which plan of yours is currently untested and undated?
5. The four habits, in your own words, on one line each — written now, not after the examination.

### Closing note

This volume closes as week 1 opened: adolescence is neither storm nor strategy but an interval — a lengthening gap between two clocks, a brain remaking itself in public, a self assembling under new mirrors, a person being inducted into responsibility in whichever grammar their community speaks. The counsellor of adolescents holds the instruments of four strands and the habits of one profession: count, date, contract, accompany. The next volume of this series takes up the ethics that govern every instrument in this one.
