# School, Work and the Long Horizon: Aspiration and Its Economies {.unit num="10" week="10" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Describe** school as a developmental context: climate, belonging, transitions, and the achievement–identity loop, and read absence and refusal as signals with a differential.
2. **Explain** adolescent work and apprenticeship as both harness and hazard, and assess the schooling–work trade-off in a household economy.
3. **Apply** Super's life-span stages to adolescent vocational development, distinguishing aspiration from expectation and locating where each is bent by gender, class and economy.
4. **Counsel** aspiration without inflation or deflation: the horizon conversation, the plan's rungs, and the household sentences that widen or narrow a child's future.
5. **Audit** one service or school practice that narrows horizons, and write its replacement.
:::

### Where this week sits

The adolescent's third institution is the one that schedules their body and prices their future: school, and beside it — for many of the adolescents of this programme — work. This week treats both as developmental contexts rather than backdrops: the school's climate and belonging as mental-health variables, absence as a symptom with a differential, apprenticeship as a real vocational pathway with real costs, and aspiration as the construct where identity (week 5), esteem (week 6) and economy meet. Super's stages give the horizon its grammar; the counsellor's work is to keep the horizon honest — neither inflated into fantasy nor deflated into fate.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=School_climate}
The school's social-emotional weather: whether students feel known, safe and counted; a strong predictor of adolescent mental health, attendance and achievement, and more malleable than any individual trait. In the room: the variable a counsellor can move with a head teacher, when the child cannot be moved at all.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Absence_differential}
School absence is a signal, not a diagnosis: its differential includes anxiety and depression (week 7), victimisation in the building (week 9), household labour and economics (this week), illness and sleep debt (week 3), and conduct-oriented avoidance (week 4's heat). The pattern — total refusal versus partial drift, mornings versus afternoons, term-onset versus mid-term — separates them. In the room: the attendance record is an assessment instrument; read it before interviewing.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Aspiration_expectation_gap}
The distance between what an adolescent hopes for (aspiration) and what they believe probable (expectation); the gap is where economies, gender and class do their work, and a wide gap predicts disengagement rather than effort. In the room: ask both questions separately — "what do you want?" and "what do you think will happen?" — because the distance between the answers is the counselling target.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Vocational_exploration}
Super's exploration stage: the adolescent's first real trials — tentative choices, transitions, part-time work, apprenticeship — in which vocational identity is assembled the way personal identity is (week 5), by exploration and commitment together. In the room: why "what will you be?" is a developmental task and not an interrogation.
:::

### Lecture I — School as a developmental context

**Proposition 1. The school is a mental-health variable.** Climate and belonging predict adolescent distress and persistence independently of family factors; the transitions that damage belonging — primary to junior secondary, junior to senior, school to school — are the same transitions week 6 located in the esteem dip. The counsellor's levers inside a school are small and real: one adult who knows the child by name, one club or team that counts the child as a member, one timetable adjustment that removes a daily humiliation.

**Proposition 2. Absence has a differential and the record discriminates.** Total morning refusal with Sunday-night somatic complaints points to anxiety or victimisation; partial afternoon drift points to peer economy or work; mid-term onset after a result points to esteem and the rankings household (week 6); term-onset absence after a move points to belonging. The attendance pattern is read first, because interviewing without it produces the story the child can tell rather than the one the term shows.

**Proposition 3. Achievement and identity run in a loop.** Results feed the self-concept's school domain (week 6), and the self-concept feeds effort and examination weather; the loop turns vicious where results are read aloud at home or where the domain is weighted heavily and scored publicly. Breaking the loop rarely starts at the studying: it starts at the reading-aloud.

**Clinical implication.** Pull the attendance record before the second session of any adolescent case, and chart it by week against the case timeline: onset, pattern, mornings versus afternoons. The chart is free, and it argues with the referral letter.

### Lecture II — Work, apprenticeship and the household economy

Adolescent work is two things at once and the balance is empirical, not ideological. **As harness:** apprenticeship and family-enterprise work supply competence in a valued domain (week 6's lever), adult mentorship, income that buys autonomy, and a vocational identity that school-failure would otherwise foreclose; for many adolescents of this programme the trade is the horizon, and counselling that treats it as a consolation prize insults the client. **As hazard:** work that competes with school hours, that carries injury or exploitation, that is the household's income rather than the child's training, converts exploration into foreclosure and adolescence into labour — and the file says so plainly, because the child-as-carer and child-as-earner are safeguarding presentations (week 8), not maturity.

The **schooling–work trade-off** is assessed in the household's own economy: what does the child's work buy, who else would earn it, and what does school cost the household this term? The counsellor who cannot answer those three has no standing to advise on the trade; the counsellor who can answer them can often find the rung — evening classes, the deferred apprenticeship, the term-by-term fee plan, the scholarship application done together in session — that keeps both doors open one more year, which is usually all the horizon needs.

### Lecture III — Aspiration, expectation and the horizon conversation

Super's sequence — growth (fantasy, interest, capacity), exploration (tentative, transition, trial), establishment — locates the adolescent in exploration's first trials: real choices made before the self is settled, which is precisely why the choices must be real and many. Vocational development runs on the same engine as identity (week 5): exploration and commitment, in domains, at statuses — the fourteen-year-old foreclosed into the trade by economics and the sixteen-year-old diffused about everything are the same map's two casualties.

**The horizon conversation has four moves.** *Aspire:* "what do you want, if nothing stops you?" — recorded verbatim. *Expect:* "what do you think will happen?" — recorded verbatim; the gap between the two sentences is the target. *Audit the gap's owners:* who and what stands between the sentences — fees, gender, the household's assumption, the school's streaming, the child's own evidence — named one by one, because a gap with owners is a plan and a gap without owners is despair. *Build the rungs:* the next visible step inside the child's reach this term — the application, the evening class, the trade's first tool, the subject choice — chosen by the child, dated, and reviewed; the rung is the intervention, never the speech.

**Gender and class bend horizons early and quietly:** the daughter whose science is praised at twelve and steered away at fifteen; the son whose repeating year is read as termination rather than delay. The counsellor's discipline is to say the bend aloud in the room where it happens — with the household, in the household's idiom (week 8) — because a horizon narrowed in silence is a horizon the child believes they chose.

::: safeguard
Where adolescent work is the household's income rather than the child's training, or carries injury, exploitation or hazardous hours, the presentation is child labour and the jurisdiction's protection law governs: the counsellor reports per policy, tells the child what must happen and in what order, and continues to counsel — the accompaniment principle (week 7) applies to protection work as much as to risk work.
:::

### Worked example 10.1 — Ada's SS3 plan, arrived early

**Task.** The vocation foreclosure watch of week 5 has matured: the household assumes Ada will leave school after SS3 for the trade apprenticeship; Ada's aspiration sentence is "I want to finish university and sing"; her expectation sentence is "I will go to the apprenticeship because Bisi cannot pay." Conduct the horizon conversation and write the rungs.

**Solution.** *The gap:* university-and-song versus apprenticeship — a wide gap with three owners: fees (real, quantified this term: the SS3 fee and the examination fee), the household's assumption (unstated until this session, therefore unargued), and Ada's own evidence (her results are mid-band, which she reads as verdict rather than position). *The owners, one by one:* fees — the scholarship list of the diocesan board and the state bursary, applications done together across two sessions, with the auntie's signature line explained so the household is inside the plan rather than ambushed by it; assumption — the household conversation of week 8's method, in which the assumption is named as an assumption and dated ("after SS3 results, not before"), which converts a fate into a decision with a calendar; evidence — the results chart re-read as position and slope, with the two subjects whose slope is rising named aloud, because rising slope is the argument against verdict. *The rungs, chosen by Ada and dated:* this term — the bursary applications and one choir solo with a witness (week 9); next term — the subject choice for SS3 kept open to the science combination rather than closed to the trade track; after results — the decision meeting with all three sentences on the table. *The sentence the file keeps:* the apprenticeship is not removed from the table — it is dated, because a dated option is a plan and an undated assumption is a ceiling. Falsifier: if the bursary applications are not submitted by the term's sixth week, the fees owner has won silently and the session returns to the applications as the agenda, not to the mood.

### Worked example 10.2 — Kelvin's repeating year as exploration, not verdict

**Task.** Kelvin (16) repeats SS1. The uncle's rankings (week 6) have named the repeat "the end of school"; the set names school "not for people like us"; the trade (the uncle's shop) offers an apprenticeship now. Write the horizon conversation and the rung.

**Solution.** *Aspire:* "I want my own thing — a shop, or the team; something mine." *Expect:* "the shop now, because school has finished with me." The gap is narrow but inverted: his aspiration is vocational and alive, his expectation is foreclosure dressed as realism — and both the household and the set are supplying the dress. *Reframe in Super's grammar, aloud:* the repeat is a transition inside exploration, not an exit from it — sixteen in SS1 is two years inside the exploration stage, not outside it; the trade is a legitimate exploration trial, and the question is sequence rather than either/or. *The rung, negotiated with the household and the uncle present:* the apprenticeship begins **now** — Saturdays and school holidays only, in the uncle's shop, with the uncle as master and the ranking ritual replaced by the trade's own standards (which are warm: the trade praises work, not position); school continues to the SS1 examination with the football pride announced at the meal (weeks 2, 6, 8); and the decision meeting is dated after the examination result, with all three sentences — school, trade, team — on the table and none removed. *Why this rung:* it gives the set's narrative ("school is not for us") nothing to fight, gives the uncle's standards a warm channel, and keeps the school door open on a hinge the household itself holds. Falsifier, dated: attendance chart read at week six — if afternoon drift exceeds two sessions per fortnight, the trade is absorbing the school day and the hours are renegotiated with the uncle directly; if attendance holds and the result rises, the hinge stays open to SS2 and the horizon has widened by one year, which is the unit this work trades in.

::: skills
**Practical 10 — the horizon conversation.** In pairs on a supplied case: conduct the four moves (aspire, expect, audit the gap's owners, build the rungs) with both sentences recorded verbatim and at least three owners named; then write the rung calendar (this term, next term, after results) and the household sentence that must be said aloud for the rung to survive. 400 words. The mark is for verbatim sentences and dated rungs — an aspiration without a date is a wish, and this course does not grade wishes.
:::

::: assessment

::: summary
**Synthesis.** School is a developmental context whose climate and belonging are mental-health variables, whose transitions feed the esteem dip, and whose absence records are assessment instruments with a differential; adolescent work is harness and hazard at once, assessed in the household's own economy before any advice is given; Super locates the adolescent in exploration's first trials, where vocational identity assembles by the same engine as personal identity; aspiration and expectation are two different sentences whose gap has owners; and the horizon conversation — aspire, expect, audit the owners, date the rungs — keeps a child's future honest, with the apprenticeship dated rather than deleted and the decision meeting on a calendar the household holds.
:::
