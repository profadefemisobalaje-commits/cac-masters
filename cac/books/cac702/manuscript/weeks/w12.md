# One Biology, Many Clocks: Adolescence Across Cultures and Adversities {.unit num="12" week="12" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **State** what is universal in adolescence (puberty, pruning, the absence of universal storm) and what varies (the social clock, the moratorium, the rites), and use the distinction in formulation.
2. **Describe** adolescence under adversity — child marriage, adolescent labour, humanitarian displacement, the adolescent as carer — with the facts, the law and the counselling stance for each.
3. **Explain** the WEIRD critique and its consequences for this volume's evidence base, and practise the correction: sample-aware citation and local validation.
4. **Describe** African and indigenous socialisation accounts of adolescence as theory in their own right, not as local colour.
5. **Audit** one intake instrument or service practice for imported assumptions, and rewrite it for the population actually served.
:::

### Where this week sits

Every claim in this volume carries a provenance, and most of the provenance is narrow: the adolescent brain, esteem and identity literatures rest disproportionately on samples that are Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic. This week makes that limitation explicit rather than decorative, surveys what the wider world shows — one biology running on many social clocks — and treats the adversities that reshape adolescence for millions (child marriage, labour, displacement) as core curriculum rather than footnote, because the adolescents of this programme meet them in the first month of practice. It closes the developmental half's outer ring before the clinical weeks begin.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=Social_clock}
The culturally set timetable of transitions — school leaving, work, marriage, parenthood, household headship — against which the biological clock is read. The same puberty at thirteen means different things under different clocks, and the distress of off-timing (week 2) is a function of the local clock, not of biology alone. In the room: the question "whose clock is this household running on?" precedes any judgement about lateness or earliness.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Moratorium_variance}
The socially granted pause (week 5) exists on a gradient from the university gap-year to the absent pause of the child earner; much of what the literature calls identity status is moratorium access in disguise. In the room: status language must always be reported with the pause that produced it.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=WEIRD_critique}
Henrich and colleagues' demonstration that the behavioural sciences' samples are unusually Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic, and that many classic findings do not generalise across populations; for adolescent science the correction is sample-aware citation, replication attention, and local validation before import. In the room: the sentence "this finding comes from a sample unlike this child" is a clinical statement, not a caveat.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Socialisation_goals}
The account, developed in African and cross-cultural psychology (Nsamenang among others), that development is organised by the community's socialisation goals — responsible participation, duty, relational personhood — rather than by individual autonomy alone; adolescence under these goals is induction into responsibility, and its crises are crises of participation rather than of separation. In the room: a second theory of the same years, with its own predictions, and a check on the autonomy-default of imported models.
:::

### Lecture I — Universals and variations, precisely

**What travels:** puberty's biology and its secular trend; the brain's pruning and myelination timetable; sleep's phase delay; the absence of universal storm and stress (week 1); the peer world's layered structure (week 9), found wherever adolescents are in number enough to form one. **What varies:** the social clock's transitions and their order; the moratorium's existence and length; the rites that mark the passage — initiation, confirmation, graduation, marriage — which supply in some cultures the public identity-conferral that Western adolescents must extract from exams and likes; the household's jurisdiction map (week 8), whose personal domain is culturally sized; and the meaning of distress's idioms — somatic, spiritual, behavioural — through which internalising arrives at the clinic door.

The formulation consequence is exact: biology supplies the timing questions (week 2), the social clock supplies the meaning questions, and a counsellor who imports the clock with the biology will pathologise a married fifteen-year-old's grief as regression, or a child earner's foreclosure as failure, when the accurate sentence is that the clock and the biology have been made to disagree by an economy.

### Lecture II — Adolescence under adversity

**Child marriage** remains prevalent in parts of the world this programme serves: a transition that ends the moratorium, removes the peer world and the school clock at once, and carries maternal and mental-health risks that are documented and grave. The counselling stance has three parts and they do not contradict: the law (the jurisdiction's marriage age and protection statutes govern, and the counsellor reports where the law requires); the child (her safety, her voice, her own timetable for any decision, and the accompaniment principle); and the household (worked with rather than indicted, because the household is usually acting inside an economy and a norm, and conversion of the norm is slower work than rescue and is the work that saves the next girl). **Adolescent labour** was assessed in week 10 as harness and hazard; under adversity the hazard end governs and the protection pathway opens. **Humanitarian displacement** breaks every transition at once — school, home, clinic, peer world, language — and the counselling task is belonging-reconstruction in order (one adult, one routine, one peer, one role), because the resilience literature's first factor is the one displacement removes first. **The adolescent as carer** — of siblings, of a sick parent — is an induction into responsibility that socialisation-goal theories can honour and deficit models cannot: the file records the load and its hours, the school is brought into the plan, and the word "mature" is banned from the record, because it is the adjective that hides a childhood.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** In every formulation, write two lines: the biological clock's state (week 2) and the social clock's timetable with its owners (household, economy, law, faith). Where the two disagree, the disagreement is a finding, not a footnote.
:::

### Lecture III — The WEIRD critique, and theory from elsewhere

Henrich and colleagues showed that the behavioural sciences' convenience samples are unrepresentative of the species on many classic tasks; the adolescent literature inherits the problem — the dual-systems curve, the esteem dip's size, the identity statuses' distribution and the risk peaks' heights are all estimated substantially on WEIRD adolescents, and cross-national work shows meaningful variation in each. The corrections this volume practises and requires of you: **cite the sample** ("in US and European cohorts…") whenever the finding is sample-bound; **prefer the mechanism over the magnitude,** because mechanisms (pruning, phase delay, peer acceleration, minority stress) travel better than effect sizes; **validate locally before importing instruments,** since a scale normed elsewhere measures something slightly different here; and **treat non-Western theory as theory.**

Socialisation-goal accounts — Nsamenang's analysis of African childhood and adolescence as induction into responsible participation, personhood as relational rather than individual — predict things the autonomy-default misses: that conflict in these settings concentrates on duty and participation rather than on the personal domain; that identity achievement may be authored in the first person plural; that contribution (week 11's sixth C) is not an add-on but the goal. None of this refutes weeks 5 or 8; it bounds them. The counsellor's discipline is to hold both grammars — the individuating self and the participating person — and to notice which one the adolescent in the room is actually using, because the formulation written in the wrong grammar describes a stranger.

::: safeguard
Adversity presentations carry mandatory edges: child marriage and child labour engage the jurisdiction's protection statutes; displacement engages registration and entitlements that are themselves therapeutic (documents restore clocks); and female genital cutting, where present in the population served, is approached with the law stated plainly, the health facts stated plainly, and the community conversion work routed through the institutions that own it — never through the child as informant against her family in session.
:::

### Worked example 12.1 — Hauwa, fifteen, and the arrangement

**Task.** Hauwa (15, SS1) is withdrawn from school mid-term by her uncle for a marriage arrangement to a trader of thirty-four; the household frames it as protection after her father's death; the jurisdiction's law sets the marriage age at eighteen. She presents to the school counsellor on the day of withdrawal with one sentence: "I want to sit my exam." Formulate and plan.

**Solution.** *The two clocks:* biological — menarche at twelve, development unremarkable; social — the household's clock runs on an economy (the uncle's debt, the norm that marriage protects girls) rather than on the statute; the disagreement between clock and law is the case's first finding. *Formulation in five parts (week 13's form, previewed):* presenting — school withdrawal and the exam sentence; developmental — no prior mental-health contact, strong school belonging, one close friendship, choir absent, faith community present; maintaining — the arrangement removes every protective factor at once (school, peer, routine, future orientation) and supplies none; context — the uncle acts inside debt and norm, the mother is present but silent in the first meeting, the law is eighteen; risk — maternal and mental-health risks of the marriage, loss of the moratorium, and the safeguarding status of a minor in a marriage arrangement. *The plan, ordered:* law first — the protection report made per statute, with Hauwa told what will happen and in what order before it happens (accompaniment principle); child second — her exam sentence is honoured literally: the school is asked in writing to keep her registration and exam entry alive pending the process, because a live exam entry is a live clock; household third — the mother and uncle met with the community's own institutions present (the imam, the ward's women's leader), the conversion argument made in the household's grammar: protection is what the arrangement claims to supply, and the school-plus-bursary route supplies it with the law on its side and the trader's money replaced by the board's fee plan (week 10's rung); service fourth — Hauwa counselled weekly through the process regardless of outcome, with the belonging-reconstruction order (one adult, one routine, one peer, one role) ready if displacement within the family occurs. *The sentence the file keeps:* her one sentence is the formulation's spine — "I want to sit my exam" is a future orientation intact, the protective factor with the shortest restoration path, and therefore the first thing defended. Falsifier: if the exam entry lapses or the report stalls at four weeks, escalation to the statutory child-protection authority's senior officer is automatic and dated in the file now, not later.

### Worked example 12.2 — Auditing the intake form

**Task.** Audit this programme's model adolescent intake form (supplied) for imported assumptions; rewrite three items.

**Solution.** *Item 1, "Who lives in your home?"* assumes a nuclear household: rewritten as "Where do you sleep, where do you eat, and who pays your fees? — name the people in each answer," which finds the compound, the auntie's household, the boarding house and the child-as-carer load in three questions instead of hiding them. *Item 2, "What do you want to be?"* assumes an individual vocational self and a moratorium: rewritten as two questions — "What work do you already do, paid or unpaid, and how many hours in a school week?" and "What does your household expect of you this year, and what do you expect of yourself?" — which separates socialisation-goal induction from foreclosure, and surfaces the aspiration–expectation gap (week 10) without importing the gap's default direction. *Item 3, "On a scale of 1–10, how sad are you?"* assumes an internalising idiom of feeling-scales: rewritten as "Since the last rains, what has your body been doing that it did not do before? (sleep, appetite, headaches, energy)" plus "What has stopped, that you used to do?" — because in this population distress arrives somatically and as cessation, and a feeling-scale will return a number where a stop-list returns a formulation. *The audit's general rule, written into the form's header:* every item must be answerable by an adolescent who has never met a psychologist, in the words they already have; where an item cannot be, the item is the assumption and the item changes.

::: summary
**Synthesis.** Adolescence runs one biology on many clocks: puberty, pruning, phase delay and the absence of universal storm travel, while the social clock, the moratorium, the rites and the idioms of distress vary, and the disagreement between clock and biology is itself a finding; under adversity — child marriage, labour, displacement, caring — the counselling stance holds law, child, household and institution at once without letting any of the four cancel the others; the WEIRD critique obliges sample-aware citation, mechanism-over-magnitude preference and local validation; and socialisation-goal theory from African and cross-cultural psychology supplies a second grammar of the same years — participation and relational personhood — so that the formulation is written in the grammar the adolescent is actually speaking.
:::
