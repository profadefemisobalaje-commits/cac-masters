# Practice I: Engagement, Confidentiality and Motivational Interviewing {.unit num="14" week="14" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Engage** an adolescent in the first ten minutes — including the non-voluntary client — using room, seating, language and the opening contract.
2. **State and administer** the confidentiality contract and its limits, and assess Gillick-type competence for consent to care in your jurisdiction.
3. **Apply** motivational interviewing's spirit — partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation — and its core skills (OARS) with adolescents.
4. **Recognise and elicit** change talk, and avoid the righting reflex that manufactures resistance.
5. **Document** consent, competence and confidentiality decisions so that the record protects the child and the practitioner.
:::

### Where this week sits

Everything the semester has taught arrives, this week, in a room with a fourteen-year-old who did not ask to come. Engagement is the precondition of every other skill: nothing works through a closed door. Confidentiality is the door's lock — and its legally-shaped keys. Motivational interviewing is the grammar of conversation with a person who is ambivalent, which is to say with every adolescent. This week is rehearsed aloud in class, because these are mouth-skills, and mouth-skills are not learned by reading.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=First_ten_minutes}
The engagement window: room and seating arranged before the child enters (angle, not opposition; exit visible); the practitioner's first sentence about the room rather than the child; the confidentiality contract stated **before** any question; and the child's agenda asked before the referrer's. In the room: the ten minutes that decide whether the next ten weeks exist.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Confidentiality_contract}
The stated rule and its limits: what is said stays, except where life or serious harm is at stake, and except where the law compels; the child is told **what** would be shared, **with whom**, and that they will know first; the contract is restated when a limit is approached, not only at intake. In the room: the sentence that makes disclosure possible, and the sentence whose absence makes every disclosure a trap.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Gillick_competence}
The principle (from *Gillick v West Norfolk and Wisbech AHA*, House of Lords, 1986) that a child under the age of majority may consent to their own care where they have sufficient understanding and intelligence to comprehend the decision proposed; competence is decision-specific, assessed and recorded per decision, and its absence returns consent to the guardian without returning the child's voice to silence. In the room: the assessment that lets a fifteen-year-old consent to counselling in some jurisdictions, and the document that protects the practitioner who accepts it.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=MI_spirit_OARS}
Motivational interviewing's spirit — partnership (not authority), acceptance (including absolute worth and autonomy), compassion (the child's interest first), evocation (drawing out the child's own reasons) — and its core skills: Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries; with change talk (the child's own arguments for change) elicited and strengthened, and the righting reflex (the urge to fix, warn and persuade) noticed and set down. In the room: the method for ambivalence, which confrontation reliably worsens.
:::

### Lecture I — The first ten minutes, and the client who did not come

**The room is arranged before the child enters:** seats angled rather than opposed, the door and the child's line to it unblocked, the desk not between, water within the child's reach without asking, and — where the service has them — the materials that let hands work (paper, the cards, the walk available). Adolescents talk sideways: the session that begins facing a table begins as an interview, and interviews produce the answers interviews deserve.

**The first sentence is about the room, not the child:** "the chair by the window is the good one; the fan is loud, I know" — because the child's first task is scanning for threat, and a sentence about the room answers the scan without demanding self-disclosure. **The contract precedes the questions:** confidentiality and its limits stated in plain words, with the child's comprehension checked by asking them to say the rule back in their own words — the teach-back is the contract's signature. **The child's agenda is asked before the referrer's:** "what would make this hour not a waste for you?" — and where the answer is "nothing, I did not choose to come," that answer is accepted as the agenda for today, because agreeing about not wanting to be there is the first true thing the room can share.

**The non-voluntary client** is engaged by three moves: naming the coercion plainly ("you are here because the magistrate said so; that is real, and it is not your fault, and it is not my fault"); finding the one thing the child wants that the mandate accidentally makes available (the hour off school, the letter for the file, a person who is not a police officer); and contracting small and honestly ("we owe each other six sessions; what shall we put in them so that they are yours as well as the court's?"). Resistance in this client is not pathology but dignity; the practitioner who argues with it becomes the second magistrate.

### Lecture II — Confidentiality, consent and the competence assessment

**The contract's words, once, plainly:** "what you say here stays here. There are three exceptions, and I will tell you all three now: if you are going to be seriously hurt, if someone else is going to be seriously hurt, and if a court orders me — and even then, you hear it from me first, and you come with me when it is said. Anything else — the pregnancy, the cannabis, the boyfriend, the anger at your mother — stays." The list is given **before** the first disclosure-shaped question, because a contract offered after a disclosure is a confession extracted under false premises.

**Gillick-type competence is assessed per decision and recorded:** the child's understanding of what the care is, what it is for, its alternatives, its risks and its likely effects; their ability to weigh these and to communicate a choice; the absence of coercion in the choice; and the maturity to grasp the decision's consequences — including that confidentiality has the three limits stated. Where competence is present, the child consents and the record says on what basis; where it is absent or borderline, the guardian consents **and the child's assent is still sought and still recorded**, because consent and voice are separate instruments and losing one must not cost the other. Where the jurisdiction's statute or policy modifies the common-law position, the statute governs and the practitioner knows it — this course teaches the principle, and your jurisdiction supplies its edges.

**Information flow inside a family case** follows week 8's geometry: individual sessions' content is not the practitioner's to release; joint sessions are prepared with each party beforehand; nothing is ambushed. The child is told, at intake, exactly what the practitioner will and will not tell their parents — and the sentence is written in the file in the child's own teach-back words.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** Rehearse the contract aloud until it is fluent in under forty seconds and survives interruption. The contract delivered haltingly teaches the child that the practitioner does not believe it either.
:::

### Lecture III — Motivational interviewing with adolescents

**The spirit, in adolescent translation:** *partnership* — the practitioner is a consultant to the child's life, not its manager; *acceptance* — including the accurate empathy that repeats the child's sentence before adding anything; *compassion* — the child's interest ahead of the institution's convenience, including the school's and the court's; *evocation* — the reasons for change are extracted from the child, because imported reasons have no owner and no fuel.

**OARS, with adolescent examples.** *Open questions:* "what is the good of the evenings, and what is the cost?" rather than "you know the evenings are bad for you?" *Affirmations:* specific and true — "you told your auntie about the arrest yourself; that took something" — because inflated praise is the currency of the rankings household and is discounted on sight. *Reflections:* the engine of MI; the simple reflection ("the set is the only place nobody ranks you") and the double-sided one ("part of you wants the league, and part of you knows the league means leaving two of the evenings") — reflections earn the next sentence where questions extract it. *Summaries:* the collecting summary that hands the child their own change talk back in order, ending with the key question: "and what do you make of all that, yourself?"

**Change talk and the righting reflex.** Change talk is the child's own argument — desire ("I wish I slept"), ability ("I could do the morning if the phone slept outside"), reasons ("the league needs me sober-ish"), need ("Bisi is tired of worrying"), and the commitment language at the far end ("I will try the phone outside this week"). It is elicited by asking for it ("what would be the good of changing the evenings, if anything?"), strengthened by reflecting it louder than the sustain talk, and never contradicted. The **righting reflex** — the urge to warn, fix, persuade and inform before being asked — is the manufacturer of sustain talk: every argument the practitioner makes for change, the ambivalent child is compelled by dignity to make against it. The discipline is to let the child hold both sides aloud and to sit on the practitioner's own side of the argument, because ambivalence argued **with** hardens and ambivalence explored **aloud** moves.

::: safeguard
MI is not used to obtain compliance with a practitioner's outcome: evocation means the child's own goals govern, including the goal of not changing today. Where a mandated outcome exists (court, protection plan), the mandate is named honestly and MI works on the child's own goals **beside** it — using MI to disguise a mandate is a breach of the acceptance the method is built on, and adolescents detect it within a session.
:::

### Worked example 14.1 — Ada's contract and the competence record

**Task.** Write the confidentiality contract as delivered to Ada at intake (teach-back included), and the Gillick-type competence record for her consent to continue counselling without parental signature at fifteen in a jurisdiction following the Gillick principle.

**Solution.** *Contract as delivered, forty seconds:* "Ada, before anything else: what you say in this room stays in this room. Three exceptions, and you will hear all three now — if you are going to be seriously hurt, if someone else is going to be seriously hurt, or if a court orders me to speak. Even then, you hear it from me first and you come with me when it is said. Everything else — the nights, the choir, your mother, anything — stays. Tell me the rule back in your own words, so I know I said it properly." *Teach-back, recorded verbatim:* "It stays unless I am going to be hurt or I will hurt somebody or a court asks; and even then you tell me first and I go with you." *Competence record, per decision (continuing counselling):* understands what counselling is (talk, weekly, no medicines, can stop); understands alternatives (stop now; school counsellor only; medical referral if the week-13 falsifier fails); understands risks and likely effects (some sessions worse before better; the auntie will know attendance but not content); weighs and communicates a choice (chose to continue, reasons given: the nights, and "somebody who is not family"); choice uncoerced (auntie informed of attendance only, and Ada states the auntie "did not push"); maturity adequate to the decision's consequences, including the three limits (teach-back above). *Decision:* competent to consent to this decision; consent recorded with date; assent-and-voice note added for the file: where any future decision exceeds this competence (medical referral), guardian consent will be sought **and** Ada's assent and teach-back recorded beside it. *The flow sentence given to the auntie, with Ada present and Ada's words first:* "she attends weekly; what she says stays with her, except the three things she will tell you herself now" — and Ada names the three, which is the contract surviving its first family test.

### Worked example 14.2 — MI micro-practice: Kelvin's evenings

**Task.** From the supplied transcript fragment of session four with Kelvin, identify the practitioner's righting-reflex failures, rewrite them as OARS, and mark the change talk.

**Solution.** *Fragment 1 — practitioner:* "You know the evenings are how the arrest happened, Kelvin." *Failure:* closed, warning, the practitioner's argument — sustain talk compelled. *Rewrite (open + reflection):* "The evenings — tell me the good of them first, because there must be good, or you would not be there." *Fragment 2 — Kelvin:* "Nobody ranks me there. I am not the repeating boy there." *Change talk?* No — sustain talk, and true; the practitioner's next line decides everything. *Original practitioner:* "But the league ranks you as a coach, which is better." *Failure:* contradiction ("but") of the child's truth — the fastest way to close the door. *Rewrite (reflection, doubled):* "There you are just Kelvin — the repeating stays outside the gate. That is not a small thing to give up." *Fragment 3 — Kelvin:* "…Bisi waits up though. And the league man asked me twice already." *Change talk:* reasons and desire, arriving unprompted once the sustain talk was honoured. *Practitioner's correct move (collecting summary + key question):* "So the set is the one room without rankings, and at the same time your auntie waits up, and the league man has asked twice — and somewhere in you those are all true at once. What do you make of it yourself?" *The discipline visible in the rewrite:* the practitioner made no argument for the league; the child supplied two of the four change-talk strands within three sentences of being reflected rather than corrected. Evaluation of the corrected transcript: change-talk count 4 (reasons ×2, desire ×1, ability-adjacent ×1), sustain-talk honoured twice, righting reflex zero — the scoring sheet this course uses in the practical examination.

::: skills
**Practical 14 — the rehearsal.** In triads, three rotations: (a) the first ten minutes including the contract with teach-back, on a hostile non-voluntary opening ("I did not choose to come"); (b) the competence assessment conducted as conversation, not interrogation, ending in a recorded decision sentence; (c) ten minutes of MI on a supplied ambivalence transcript, scored live by the observer for OARS counts, change-talk counts and righting-reflex failures. Each counsellor performs all three; observers score on the sheet used in 14.2. 400-word reflection naming your own most frequent reflex. The mark is for fluency under hostility — the contract and the reflection must survive the slammed door, because that is the only door some adolescents have.
:::

::: assessment
**Assessed task.** A practice portfolio of 1,300 words: the first-ten-minutes plan for a named non-voluntary case (room, first sentence, contract, agenda question, the three moves for the mandated client); the confidentiality contract as you would deliver it verbatim with the teach-back question; a Gillick-type competence record for one real decision in the case, with the assent-beside-consent sentence; an MI transcript rewrite of a supplied fragment scored for OARS, change talk and righting reflex; and 200 words on your own most frequent reflex and its discipline.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| First ten minutes concrete, mandated client handled | 20% |
| Contract verbatim, limits complete, teach-back present | 20% |
| Competence record per decision, assent beside consent | 20% |
| MI rewrite scored accurately | 25% |
| Reflex named with discipline | 15% |

**Formative.** Record yourself delivering the contract once. Listen for the hesitation. The hesitation is the limit you do not yet believe you will enforce.
:::

::: summary
**Synthesis.** Engagement is arranged before the child enters — angle, exit, water, hands-able materials — and opened with a sentence about the room, the contract before the questions, and the child's agenda before the referrer's, with the mandated client met by naming the coercion, finding the one wanted thing, and contracting small; confidentiality is a stated contract with three limits given before the first disclosure-shaped question, teach-back as its signature, and family information flow governed by week 8's geometry; Gillick-type competence is decision-specific, assessed and recorded per decision, with assent kept beside consent where competence is absent; and motivational interviewing — partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation, OARS, change talk elicited and the righting reflex set down — is the grammar by which ambivalence is explored aloud rather than argued with, because imported reasons have no owner and dignity compels the ambivalent child to defend whichever side the practitioner takes.
:::

### Seminar questions

1. Your first sentence is about the room. Why is a sentence about the child a threat in minute one and a gift in month three?
2. The contract offered after a disclosure is a confession extracted under false premises. Which limit have you been leaving out?
3. Competence is decision-specific. Name one decision where the same child is competent and one where they are not, and say what differs.
4. Every argument you make for change, the child is compelled to make against. Recall your last righting reflex: what did it cost?
5. MI beside a mandate, never disguising it: what sentence tells the court-mandated client the difference?
