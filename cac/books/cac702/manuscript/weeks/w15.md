# Practice II: The Practicum Panel — Thirteen, Fifteen, Seventeen {.unit num="15" week="15" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Adjust** the counselling method across the age gradient — session length, opening, language, tools, consent architecture — while keeping the method itself constant.
2. **Conduct** a full first session with a thirteen-year-old presenting with somatic complaints and school refusal, using side-by-side and doing-based technique.
3. **Conduct** a full session with a fifteen-year-old presenting with self-harm, integrating weeks 6, 7 and 14 in one continuous record.
4. **Conduct** a session with a seventeen-year-old presenting with examination anxiety and substance use, in the adult register, with the client's agenda governing.
5. **Write** a supervision record including one rupture and its repair, and use supervision as the method's quality control.
:::

### Where this week sits

This is the semester's practicum: three adolescents in full panel, each session planned and recorded end to end, each at a different point on the age gradient of week 14's register. The panel's purpose is integration — every week's instrument used in one continuous record under time pressure — and the supervision record, because practice without supervised review is repetition rather than training. The three cases are fictitious composites, as all cases in this volume are.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=Method_gradient}
The method is constant; the register changes: at thirteen, concrete, short, side-by-side and doing-based, with guardian consent plus the child's assent; at fifteen, the peer register — loyalty first, then the worry, hot-and-cold sequencing, the safety card in the pocket; at seventeen, the adult register — their agenda, their words, their plan, the practitioner's questions last, consent their own. In the room: what fails at thirteen is not the therapy but the grammar.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Doing-based_technique}
With younger adolescents, the session's work happens beside rather than across: the walk, the draw, the cards, the task with hands — because disclosure follows activity at thirteen and follows silence at seventeen. The tool is not a game but a shared third object that lets the worry be spoken about at one remove until it can be spoken about directly. In the room: the draw-and-walk session is a full session, not a warm-up.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Hot_cold_sequencing}
At fifteen, the session is sequenced by temperature: cold material (school, the set, the fixture, the feed) first and at length, hot material (the wound, the scar, the night) later and briefly, with the safety plan closing — because a fifteen-year-old who is asked for the hot material in minute four learns that the room is an interrogation and returns guarded or not at all. In the room: the agenda's order is a clinical decision, recorded as one.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Rupture_repair}
The alliance break — the missed session, the wrong sentence, the broken promise, the practitioner's irritation — and its explicit repair: named, owned, and re-contracted. Repair predicts outcome better than smoothness, and the supervision record that hides ruptures hides the only material supervision can work on. In the room: the sentence "I got that wrong last week, and here is what I think I got wrong" is an intervention.
:::

### Lecture I — The gradient, and the thirteen-year-old with the stomach

**Case A: Tunde, 13, JSS3** — six weeks of Monday stomach pains, school refusal rising from one day per fortnight to three, Sunday-night vomiting once, attendance chart showing morning-only absence with afternoons intact; medical review unremarkable (documented, week 13's screen); referral from the school with the word "manipulative" in it.

**Session architecture, thirteen:** forty minutes, not fifty; seats beside a table with paper and the ward map from the floods appeal; the walk available and used from minute fifteen; guardian consent recorded with the mother present, and the child's assent recorded separately in his own teach-back words (week 14); the contract delivered at child height: "stays here, three exceptions, say it back."

**The session, in order:** the room sentence; the contract with teach-back; the agenda question answered with "the stomach" — his word, adopted verbatim as the case's name for the presentation; the draw — "draw the stomach's week": seven boxes, and in his drawing Monday's box is black and Saturday's is a football, which is the formulation's first page without a single question; the walk, in which the peer layer arrives unprompted (the JSS3 class's new teacher who "reads names aloud and laughs at the slow ones" — the humiliation niche, week 6's loop in a staff member's hands); the doing-based plan — not "feelings work" but two concrete moves: the mother's Monday-morning sentence changed from "stop it, you are late" to "your stomach is loud today; I am still going to work and you are still going to school, and I will ask again at four," removing the audience-payoff without removing the mother; and the school lever of week 10 — one adult in the building (the games master) named as Tunde's person, with the reading-aloud teacher addressed through the head teacher's discipline channel, because a child's somatic refuge is sometimes a staff member's conduct. **Closing:** the stomach diary as a draw, not a form — seven boxes weekly, brought to the walk. Falsifier, dated: Monday attendance at four weeks; if refusal consolidates despite the audience change and the school lever, the anxiety differential of week 13 reopens with the mother's own history asked, because heterotypic continuity runs in families.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** At thirteen, plan the session around a third object (draw, walk, cards, task) and a length of forty minutes; write the object and the length into the session plan before the objectives, because the grammar governs what the objectives can be.
:::

### Lecture II — The fifteen-year-old with the scar

**Case B: Chiamaka, 15, SS2** — healed cuts on the left thigh discovered by the matron at boarding; two episodes, six weeks apart; no suicidal intent on direct question (asked, recorded, week 7); function: regulation ("when the noise in my head is too loud, the pain makes it quiet") plus a communication edge ("at least the matron asked"); sleep debt, comparison feed, parents divorced with the father absent since JSS1; high achievement, choir absent, one friendship of quality at home but not at boarding.

**Session architecture, fifteen:** fifty minutes; hot–cold sequencing written into the plan as an agenda order — cold first (boarding, the matron, the subjects, the feed, the home weekend), hot later (the scar, the nights), safety plan closing; the contract restated at the top with the three limits and the teach-back, because the matron's discovery has made the child test the lock first ("will you tell my mother?") — and the answer is the contract's own words: not today, not for this; the three exceptions named again; the mother informed of **attendance and the safety plan** with Chiamaka present and speaking first, per week 8's geometry and week 14's flow sentence.

**The session, in order:** cold material at length — the boarding week hour by hour, the feed audit collaborative (week 6), the friendship-at-home named and its weekly call scheduled as architecture rather than hope; then the hot material, opened by her, in minute thirty-one — the function question asked without shock ("tell me what it does for you"), the grammar recorded (regulation, communication); the interruption repertoire built **from what already works** (the cold shower she already uses at lights-out, the rosary's ten minutes, the walk to the gate) and written on the card; means conversation with the matron and mother in plain, unshaming language — the blade not hunted but the household and boarding store agreeing the ordinary storage, said to Chiamaka aloud as friction-removal, not punishment (week 7); the safety plan in her words with the rehearsed sentence said twice; the school lever — one staff member (the maths teacher she named in the cold hour) as her person in the building. **Closing:** review in two weeks; the card in the pocket; the falsifier dated — any fresh mark reopens assessment at medical review the same week; the nights counted at review, because sleep is the first lever (week 7) and boarding lights-out is where the debt lives.

### Lecture III — The seventeen-year-old in the adult register, and supervision

**Case C: Emeka, 17, SS3 final year** — self-referred (the first in this panel, and the register changes at the door): examination anxiety with insomnia, and cannabis "to sleep, most nights since the mocks"; aspiration–expectation sentences recorded verbatim (week 10): "medicine at the university" versus "I will fail and the house will sell the land"; father's stroke last year, Emeka as the eldest son carrying the household's entire future orientation alone.

**Session architecture, seventeen:** fifty minutes; his agenda governs and is written first — the sleep and the cannabis, in his order, not the practitioner's; consent his own, competence recorded per decision including the decision about what the family is told (nothing, today; the plan for the father's conversation drafted **by him** in session four); the practitioner's questions deferred to the final third; MI as the grammar throughout, because ambivalence is the case (the cannabis works tonight and costs tomorrow — a true double-sided reflection, not a contradiction).

**The session, in order:** his agenda; the contract; the sleep architecture worked as engineering (week 3): the phone's exile to the hallway, the revision hour moved from midnight to after the dawn prayer, the caffeine of the evening kiosk named; the cannabis addressed by evocation only in session one — the good and the cost in his own words, change talk counted (desire, reasons, one commitment-language sentence: "I want the nights to be mine again"), no advice given, the information offered when asked and only then; the household's future orientation **shared rather than carried** — the session's deepest work: the sentence "the land is not yours alone to save" rehearsed for the family conversation he will lead, with the father's stroke-grief named as the room's unspoken fourth chair; the aspiration rung dated (week 10): the university form completed in session five, together, because at seventeen the rung is completed in the room or not at all. **Closing:** his own plan, in his words, on his phone's note app — the card's adult form.

**The supervision record, and rupture:** in session three the practitioner, tired, answered Emeka's hesitation with "you know what you need to do" — the righting reflex in its laziest costume; Emeka went quiet and the session ended eight minutes early. The record carries the rupture named and owned; supervision's three questions (what was the practitioner carrying? what did the child's hesitation actually say? what is the repair sentence?); and the repair delivered at session four's opening, verbatim: "Last week I answered your silence with a sentence that belonged to your father, not to me. I got that wrong. Your hesitation was telling me something, and I talked over it — say it again, I am listening now." Emeka's change talk returned in the fourth sentence. The lesson the panel exists to teach: the repair is not damage control; at seventeen, being repaired by an adult is rarer than being apologised to by none, and the alliance that survives a named rupture is the alliance that holds at two in the morning.

::: safeguard
The panel's three cases each carry one safeguarding edge to check in examination conditions: Case A — the staff member's conduct (report per policy; the child's person is not the accused adult); Case B — means storage and the matron's role (institutional child-protection officer engaged; boarding policy reviewed); Case C — the cannabis's supplier within the school (statutory and school policy edges named; the child's own use handled clinically, the supply handled institutionally). A practicum record that misses its safeguarding edge fails regardless of technique marks.
:::

### Worked example 15.1 — Case A's full session plan

**Task.** Write Tunde's session-one plan in the examination format: architecture line, contract line, agenda line, third object, doing-based moves, closing, falsifier.

**Solution.** *Architecture:* 40 minutes; room B (the window chair, the fan off); seats beside the table; paper, pencils, the ward map; walk route agreed with the office before the session. *Consent:* mother's written consent recorded 12/—; Tunde's assent teach-back verbatim: "I can stop when I want and my mum knows I come." *Contract:* delivered at minute two; teach-back verbatim: "stays here unless hurt or court; then you tell me first." *Agenda:* "the stomach" (his word adopted). *Third object:* "draw the stomach's week" — seven boxes; Monday black, Saturday football; the draw discussed at one remove ("what does Monday's box know?") before directly. *Walk, minute fifteen:* route via the junior pitch; the peer layer arrives unprompted (the reading-aloud teacher); the practitioner reflects only, no questions for four minutes. *Doing-based moves:* (i) the mother's Monday sentence rewritten with Tunde choosing the words; (ii) the games master named as his person, introduction arranged for Thursday; (iii) the head-teacher channel opened for the reading-aloud conduct, with Tunde informed of every step beforehand (accompaniment). *Closing:* the seven-box draw diary; next session booked at his chosen day (Saturday — his football day, chosen deliberately so the session rides the good day); the mother's teach-back of the Monday sentence recorded. *Falsifier:* Monday attendance ≥3 of 4 at week four; else week 13's anxiety differential reopens including maternal history.

### Worked example 15.2 — Case C's rupture record

**Task.** Write the supervision record for Emeka's session-three rupture in the house format: event, practitioner's carrying, child's hesitation decoded, repair sentence, outcome, learning.

**Solution.** *Event:* minute 34, Emeka hesitates on the cannabis-cost sentence; practitioner says "you know what you need to do"; silence; session ends eight minutes early; practitioner's note that night reads "client resistant" — corrected in supervision to "practitioner reflex". *Practitioner's carrying:* the practitioner's own brother's examination death the previous year, unspoken in nine months of caseload; the hesitation touched the practitioner's grief and the reflex spoke instead of the practitioner. *Child's hesitation decoded:* not resistance — the cost sentence's other half is "if I stop, I sleep without it and the nights are where my father's stroke lives"; the hesitation was grief approaching, and the reflex closed the door on it. *Repair sentence (delivered session four, minute one):* as recorded in Lecture III, verbatim. *Outcome:* change talk returned at sentence four ("the nights are where he is… I want the nights to be mine again"); the father conversation drafted that session, by Emeka; cannabis reduced to three nights by review without any practitioner argument — the evocation record standing as the method's evidence. *Learning, one sentence for the practitioner's file:* the righting reflex speaks loudest where the practitioner is un-grieved; supervision's first question is always "what am I carrying?", asked before "what is the client doing?"

::: skills
**Practical 15 — the panel rehearsal.** In fours (client, practitioner, observer, supervisor), run Case B's hot–cold session to the safety plan, timed at fifty minutes; the observer scores sequence fidelity (cold before hot, plan closing) and the supervisor conducts a ten-minute review using the three questions; rotate all four roles across the three cases in the week's two contact sessions. 500-word supervision record including one rupture named and owned — a record without a rupture is a record of a session that was not observed. The mark is for sequence fidelity and for the repair sentence delivered aloud, fluently, at minute one.
:::

::: assessment
**Assessed task.** A practicum portfolio of 1,500 words: one full session plan per case in the examination format (architecture, consent and contract lines, agenda, technique by register, closing, falsifier); the hot–cold agenda order justified for Case B with the minute of the hot opening; Case C's MI scored for OARS, change talk and righting reflex on a supplied fragment; and one supervision record with rupture, decode, repair and learning.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Three session plans in format, registers distinct | 30% |
| Sequence fidelity justified (Case B) | 15% |
| Consent and contract lines complete per age | 15% |
| MI fragment scored accurately (Case C) | 15% |
| Supervision record with owned rupture and repair | 25% |

**Formative.** Write the repair sentence you owe someone — client, supervisee, child of your own — and say whether you will deliver it this term. The practicum's final mark is conduct, not prose.
:::

::: summary
**Synthesis.** The gradient holds the method constant and changes the register: at thirteen, forty minutes beside a third object with assent beside consent and the plan built from a draw and a walk; at fifteen, hot–cold sequencing with the contract retested at the door, function-seeking inquiry, means as friction-removal, and the safety plan in the child's words closing; at seventeen, the client's agenda first and the practitioner's questions last, evocation as the grammar, the household's future orientation shared rather than carried, and the rung completed in the room; and over all three, supervision as quality control — the rupture named and owned, the practitioner's carrying asked first, and the repair delivered at minute one, because the alliance that survives a named rupture is the alliance that holds at two in the morning.
:::

### Seminar questions

1. What fails at thirteen is the grammar, not the therapy. Name one technique of yours that does not survive the gradient downward.
2. Hot–cold sequencing: what is the cost of asking for the hot material in minute four, and who pays it?
3. The repair sentence at minute one: why is owning the rupture an intervention rather than a confession?
4. Supervision's first question is "what am I carrying?" What are you carrying this term, and who knows?
5. At seventeen the rung is completed in the room or not at all. Which rung of your current caseload is waiting on a pen?
