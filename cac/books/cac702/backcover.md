A complete lecture course in the development of the adolescent, sized for a sixteen-week
semester and written to be taught from: objectives, three lectures, worked examples,
practicals, an assessed task with its criteria, and a reading list for every week.

Adolescence is treated here as what the science says it is: not a storm to be survived but a
period of specialised learning — a brain reorganising under new hormones, a self rebuilding
under new eyes, a person rehearsing the adult world in the safest available theatre. The
volume moves from puberty and the brain through identity, emotion, family, peers, school,
risk and culture to the clinical onsets and the practice of counselling adolescents well.

**In this volume**

- what adolescence is, how it was invented, and why its length keeps changing
- puberty: the axis, the stages, the timing and its psychological consequences
- the adolescent brain: dual systems, pruning, and sleep's perfect storm
- adolescent thinking: formal operations, egocentrism, hot and cold cognition
- identity: statuses, ethnic and religious identity, orientation and gender
- self and body: esteem trajectories and the comparison machine
- emotion: the internalising gap, self-harm, and the conversations that save
- family: autonomy renegotiated, conflict reframed, monitoring without snooping
- peers, romance and sexual development; school, work and aspiration
- risk as function, resilience, and positive youth development
- adolescence across cultures, adversities and the WEIRD critique
- clinical onsets, the adolescent formulation, and practice that engages
