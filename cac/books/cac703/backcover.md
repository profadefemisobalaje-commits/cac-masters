Every counsellor eventually meets the night question: two duties, both real, both mine, and only
one can be kept by morning. This volume is the preparation for that night. It teaches the four
principles and the fifth that holds them, the three great duties — confidentiality, consent and
boundaries — in their full depth and their legal edges, and the habits that make ethical
decision-making a discipline rather than a temperament: name the dilemma, consult, document, and
be able to say aloud why the other duty was set down.

**Inside this volume**

- The four principles plus fidelity, and the history of harm that makes codes necessary
- Confidentiality deeply: privilege, the three limits, records, data and the digital room
- Consent and competence across the lifespan, including the child's own voice and the Gillick question
- Boundaries and multiple relationships in small communities where every client is also a neighbour
- Competence and its limits: scope, referral, supervision, impairment and the colleague who is struggling
- Values, culture and faith in the plural room, and the discipline of not imposing
- Assessment, labels, records and reports: who owns the record, and what a court may ask
- Ethics with children and adolescents: protection duties, the guardian, the school and the child's voice
- Research, student practice, case presentation and the new questions of technology and AI
- Institutions, caseloads and whistleblowing; justice, access and the counsellor's public voice
- A decision-making model practised under time, and the law mapped without fear
- A dilemma panel of three full cases and an ethics committee in role, then the examination

Written for the Master in Children and Adolescent Counseling of Triune Biblical University Global
Extension USA, this volume closes over the instruments of the preceding volumes with the duties
that govern every one of them. The cases are fictitious composites; the duties are real.
