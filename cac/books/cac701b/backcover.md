A complete lecture course in the theories and evidence of human development from
conception through the school years, written for counsellors of children and adolescents
and sized for a sixteen-week semester: objectives, three lectures, worked examples,
practicals, an assessed task with its criteria, and a reading list for every week.

The volume follows one fictitious family — Ife, three years and eight months, and her
brother Kunle, seven — from week to week, so that each theory meets the same children and
the theories can be watched disagreeing about them.

**In this volume**

- the four questions of developmental science, methods, ethics, and the child in context
- foundations before birth: teratogens, epigenetics, prematurity, early adversity
- Piaget's constructing child and Vygotsky's social mind, argued against each other
- executive function, theory of mind, and the bottlenecks mistaken for character
- temperament, attachment and the co-regulation of emotion; goodness of fit
- language, bilingualism, and the red flags that must never be waited out
- self, identity and moral reasoning; peers, play and gender
- family, culture, adversity and resilience; atypical development read developmentally
- integration: the developmental formulation, screening, and the three-ages practicum
