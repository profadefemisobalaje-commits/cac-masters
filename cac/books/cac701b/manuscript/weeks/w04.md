# The Social Mind: Vygotsky, the Zone and the Scaffold {.unit num="4" week="4" hours="4"}

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. **Contrast** Vygotsky's social origin of mind with Piaget's individual construction, stating what each explains that the other cannot.
2. **Define** the zone of proximal development and distinguish dynamic from static assessment, stating what each predicts.
3. **Demonstrate** scaffolding with its six moves — recruiting, reducing degrees of freedom, direction maintenance, marking critical features, frustration control, demonstration — and its withdrawal.
4. **Interpret** private speech developmentally, as a tool rather than a fault, and counsel a teacher or parent who hears it as one.
5. **Apply** the zone to emotional and social learning: the scaffolded regulation of a child who cannot yet regulate alone.
:::

### Where this week sits

Piaget watched the child alone with the world; Vygotsky watched the child with someone. The difference is not academic: it decides whether the counsellor sees a score or a trajectory, a deficit or an untaught zone. This week installs the most practically powerful idea in the volume — that what a child can do with help today, they can do alone tomorrow, and that help has a grammar — and carries it from homework to heartbreak.

### Key concepts

::: {.keypoint title=Zone_of_proximal_development}
The distance between what the child does independently and what they do with a capable partner; the region where teaching lands. In the room: the reason a static test measures yesterday and a scaffolded session measures tomorrow — and the region every intervention should be aimed at.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Scaffolding}
The contingent support a partner provides inside the zone — recruiting interest, simplifying, holding the goal, marking what matters, controlling frustration, demonstrating — handed over step by step and withdrawn step by step (Wood, Bruner & Ross). In the room: the grammar of help; support that never fades is not scaffolding, it is substitution.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Private_speech}
The child's speech to self: for Piaget, egocentric residue to be outgrown; for Vygotsky, thought in the making — social speech turned inward, audible at first, whispered later, silent finally. In the room: the child muttering through a hard task is not disturbing the class; they are regulating it.
:::

::: {.keypoint title=Dynamic_assessment}
Test, teach, retest: the measure of the zone rather than of the stock. Its score is the amount and kind of help that moved the child, and it predicts learning better than a static score in several populations, including children whose language or culture the static test does not speak. In the room: the antidote to the label that a single morning's testing can otherwise write on a child.
:::

### Lecture I — Mind begins between people

![The zone of proximal development as three regions: what the child does alone, what the child does with a scaffolded partner, and what is not yet reachable even with help; with the counsel to test with help as well as alone. *Source:* prepared for this edition.](/books/cac701b/figures/fig-04-01.png){width=94%}

**Proposition 1. Every function appears twice.** First between people, on the social plane; then within the child, on the psychological plane. Attention is first jointly held ("look!"), memory is first stored in a parent's narrative ("remember when…"), regulation is first co-regulation (week 6). The child's inner tools are internalised conversations — which is why the quality of the conversations a child is given is the quality of the mind they build.

**Proposition 2. Tools shape thought.** Language is the master tool, but cultures hand children others: counting systems, scripts, calendars, proverbs, prayer. A child's reasoning carries the marks of its tools; the counsellor who forgets this mistakes a tool difference for a capacity difference, which is week 1's universality question with consequences.

**Proposition 3. The zone is where development happens, and it is invisible to static testing.** A test administered alone scores the achieved; two children with the same achieved score can have entirely different zones, and it is the zone that predicts what the next term of teaching — or therapy — will produce. Dynamic assessment makes the zone visible by measuring the help: how much prompting, of what kind, before the child crosses.

**Proposition 4. Scaffolding has a grammar and a morality.** The grammar is contingency: help is given in inverse proportion to competence, marked at the critical features, and faded as competence arrives; the six moves of Wood and colleagues are its verbs. The morality is this: scaffolding aims at its own withdrawal. Help that persists after competence arrives is not kindness; it is the quiet theft of the child's next step.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** In every session ask the zone question twice: what did this child do today only with me, and what did I do for them today that they could have done with less? The first answer is next week's target; the second is this week's confession.
:::

### Lecture II — Private speech, and the teacher who mishears it

Private speech peaks audibly between roughly three and seven, rises with task difficulty, and falls to whisper and then silence as it becomes inner speech. It predicts better task performance, not worse; children with attentional difficulties often use **more** of it, later, because their regulation work is harder. The teacher who hears muttering as disobedience is hearing a tool as a fault.

The counsel to the teacher has three sentences: what the muttering is (the child thinking aloud, exactly as the teacher once did); what it will do (fade by itself as the skill internalises, typically across the primary years); and what to do instead of stopping it (watch whether it rises when the task hardens — that is the child working, and the moment to offer a scaffold rather than a reprimand). For the child with a language difference, private speech may arrive in the home language first: a further reason never to correct it mid-task.

::: safeguard
Dynamic assessment carries a safeguarding use: where a child's static scores are low and the culture or language of the test is not the child's, a dynamic session can be the difference between a misdiagnosis of intellectual disability and the discovery of an untaught zone. Before any label of global delay is accepted, ask what the child did with help that they could not do alone; record both numbers; and insist that the report carry both.
:::

### Lecture III — Culture, tools and the classroom: what the scaffold is made of

**Proposition 1. Scaffolds are built from cultural materials.** The tools a community hands its children — proverbs, stories, songs, counting systems, prayer, the market's arithmetic — are the substance of contingent help; a scaffold built only from the school's materials reaches only the children the school already holds. The counsellor who scaffolds with the family's proverbs is not decorating the method but using its strongest local timber.

**Proposition 2. Guided participation is the ordinary classroom's Vygotsky.** Rogoff's apprenticeship in thinking describes how children learn inside shared activity with gradual transfer of responsibility — the market stall, the kitchen, the farm, the choir — and much of what a child can do was learned there rather than at a desk. Assessment that sees only the desk under-measures the child.

**Proposition 3. The classroom is a zone, and it can be designed.** Seating that pairs the assisted with the assistable, tasks whose roles rotate, thinking aloud modelled by the teacher, and private speech tolerated rather than policed: each turns the room into a scaffold without adding a single adult. The counsellor's consultation to a school is most powerful when it designs these ordinary arrangements.

::: practice
**Clinical implication.** Ask every family one tools question: what does this child already do well with someone, at home, in the market, at church? The answer is the zone's address and the scaffold's material, in the family's own possession.
:::

### Worked example 4.1 — Scaffolding Kunle's homework, move by move

**Task.** Kunle (7) must write three sentences about his weekend. He stalls at the blank page, jaw set. Script the scaffold with the six moves, then script its fading next week.

**Solution.** *Recruiting:* "Tell me the best thing first — the football or the rain?" (interest before task). *Reducing degrees of freedom:* the page is folded into three boxes; one sentence per box; "we only need the football one now." *Direction maintenance:* after the first sentence, "two boxes left; the rain one is next" — the goal held visibly. *Marking critical features:* "your sentence has who and what; listen: 'Kunle played football.' Who? What? Yours needs the same two." *Frustration control:* "the first sentence is always the worst; everybody's is." *Demonstration:* the counsellor writes one sentence about their own weekend aloud, thinking visibly, including one crossed-out word. Kunle writes two sentences with the fold and one without. *Fading, next week:* the fold becomes a line, the line becomes a page; the who–what prompt becomes a raised eyebrow; the demonstration becomes "show me how you'd start". By week four the scaffold is a question — "what's the best thing?" — and then it is nothing, which was always the plan. The record notes the zone: three sentences with structural support, one alone; next week's target is two alone.

### Worked example 4.2 — The zone of feeling

**Task.** Ife (3:8) melts at nursery drop-off. Using this week's theory, design the regulation scaffold and name what would make it substitution instead.

**Solution.** Regulation at three is co-regulation (week 6): the zone runs from "dysregulated alone" to "settled with a familiar adult who lends a slow voice and a predictable sequence". The scaffold: a goodbye ritual of exactly three steps (wrapper hug, window wave, cup on shelf), rehearsed at home in play first (the social plane before the psychological); the key worker receives her with the ritual's words, not new ones; the settling time is recorded daily — twenty minutes, then twelve, then six — because the fading is the measure. The withdrawal: the ritual shortens by one step when settling holds under five minutes; then the key worker's presence recedes to the doorway; then the wave is from the gate. Substitution would be: the key worker holding Ife all morning, every day, indefinitely — settling without transfer, comfort without competence, the zone never crossed. The difference between scaffolding and substitution is the record of fading: if the steps are not shrinking month by month, the adult is not a scaffold, they are a crutch the service has grown fond of.

::: skills
**Practical 4 — dynamic versus static.** In pairs with a supplied child or recording: administer a static task (e.g., a four-piece pattern completion) and score it; then teach the same task's principle for five minutes using the six scaffold moves and re-administer; record the static score, the amount and kind of help required, and the post-teach score. Write 200 words: which number predicts next month's learning, and what the help profile says that neither score says. Debrief in plenary: whose children are most mis-served by static-only testing in our setting?
:::

::: assessment
**Assessed task.** A scaffolding exercise: a recorded ten-minute scaffolded teaching session with a child on a task of your choice, annotated move by move, with a written fading plan for the following three sessions (500 words).

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| All six moves present and contingent (help inversely matched to competence) | 35% |
| Zone identified: achieved level, assisted level, next target | 25% |
| Fading planned concretely across three sessions | 25% |
| Private speech respected or appropriately harnessed | 15% |

**Formative.** Write the three-sentence counsel to a teacher who calls a muttering child disobedient.
:::

::: summary
**Synthesis.** Vygotsky relocates mind into relationship: functions appear first between people, tools shape thought, the zone of proximal development is where teaching lands, scaffolding is the grammar of help with withdrawal as its purpose, and private speech is the sound of a child building regulation — so assessment must measure the help as well as the stock, and every intervention aims at the day it is no longer needed.
:::

### Seminar questions

1. Two children score identically on a static test; their zones differ by half a year. What does the school owe each, and how would it know?
2. Where is the line between scaffolding and doing the task for the child? Give an observable test, not a feeling.
3. Private speech in the home language: what does a school lose by banning it, in Vygotskian terms?
4. Is co-regulation scaffolding? Use the six moves to argue yes or no.
5. Vygotsky makes the counsellor part of the child's cognition. What does that do to the idea of a "neutral" therapist?
