# Programme Overview {.front}

The Master in Children and Adolescent Counseling is taught in three semesters of core courses, with a pool of electives from which students select. The tables in this section are generated directly from the University's curriculum record; course codes, course titles and unit values are reproduced exactly as supplied, and chapter numbers are the handbook's own. Where a code appears against more than one course in the curriculum record, both entries are printed and the duplication is noted; the handbook does not silently renumber the University's curriculum.

## Units, notional hours and lecture units

Every course in the curriculum record carries a unit value of **1.6**. Taking one unit as fifteen notional learning hours, a 1.6-unit course represents approximately **24 notional hours**, of which the handbook's teaching material is organised into **6 lecture units** of about four hours each. A lecture unit is not a contact-hour prescription: the four hours divide across synchronous teaching, seminar or skills work, supervised or independent practice, and reading. Students should expect roughly one third of the time in each unit to be scheduled teaching and two thirds to be their own preparation, practice and writing.

The programme as listed therefore represents approximately **744 notional hours** across all 31 course entries, of which the required core (Semesters 1–3) accounts for **552 hours**.

## Progression and assessment

Core courses in Semesters 1–3 are taken in the order listed. The first semester establishes the theoretical, developmental and ethical foundations on which every later course depends; CAC. 703 Ethics in Counseling should be taken before any course involving contact with children, and its content is assumed in the safeguarding material throughout Parts II and III. The second semester moves from foundation to application: assessment, diagnosis, behavioural and crisis intervention, and the settings in which young people are actually seen. The third semester consolidates advanced practice and research, ending in the Master Thesis (CAC. 760).

Each lecture unit carries formative work and one assessed component. Assessment across the programme is by written examination, case formulation, observed or recorded skills demonstration, critical review, and — for CAC. 726 and CAC. 760 — supervised practice and dissertation. The assessment criteria printed in each unit are the criteria by which that unit's work is marked; the programme-level grade scale and the regulations for resits, extenuating circumstances and academic misconduct are those of the University, published separately and binding on this handbook.

## Notes on the curriculum record

Three features of the curriculum record are reproduced here exactly as supplied and are flagged for the reader's attention rather than corrected by the editor. First, **CAC. 701 appears twice** in the First Semester, against two different courses — *Fundamentals of Counseling Theories* (chapter 1) and *Child Development Theories* (chapter 2). Second, **CAC. 708 carries the same title as the first CAC. 701** (*Fundamentals of Counseling Theories*, chapter 9); the handbook treats it as a distinct entry and prints it as supplied. Third, **CAC. 717 *Research Methods in Counseling* (chapter 18) and CAC. 721 *Research Methodology* (chapter 22)** both fall in the Third Semester and overlap in scope; the handbook distinguishes them by treating CAC. 717 as the foundation course in method and design and CAC. 721 as the advanced course in methodology for the thesis, and cross-references between them. Students should confirm all three points with the registrar's office before planning a semester.
