# Foreword {.front}

A handbook is a promise about what a student will be asked to do, and a promise about how they will be helped to do it. This volume makes both promises explicitly. It sets out the whole lecture course of the Master in Children and Adolescent Counseling — every core course of the three semesters and the elective pool — in one place, in one order, to one standard.

The University teaches at a distance. Students read this handbook in Lagos and in Louisville, in a quiet study and on a bus, at midnight after a shift. That fact governs the design. Nothing here assumes that a lecture was attended, that a slide was seen, or that a tutor was in the room. Each unit states what the student will be able to do by the end of it, gives the argument in full, supplies the reading, sets the task, and then says plainly how the task will be judged. A student who works only from these pages should be able to complete the programme.

The subject itself imposes a second obligation. The people whose difficulties this handbook discusses are children. They cannot consent to be examples, they cannot correct a misleading account of themselves, and they are frequently described by adults in language that fixes them. Every case in these pages is therefore invented and labelled as invented. Where a real published case is used, it is cited. Where the law or the reporting duty is described, it is described as a structure to be checked against the jurisdiction in which the student actually works, because a handbook that states a duty incorrectly is worse than one that says nothing.

The University's expectation of its students is that they become practitioners who can think. That is a harder thing than it sounds. Technique is learnable; judgement is not taught directly but grows from repeatedly being asked to account for oneself. The seminar questions, the skills laboratories and the formulation exercises in this handbook exist for that purpose. They are not decoration, and they are not optional.

I commend this volume to the students for whom it was written.
