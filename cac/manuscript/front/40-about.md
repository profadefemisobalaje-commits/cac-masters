# About the University and This Programme {.front}

**Triune Biblical University Global Extension USA** teaches at a distance and awards its own degrees. Its programmes are built on a conviction that serious professional education can be conducted in writing, provided the writing is exact, the requirements are explicit, and the student is never left to guess what is expected.

The **Master in Children and Adolescent Counseling** prepares practitioners to work therapeutically with children, adolescents and the families and schools around them. It is a counselling qualification in method: it teaches theory, development, ethics, assessment, intervention, research and supervised practice, and it examines students on all of them. It is not a licensure in itself. Graduates who intend to practise clinically must meet the licensing or registration requirements of the jurisdiction in which they work, which in most places include a period of post-graduate supervised practice, an examination, and continuing professional education. The University's programme is designed to be recognisable within those requirements, but it cannot satisfy them on a student's behalf, and students should establish their own local position before enrolling.

## Mode of study

The programme is delivered wholly online. Teaching is asynchronous by default: this handbook, the accompanying lecture decks, assigned reading and recorded material are released on a semester schedule, and seminars are convened at times announced in advance. Assessment is submitted electronically. Supervised practice courses (CAC. 726 Internship, and the practice components of the clinical courses) require a placement with a qualified supervisor approved by the University; arranging a placement is the student's responsibility and should begin early, because suitable placements with children are not easily found.

## The three semesters

The First Semester builds foundations: what counselling theories are and what they are for, how children and adolescents develop, the ethics that govern work with minors, and the core skills of the room. The Second Semester turns to application: crisis, cultural context, assessment and diagnosis, behavioural intervention, schools, substance use and trauma. The Third Semester consolidates advanced practice and research method and ends in the Master Thesis.

The elective pool allows specialisation — developmental disabilities, legal issues, community referral, technology, internship, professional identity, parent–child interaction therapy, or advanced topics.
