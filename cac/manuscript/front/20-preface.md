# Preface {.front}

This handbook is the printed form of the lecture course of the Master in Children and Adolescent Counseling. It exists because the programme is taught wholly at a distance, and because a student who cannot attend a lecture is entitled to something better than a transcript of one.

## What this volume is

It contains the complete required programme — the nine course entries of the First Semester, the seven of the Second, the seven of the Third including the Master Thesis — together with the eight-course elective pool. Each course is a chapter. Each chapter is divided into six lecture units of about four notional hours, which is the whole of a 1.6-unit course: twenty-four hours of study, of which roughly a third is scheduled teaching and two thirds the student's own reading, practice and writing.

Each unit has the same shape, and the shape is deliberate. It opens with what the student will be able to do on completing it, written as capabilities that can be observed and examined rather than states of mind that cannot. It says where the unit sits in the argument of the course. It defines four key concepts and immediately attaches each to something a counsellor does in a room with a child. It then gives three lectures, each built from propositions rather than from a narrative, each ending in a clinical implication and a seminar question. A figure interrupts the argument where an argument needs one. A fictitious case follows, with prompts for reflection. A skills laboratory sets out timed practice with a debrief. Five seminar questions, the assessed task with its criteria, six readings with the core text marked, and one sentence of synthesis close the unit; the next unit is previewed.

That grammar is identical to the one used for the University's lecture decks, so a student who has watched a week's slides and a student who has only this book are working from the same material in the same order. The handbook is not a summary of the teaching. It is the teaching.

## Standards observed

The volume is written to a published specification, *HANDBOOK-STANDARD.md*, held with the manuscript. Its principal commitments are these.

Course codes, titles and unit values are the University's, reproduced exactly, and the programme overview is generated from the curriculum record rather than retyped, so that the printed tables cannot drift from it. Five anomalies in that record — a code used twice, a title used twice, uneven semester loading, two overlapping research courses, an unusual uniform unit value — are printed as supplied and flagged, because an editor has no authority to renumber a university's curriculum.

Citations follow the seventh edition of the APA *Publication Manual*. Only works actually cited are listed. No quotation, statistic, page number or finding in this volume has been invented, and where the evidence is contested the contest is reported rather than resolved.

The handbook's own prose is in British spelling. Quoted titles, journal names and direct quotations keep their original spelling, which in a field dominated by American literature means the reader will meet both. This is a decision, not an oversight, and it is recorded so that it is not mistaken for carelessness.

Every case involving a child is a fictitious composite, labelled at the point of use, with ages and identifying detail altered. Every photograph generated for teaching is captioned as such. Standardised instruments are described and attributed but not reproduced, because most are copyright and their free circulation would destroy their validity.

The file is exported as a tagged PDF conforming to PDF/UA-1: structure tree, bookmarks to heading level three, British English language tag, alternative text on informative figures, and no text-and-background pairing below the WCAG 2.2 Level AA contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1. One correction was necessary to achieve this. The bronze used for callout labels and part openers in the University's existing book design measured 4.3 to 1 against ivory and 3.9 to 1 against the tinted grounds, which is below the threshold for text of that size; the bronze ink used for *text* in this volume is darkened accordingly, while the original remains for rules and fills, where only a 3 to 1 ratio is required.

## What this volume does not do

It does not make a counsellor. Competence in work with children is acquired through supervised practice, direct observation, feedback, the counsellor's own development, and compliance with the regulatory and ethical framework of the place where one works. A book can teach theory, evidence, method and the discipline of accounting for one's judgements. It cannot confer the thing employers and clients actually need.

It does not state anyone's legal duty. Safeguarding obligations, reporting timeframes, consent rules and record-keeping requirements are creatures of local law and of the student's employing institution. The handbook teaches their structure and insists that the operative version be checked locally. A student who relies on these pages instead of on the procedure of their jurisdiction has misread them.

It does not authorise diagnosis. Assessment and diagnostic material is taught as requiring supervised practice and licensure, and the notices on the copyright page bind the whole volume.
