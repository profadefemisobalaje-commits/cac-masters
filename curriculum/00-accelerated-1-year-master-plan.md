**Institution:** CAC Masters

**Standard duration:** 18 months | **Accelerated duration:** 12 months (52 weeks)

**Document status:** Draft 1 — for approval before video production begins

## 0. What this document does

It compresses the standard 18-month curriculum into a single 12-month academic year, sets the
week-by-week teaching calendar, and defines the lecture-video blueprint that follows from it.

> **Nothing is produced (slides, audio, video) until you approve Section 11 — the video blueprint.**

## 1. The compression in numbers

| | Standard 18-month route | Accelerated 1-year route |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar length | 78 weeks | 52 weeks |
| Semesters | 3 × ~24 weeks + breaks | 3 × 16 weeks + 4 weeks breaks |
| Weeks of teaching | ~62 | 48 |
| Courses per semester (max) | 4–5 concurrent | 8 (delivered 2 at a time) |
| Contact hours per course | 41.25 (2.75 CH × 15 hrs) | 41.25 — **unchanged** |
| Weekly contact load | ~11 hrs | ~21 hrs in block, ~14 hrs averaged |
| Weekly total study load | ~25 hrs | ~36–40 hrs |
| Thesis | 2 semesters part-time | Runs the full year (Wk 9 → Wk 51) |
| Practicum + Internship | 2 semesters | 14 + 16 weeks, concurrent with coursework |

**The non-negotiable principle:** the *credit hours are not reduced*. Every course still delivers
its full 41.25 contact hours. Only the **calendar** shrinks — the saved time is bought back with
higher weekly intensity, block delivery, and asynchronous recorded lectures (the video series).

## 3. Delivery model — Block (carousel) delivery

Each 16-week semester is divided into **four 4-week blocks**. In each block, **two courses run
concurrently** (not eight). This is what makes 8 courses per semester survivable.

```
SEMESTER (16 weeks) = BLOCK A → BLOCK B → BLOCK C → BLOCK D
                       Wk 1–4    Wk 5–8    Wk 9–12   Wk 13–16
                       Course 1  Course 3  Course 5  Course 7
                       Course 2  Course 4  Course 6  Course 8
```

**Weekly shape of a block (per course, 2 courses running):**

| Day | Activity | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Lecture 1 (video + live seminar) — Course A | 3.5 |
| Tue | Lecture 1 (video + live seminar) — Course B | 3.5 |
| Wed | Skills lab / micro-counselling practicum — Course A | 3.5 |
| Thu | Lecture 2 (video + live seminar) — Course B | 3.5 |
| Fri | Case conference / supervision (both courses) | 3.5 |
| Sat | Lecture 2 — Course A + block assessment clinic | 3.5 |
| **Total contact** | | **21 hrs/week** |
| Independent study | Readings, journal, reflective log | 15–18 hrs |

Per course per block: **12 contact sessions × 3.5 hrs ≈ 41.25 hrs = full 2.75 CH.** ✔

**Why blocks work for this programme:** counseling is a skills discipline. Studying
"Group Counseling" and "Family Counseling" side-by-side for four weeks, then examining, means the
student is practising group facilitation in the same weeks they are learning group theory — rather
than carrying eight parallel syllabi they cannot integrate.

## 9. Assessment and progression

**Per course (100 marks):** Continuous assessment 40% + End-of-block examination 60%
- Continuous assessment: reflective journal 10, seminar presentation 10, mid-block test 10, skills lab/case work 10

**Per semester:** end-of-block examinations in Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16 (and 21, 25, 29, 33; 39, 43, 47)
plus clinical/research components.

**Grading:** 5-point scale (A=5 … F=0). Pass mark 50%. Minimum CGPA 3.00 to remain in good standing.

**Typical classification:** Pass 3.00–3.49 • Merit 3.50–4.49 • Distinction 4.50–5.00
*(confirm against your Senate-approved regulations)*

**Progression gate:** a student may not enter Block C of any semester with more than one
outstanding carry-over from the previous semester, given the compressed calendar.

## 10. Cost of acceleration — and how it is absorbed

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Cognitive overload from 21 hrs contact/week | Block model = only 2 live courses at a time |
| Reduced reflection/free time | Recorded lecture videos → watch at 1.25×/on demand; live time reserved for labs and supervision |
| Supervisor bottleneck in Semester 3 | Supervisors allocated in Week 9 of Semester 1, not Semester 3 |
| Clinical hours squeezed | Placement starts Semester 2, not Semester 3; 3-day pattern in Semester 3 |
| Burnout | Week 17 and Weeks 34–35 breaks are protected; no teaching on placement days; one rest day/week enforced |
| Thin attendance evidence | Video series doubles as attendance-tracked asynchronous lecture record |

## 11. VIDEO BLUEPRINT — needs your decision before production

**The subject list:** 28 courses. **Your stated series:** 16 weeks × 35 minutes per video.

Those two numbers do not map 1:1, so here are the three coherent options.

### Option A — One video per course subject (28 videos)
- 28 × 35 min = **16 hrs 20 min** of video
- Each course gets exactly one self-contained lecture
- Mapped onto the calendar: Weeks 1–16 cover Semester 1 courses, etc.
- **Best if:** the videos are course-introduction / flagship lectures, and live teaching carries the depth.

### Option B — One video per teaching week, per semester (3 × 16 = 48 videos)
- 48 × 35 min = **28 hours** of video
- Every week of every semester has its weekly 35-minute lecture, exactly as you described
- Each course is covered by 2 weekly videos (16 weeks ÷ 8 courses) → genuine syllabus coverage
- **Best if:** the videos *are* the lecture delivery (asynchronous/flipped programme).

### Option C — 16 videos total, each merging 1–2 subjects (16 videos)
- 16 × 35 min = **9 hrs 20 min**
- A compressed survey series: every subject touched, none covered in depth
- **Best if:** the videos are marketing/preview material rather than instruction.

**Volume implication for narration:** at ~130 words/minute, a 35-minute lecture is
**~4,300 words**. Option A ≈ 120,000 words total; Option B ≈ 210,000; Option C ≈ 70,000.

### Recommended pairing
Option B for delivery + Option C's 16-video cut as a public-facing preview (reusing Option B audio).
If budget allows only one, Option A gives the best coverage-per-hour of production.
