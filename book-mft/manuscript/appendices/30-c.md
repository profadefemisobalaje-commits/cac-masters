## C.3 A nine-week study pathway for MFT. 901

The following pathway assumes a fifteen-week semester with a weekly three-hour seminar. Weeks 1–9 cover the analytical content; weeks 10–12 are devoted to integrative work; weeks 13–15 to assessment and consolidation.

| Week | Reading focus | Seminar work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Section 1.1; Bateson (1972), selections; von Bertalanffy (1968) | Map the origins of systemic concepts; identify what was lost in translation |
| 2 | Section 1.2; Minuchin (1974) | Construct structural maps of two recorded or role-played families |
| 3 | Section 1.3; Watzlawick, Weakland, and Fisch (1974); Selvini Palazzoli et al (1978) | Practise circular questioning; debate the ethics of paradoxical technique |
| 4 | Section 1.4; Bowen (1978), selections; Boszormenyi-Nagy and Krasner (1986) | Build genograms; practise multidirected partiality in a role-play |
| 5 | Section 1.5; Watzlawick, Beavin, and Jackson (1967); Satir (1983) | Analyse session transcripts for punctuation, report, and command |
| 6 | Section 1.6; Johnson (2019); selected observational research | Track a negative cycle from a transcript; evaluate a neurobiological claim |
| 7 | Section 1.7; White and Epston (1990); de Shazer (1988); McGoldrick et al (2005) | Externalising practice; critique a formulation for cultural assumptions |
| 8 | Section 1.8; Epstein and Baucom (2002); Christensen and Jacobson (2002) | Design a behaviour-exchange and an acceptance intervention for one case |
| 9 | Section 1.8 (integrative frameworks); Pinsof et al (2018); Sprenkle et al (2009) | Apply the integrative sequence (Figure 1.9) to the week 2 cases |
| 10 | Review of all sections | Comparative grid exercise: complete Tables B.1 and B.2 from memory |
| 11 | Evidence base review; meta-analyses and systematic reviews | Appraise three studies against the checklist in Appendix B.3 |
| 12 | Critical traditions and ethics | Seminar debate: circularity, responsibility, and safety in a violent case |
| 13 | Applied Exercise 1 | Group presentations and cross-examination |
| 14 | Examination preparation | Mock viva on competing formulations |
| 15 | Consolidation | Written examination or assignment submission |

## C.4 Assessment design

Three assessments are recommended for this course, in ascending order of independence.

**Assignment 1 (2,000 words): concept analysis.** Select one theoretical construct from Chapter 1 (for example, homeostasis, triangulation, enactment, punctuation, differentiation, or externalising). Trace its origin, describe its migration into family therapy, and evaluate the extent to which its clinical use departs from its original technical meaning.

**Assignment 2 (3,000 words): comparative formulation.** Using a case vignette supplied by the course leader — or a documented case from the literature, fully referenced — construct formulations of the same presentation from three traditions, state what each predicts, and defend a choice of treatment with reasons that address the alternatives.

**Assignment 3 (5,000 words): theoretical framework for a research proposal.** Develop the theoretical framework for a prospective doctoral study: state the research problem, specify the theories that frame it, justify their selection against at least two rival frameworks, and state the study's key constructs and their proposed operationalisation. This assignment is designed to feed directly into the dissertation stage.

Examiners should require candidates to state, for any theoretical claim they advance, what evidence would count against it. A candidate who cannot do so has not yet completed the course in the sense in which this volume defines it.
