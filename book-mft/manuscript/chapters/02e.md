## Summary

Research methods in MFT begin with the uncertainty that matters, not with a preferred technique. A well-formed question defines population and context, clarifies constructs, and implies an answer that can alter knowledge or practice. The researcher aligns the question, theoretical account, design, measurement, analysis, and intended conclusion. Descriptive, explanatory, predictive, evaluative, and interpretive questions require different forms of evidence; quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods approaches have complementary purposes and distinct assumptions.

Design determines the comparisons available and the inferences that can reasonably be made. Cross-sectional association is not causal evidence; longitudinal order improves temporal description but does not eliminate confounding; randomisation supports causal inference under conditions that include sound allocation, follow-up, measurement, and analysis. Quasi-experimental and single-case designs can be valuable when their assumptions and limitations are explicit. Sampling shapes both precision and applicability. Feasibility, recruitment, language access, participant burden, and equity are methodological concerns, not administrative details.

Measurement is a theory-laden act. Instruments represent constructs imperfectly, and validity depends on evidence for the intended interpretation and use. Outcomes should matter to families and be sufficiently precise for the question. MFT research must represent interdependence: partner reports, family members, repeated observations, therapists, and clinics may be clustered or mutually influential. Actor-partner and multilevel approaches can model some of this structure, but they do not turn association into causation. Missing data, measurement invariance, fidelity, therapist variation, and adverse effects deserve planned attention.

Ethical research protects voluntary participation, safety, privacy, and fair inclusion. Relational research demands special care because one participant's disclosure can affect others; consent, confidentiality, recording, safeguarding, and dual roles must be addressed before data collection. Secure governance and meaningful community partnership are essential. Transparent analysis and reporting, appropriate registration, and honest distinction between confirmatory and exploratory results strengthen the credibility of findings. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are only as trustworthy as their search, appraisal, comparability, and interpretation.

The translation of evidence into practice is a reasoned decision process rather than a prescription. Efficacy, effectiveness, and implementation answer related but different questions. Families' goals, circumstances, safety, preferences, and access must remain central. Doctoral researchers demonstrate methodological maturity when they state not only what their study may show, but what it cannot establish.

## Doctoral Research Questions

1. How can MFT research design represent reciprocal influence between partners while distinguishing temporal prediction from causal effect?
2. Which outcomes do families identify as meaningful after couple or family treatment, and how do these compare with commonly used clinician- or researcher-selected measures?
3. Under what conditions do brief family interventions improve adolescent engagement with care, and what mechanisms are supported by temporally ordered evidence?
4. How do language, culture, socioeconomic conditions, disability, and service context affect measurement equivalence and treatment access in family research?
5. Which strategies improve recruitment and retention of diverse families without increasing burden or compromising voluntariness?
6. How should family-level adverse experiences be defined, monitored, and reported in couple and family intervention trials?
7. What is the added value of integrating observational interaction coding with each partner's account of relational change?
8. How do therapist effects and supervision practices contribute to variation in outcomes across routine MFT services?
9. Which forms of adaptation preserve an intervention's intended function while improving local acceptability and feasibility?
10. How can routine outcome monitoring be used collaboratively without converting measurement into a compliance or surveillance tool?
